## Stronger objections to paired improver credit

Critic correctly objected that Var(lossdifference)=v_e+v_0-2cov is not the variance of Delta*score. Favorable loss covariance alone does not imply lower policy-gradient variance or lower cost per improvement. The unedited program's loss is not necessarily an optimal control variate. Critic supplied two equally likely actions with scalar-logit scores -1/2,+1/2, deterministic edited losses0,1 and unedited loss2. Without baseline, negative-loss gradients are0,-1/2: mean-1/4, variance1/16. With improvement reward2,1, gradients are-1,+1/2: the same mean-1/4 but variance9/16. I actually checked and accepted this arithmetic. This is a constructed policy-gradient counterexample, not a biological or general no-benefit claim.

I proposed another explicit noise model to avoid making task heterogeneity a free advantage: e in {-1,+1}, reward a*e+mu_task+epsilon, with mean-zero task nuisance variance tau² and independent observation noise variance sigma². For the exponential signed-action parameterization at zero, score=e. A same-task candidate-reference difference gives gradient variance2sigma² and costs two evaluations. Merely averaging two independent unpaired candidate gradients has variance(tau²+sigma²)/2; a comparison only to this weaker control could make paired credit look useful when tau²>3sigma².

Root supplied a strictly stronger ordinary control for this declared grammar: evaluate both opposite edits on the same task, g=[r(+)-r(-)]/2. Task nuisance cancels and independent equal-variance candidate noises give Var(g)=sigma²/2 at the same two-evaluation cost. The proposed candidate-reference estimator is therefore four times noisier even in the high-task-heterogeneity regime. I accepted this and sent it to source/critic. The score normalization differs from critic's scalar-logit example; the numerical variances must not be mixed. Exact finite enumeration can check these stipulated populations after root separately freezes it, without new biological outcomes.

These counterexamples do not assert universal antithetic superiority for arbitrary asymmetric edit grammars, nonlinear reward functions or other noise covariance. Real squared functional losses evaluated on the same noisy outcome y have difference L0-Le=(f0²-fe²)-2y(f0-fe), so outcome noise contributes -2epsilon(f0-fe); shared assay noise does not automatically cancel completely. Candidate-dependent early stopping or reference construction also cannot be silently included in an action-independent-baseline proof. A genuinely fixed same-snapshot reference may be cached across candidates; an ordinary control must have the same caching opportunity and total accounting.

The whole proposed update remains ordinary baseline REINFORCE. Compared with WIRL, using a same-snapshot reference rather than the previous submission is a concrete reward-construction difference, not an algorithm invention or demonstrated finite-budget improvement. No observed biological error identifies a useful new edit grammar. The useful disagreement with a naive paired-credit rationale is now explicit: matched comparisons can reduce nuisance variability, yet the strongest equal-cost candidate design and gradient-weighted control variates may be substantially better.
