## Constructive: paired descendant credit

The proposed matched-base edited-versus-unedited functional reward is well-defined and improves attribution over a previous-submission trend when source/model/history differ between submissions. Its precise full estimator is ordinary REINFORCE with an action-independent paired baseline; the baseline expectation cancellation follows for a fixed common episode, including the baseline evaluation cost. A generic statement that policy-gradient families are old would be insufficient, but the report gives a full-operation counterpart and does not claim a new algorithm.

Biological challenge: a useful editor needs an update grammar tied to a real repeatable functional error. The new NK study supplies an actual initial-kill versus later-clearance discrepancy, but not a public trajectory task with lawful sequential contact/death/release supports and later queries. Published aggregate means and selected movie illustrations cannot fill that contract. The exposed BeatAML task can lawfully support functional completion tests, yet its endpoint AUC support is not NK state-transition feedback and does not establish virtual-cell necessity. The paper's absence of public microtrajectories does not forbid synthetic software tests of paired attribution, or ordinary outcome-level prediction on an actually public endpoint panel.

Comparing trained G1 and starting G0 from a matched later base U/model/history and equal total cost is a legitimate test of whether a learned improver helps. It does not demand infinite self-rewriting. It also does not establish the requested advantage until real later outcomes and competent identical paired-search controls are included. The unresolved missing piece is a biologically useful non-equivalent operation/constraint, not an extra editor level.

## Critic: inherited semantic witnesses

The rational quadratic witness is a useful exact software falsifier. It demonstrates safely recoverable coefficient equivalence inside its fixed grammar, finite-suite collisions, loss of validity outside the grammar, and a declared case where canonicalization is cheaper. The stipulated heavy duplicate stream and artificial cost units cannot be represented as measured search or assay savings. The exact ordinary coefficient comparison is enough to reject the claimed advantage in that construction; one need not declare all CEGIS or semantic-testing families ineligible.

Biological challenge: equivalent current predictions or equal aggregate kill fractions do not certify an updater's response to later histories. For NK applications, relevant legal input histories include effector survival, target availability, observation cadence, event interval-censoring, contact multiplicity and risk time. Equal early aggregate scores can hide opposite changes in kill and release distributions. The selected paper does not offer authenticated public microtrajectories to distinguish these update semantics. Treating a simulator-generated history as a biological functional witness would manufacture the evidence. A finite software grammar and later-history counterexamples remain valid necessary tests without those biological assets, as the critic explicitly allows.

For a stateful updater the semantic object includes future fast/hidden-state behavior, not only the reported next predictor. Any certificates must freeze those inputs or account for them. Discovery and verification costs count. No evidence here establishes that real updater searches have sufficient safely removable duplication beyond ordinary canonicalization, inherited memoization, or paid active discrimination.

## Shared disposition

These reports expose two separate obstructions: no qualified distinctive updater/improver operation in the examined constructions, and no authenticated public dynamic functional task in the selected new biological source. Neither obstruction proves that all meta-learning, program search, or NK functional research is unpromising. They do justify not fitting a renamed estimator or adding agent levels to these specific candidates. No new biological fit or performance claim follows from this cross-review.
