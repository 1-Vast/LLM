Abstract
Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires agents to follow instructions and navigate to target locations. However, current VLN agents often suffer severe performance degradation in unseen environments due to distribution shifts. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) offers a promising paradigm to bridge this gap, yet existing methods that rely on unsupervised entropy minimization can propagate errors through the trajectory, causing cascading failures in navigation. Recent methods mitigate this by incorporating human feedback, but they apply trajectory-level supervision uniformly across all steps without localizing the actions is truly informative, leading to inefficient feedback utilization and mismatched optimization granularity. To address these limitations, we propose FETA, a
F
eedback-
E
fficient
T
est-Time
A
daptation method that improves both feedback efficiency and optimization reliability.
To reduce unnecessary human interventions, we introduce a hierarchical sample selection strategy to localize informative samples. Specifically, we first filter trajectories whose normalized policy entropy exhibits large fluctuations. Within each selected trajectory, we further select the critical action via an entropy-gradient saliency criterion and query a single binary feedback on its correctness.
A discrepancy-aware entropy optimization objective then updates the policy only at the queried step, with adaptive scaling that assigns larger relative weights to samples with greater prediction–feedback discrepancy. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that FETA achieves state-of-the-art performance with fewer human interventions, while remaining robust under noisy feedback and continual adaptation settings.

Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)

[
6

19

typically rely on unsupervised objectives, such as entropy minimization, and perform policy updates at every step along the navigation trajectory. This strategy is particularly brittle in VLN, where navigation unfolds as a sequential decision-making process: an erroneous update at one step can change the subsequent state distribution, distort later optimization signals, and eventually cause cascading failures. Therefore, the key challenge in VLN test-time adaptation is not simply how to adapt online, but how to derive optimization signals that remain reliable throughout sequential decision-making.
To obtain more reliable optimization signals, recent VLN-TTA methods have begun to incorporate human feedback at test time. FeedTTA
[
20
]
queries human whether each trajectory is successful or not, and uses the feedback as a reward signal to update the policy via reinforcement learning. ATENA
[
7
]
requests feedback only for trajectories with high entropy, calibrating policy uncertainty by boosting decision confidence on successful trajectories and suppressing it on failed ones. Despite their promise, these methods still suffer from two key limitations.
1) Inefficient Feedback Utilization
: Once a trajectory-level label (
i.e.,
success or failure) is obtained, existing methods generally use every obtained label to update the policy on the corresponding trajectory, even when the resulting adaptation gains are limited.
2) Mismatched Optimization Granularity
: Existing methods use the same trajectory-level feedback signal across all steps, even though successful trajectories may still contain suboptimal actions and failed ones may still include locally correct decisions. This creates a mismatch between trajectory-level supervision and local action decisions being optimized.
To address these challenges, we propose a
F
eedback-
E
fficient
T
est-Time
A
daptation method (FETA) that improves both feedback efficiency and optimization reliability. Our key observation is that the choice of trajectories and steps for feedback acquisition affects adaptation performance (as shown in Fig.
3

IV
). Based on this observation, we design a hierarchical feedback selection strategy that improves both feedback efficiency and optimization granularity. At the trajectory level, we prioritize trajectories with large fluctuations in normalized policy entropy, as such trajectories indicate unstable decision confidence and may benefit from external correction. At the step level, for each selected trajectory, we select the critical decision state using an entropy-gradient saliency criterion, which measures the sensitivity of normalized policy entropy to the action logits. We then query a single binary human feedback signal on the correctness of the selected action. This hierarchical design significantly reduces unnecessary human intervention while refining supervision from the trajectory level to the action level.
Based on the queried feedback, we design a discrepancy-aware objective for policy optimization. Instead of updating the entire trajectory from a single final outcome, our method corrects the policy only at the queried step, where the feedback determines both the update magnitude and direction of entropy optimization. Specifically, we introduce an adaptive scaling strategy that assigns larger relative weights to samples with greater mismatch between the current policy prediction and the feedback. We then apply directional entropy optimization: for actions verified as correct, the objective sharpens the policy distribution to reinforce the current decision. Otherwise, it relaxes overconfident predictions and encourages alternative actions. Our contributions can be summarized as follows:
•

•
We design a discrepancy-aware entropy optimization loss that dynamically adjusts the direction of entropy optimization and the relative sample weights according to the human feedback, enabling efficient utilization of sparse feedback signals.
•
Extensive experiments show that FETA achieves the state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks with fewer human interventions, including an 18.30% relative improvement in SPL over the strongest existing navigation TTA baseline, using DUET as the backbone on the REVERIE val unseen split, while remaining robust under noisy feedback and continual adaptation.

In real deployment, VLN agents often encounter environments that differ from those seen during training, which can degrade navigation performance. This motivates test-time adaptation (TTA), which updates model parameters online using incoming test samples. Existing TTA methods typically rely on unsupervised surrogate objectives,

As a result, once the adaptation signal is unreliable, an erroneous update can propagate through the trajectory and cause cascading failures. The challenge is how to obtain reliable optimization signals under sequential decision-making and environment shift.
To improve adaptation reliability, recent VLN test-time adaptation methods have begun to introduce reliable human feedback during deployment. However, existing human-feedback-driven methods still remain limited in two important aspects. First, the feedback they acquire may not always translate into effective adaptation, as policy updates on some labeled trajectories yield only limited gains. Second, they typically use a single trajectory-level feedback signal to optimize all actions within that trajectory in the same direction. Such coarse supervision is mismatched to the fine-grained nature of sequential decision-making: successful trajectories may still contain suboptimal actions, while failed trajectories may still include locally correct decisions. Applying the same feedback to all steps can therefore provide inappropriate optimization signals for individual actions, reducing the effectiveness of human guidance for policy adaptation. These limitations suggest that human-feedback-driven TTA for VLN requires a more structured use of sparse supervision, with both feedback-efficient querying and fine-grained action-level correction.

V-C
Ablation Study
To verify the effectiveness of each component in our method, we conduct ablation studies on the REVERIE Val Unseen split, with results reported in Table
IV
. To ensure a fair comparison under an equal feedback budget, the ablations of Trajectory-Level Selection (TLS) and Step-Level Selection (SLS) replace the respective selection method with uniform random sampling while keeping the total number of feedback queries identical.
Disabling Discrepancy-Aware Scaling (DAS) assigns unit weight to each queried sample. Relative to the full method, replacing TLS with random trajectory sampling leads to a performance drop across all reported metrics, supporting the effectiveness of trajectory selection based on the standard deviation of normalized entropy. Replacing SLS with random step sampling also decreases performance, indicating that entropy-gradient saliency helps make effective use of sparse feedback. Removing DAS while retaining TLS and SLS also decreases performance, supporting the benefit of weighting each queried sample according to the prediction–feedback discrepancy. The variants with only TLS, SLS, or DAS enabled also outperform the unadapted baseline, showing that adaptation remains effective when only one of the three components is enabled.
These results collectively demonstrate that our method efficiently exploits sparse human feedback to enable effective agent adaptation in unseen environments.

Comparison of Step-Level Selection Methods.
Table

Under an equal budget, entropy gradient saliency outperforms the entropy-based selection method across all metrics, yielding an 8.97% relative improvement in SPL on the REVERIE Val Unseen split. A possible reason is that time steps with high entropy may correspond to policy outputs that approach a uniform distribution. Negative feedback at such steps provides a weak entropy-maximization signal, as the entropy gradient with respect to the logits approaches zero near a uniform distribution. By contrast, selecting the step with the largest entropy-gradient saliency

Robustness of the Method.
In practical applications, binary feedback provided by humans is rarely perfectly accurate. To evaluate the robustness of our method under noisy feedback, we simulate noise by randomly flipping feedback labels with varying probabilities, gradually reducing the feedback accuracy from 100% to 50%. As shown in Fig.
4
, the navigation success rate decreases as the noise level increases. Even at 60% feedback accuracy, our method still outperforms the unadapted baseline. Among the tested noise levels, performance falls below the baseline only when feedback becomes random (50% accuracy). These results demonstrate that FETA can tolerate substantial simulated label noise while retaining an advantage over the unadapted baseline.

Continual Adaptation.
We evaluate continual adaptation by progressively adapting the model over multiple rounds and measuring performance after each round, starting from the unadapted model (Round 0). As shown in Fig.

In contrast, FETA improves in each of the three adaptation rounds. This contrast may be related to the granularity of supervision: ATENA uses the same trajectory-level feedback signal across all steps, potentially reinforcing suboptimal actions or suppressing locally correct ones. By contrast, FETA computes the adaptation loss at a selected step using feedback on the corresponding action, aligning the supervision granularity with the decision being optimized.

VI
Human–Simulator Feedback Agreement
To assess how closely the progress-based simulator feedback reflects human judgments of action usefulness, we conduct a human annotation study on 90 feedback queries from the REVERIE Val Unseen split. Three annotators each label a disjoint set of 30 queries, so each query receives one human annotation. For each query, the annotator is shown the navigation instruction, the complete observation sequence along the trajectory, the observations immediately before and after the queried action, and the action itself. The annotator then assigns a binary label indicating whether the action is helpful for completing the task. Human annotations agree with the simulator feedback on 78.9% of queries, with agreement rates of 81.8% for simulator-positive queries and 76.1% for simulator-negative queries. The label distributions are approximately balanced: 48.9% of simulator labels are positive, while annotators judge 52.2% of actions as helpful. These results provide empirical support for using the simulator feedback as a proxy for human judgments in this setting. The annotation interface is shown in Fig.
8
in the appendix.

IX
Conclusion
We propose FETA, a
F
eedback-
E
fficient
T
est-Time
A
daptation method for Vision-and-Language Navigation. To address the inefficient feedback utilization and mismatched optimization granularity of prior methods, FETA performs hierarchical sample selection that filters informative trajectories via the standard deviation of normalized entropy and
selects a critical step for targeted feedback
via entropy-gradient saliency. The queried feedback is then converted into a fine-grained step-level update through a discrepancy-aware entropy optimization objective. Extensive experiments show that FETA achieves state-of-the-art performance with fewer human queries and lower per-episode latency.
