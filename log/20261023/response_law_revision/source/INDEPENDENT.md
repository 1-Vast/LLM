## Exact distinction needed: a predictive revision claim is possible without causal identification

Changing an empirical conditional covariance can improve predictions even if no mechanistic intervention law changed. Conversely a biological law change can alter that covariance; observing the covariance does not uniquely identify its cause. Internal component updates and direct response adaptation can have the same predictions on a fixed finite action set. Fixed linear-Gaussian factorization gives exact equivalence to direct total-covariance conditioning, including correlated observation noise when both use the same total covariance. This is a limited model equality, not a general theorem that nonlinear world models cannot help.

To make a testable predictive claim, it is sufficient to specify a restricted revision whose induced forecasts differ from ordinary adaptation under an identical legal history, and assess real held-out functional outcomes. One need not prove a molecular cause to test its usefulness. For example, a predetermined change in h_pair shared across specified downstream contexts could be compared with full-history direct refit, covariance adaptation and ordinary hierarchical interaction regression. But the source evidence must prospectively define which contexts/actions share that change, what feedback estimates it, and which untouched outcomes audit it. Merely estimating a new h from all new outcomes is ordinary response-surface refitting. Merely learning when to choose one such h is contextual meta-learning unless it adds a demonstrated restricted benefit.

If additionally claiming biological rule identification, a matched intervention or measured effect modifier must discriminate rival explanations: e.g. documented target/context modulation with same drug/dose/functional assay and an independently held-out context/intervention consequence. The intervention semantics, assay normalization and costs must be authenticated. No such acquired discriminating modifier is supplied by the selected local contracts here; controls/counts do not automatically fill that role. This is a gap for attribution, not a universal minimum prerequisite to any small functional experiment.
