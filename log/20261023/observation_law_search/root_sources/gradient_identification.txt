Abstract
The overdamped Langevin stochastic differential equation (SDE) is a classical physical model used for chemical, genetic, and hydrological dynamics. In this work, we prove that the drift and diffusion terms of a Langevin SDE are jointly identifiable from temporal marginal distributions if and only if the process is observed out of equilibrium. This complete characterization of structural identifiability removes the long-standing assumption that the diffusion must be known to identify the drift. We then complement our theory with experiments in the finite sample setting and study the practical identifiability of the drift and diffusion, in order to propose heuristics for optimal data collection.

Conclusions
Contributions.
In the last decade, marginals-based SDE inference methods have been developed based on existing conditions for structural identifiability, which required a known diffusion parameter to identify the drift. In this work, we resolve this long-standing problem by proving that both drift and diffusion of an overdamped Langevin SDE are identifiable from observational data if and only if the marginals are observed outside of equilibrium. This result opens a path for novel methods that rely on fewer assumptions, by providing a theoretical basis for accurate recovery of trajectories, gene regulatory networks, and post-intervention distributions from observational data.
We also perform extensive experiments in the finite sample setting, which complement our theory while enabling us to study practical identifiability. In particular, our results and analyses suggest that measurements from highly spread out marginals provide more information about the drift, whereas marginals with concentrated around one or more points offer greater insight into the diffusion.
Limitations.
We note several important limitations. First, our main identifiability results pertain to the regime of infinite data, such that the exact marginal distributions
{
p
⁡
(
⋅
,
t
)
}
t
∈
[
0
,
T
]
\{p(\cdot,t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
are observed during a continuous time interval.
Moreover, we assume the drift to be the gradient of a potential, and the diffusion to be isotropic. However, this may not form a good model in certain applications. As an example, in single-cell dynamics, one may expect rotational dynamics due to genetic feedback loops. As another example, in hydrology, material heterogeneity requires anisotropic diffusion.
Important questions also remain with respect to practical identifiability. Although our experiments in
Section
3.2
provide heuristics for optimal measurement strategies, we note that a rigorous analysis, which quantifies the difference between the Fisher information from observed trajectories versus observed marginals, remains an important direction for future work.

Appendix A
Theoretical background and Fisher information analyses
A.1
Existence and uniqueness of solutions for a Langevin SDE
It is well-known that restrictions on the drift and diffusion terms of an SDE must be assumed in order for the SDE to yield a well-defined law on paths. Indeed, if the drift and diffusion are not sufficiently regular, then the SDE may correspond to multiple laws on paths. Conversely, if the drift and diffusion grow too quickly, then there may not exist a solution to the SDE. For concrete examples, see
