## 0. Bottom line

1. **Three days of careful work produced no core.** There were about 140 recorded phases, roughly 45 of them numerical tests on real data, and no mechanism was promoted. Every positive result is an ordinary method:
   - Gaussian or covariance conditioning on a few anchors.
   - Ridge feedback.
   - Shrinkage.
2. **The search cannot succeed as framed.** A candidate core must beat ordinary estimators and must also not be equivalent to one on the same information. With a fixed information set and squared-error scoring, the best update is the posterior mean. On small exposed assets that is an ordinary conditioning, ridge or shrinkage estimator. Novelty has to come from one of three places:
   - What the system optimises.
   - What it chooses to measure.
   - How predictions enter decisions.
3. **Many STOPs are absence of evidence.** Several gates sat above the oracle headroom of their own task, or ran at power near 0.2. Those tests could not have passed even for a correct idea.
4. **Two things work, and they fit together.**
   - Cross-context priors conditioned on a few anchors. This is the virtual cell used as a transfer prior, and it was confirmed on sealed GSE70138 and PRISM tiers.
   - Certification economics: FDR and yield certificates, verify-hits allocation, stopping rules, replicate reads and standardised endpoints.
5. **Proposed core: an evidence portfolio.**
   - Discoveries are certified with anytime-valid, design-based e-processes and e-BH.
   - Every improvement enters only as a predictable bettor inside a mixture. That covers world-model refits, allocation rules, LLM-proposed edits, and the editors that produce them.
   - FDR control then holds whatever the improver does.
   - Each improvement is credited by the evidence it earns on fresh wells. Under the null, that signal cannot be inflated.
   - Self-improvement stops being a gated, underpowered decision and becomes a process with a pathwise regret bound.
   - The world model gets an exact, decision-level contract: expected evidence lost per read equals KL(truth || prediction).
6. **The next step is cheap and decisive.**
   - Run Gate 0 (headroom) and Gate 1 (validity on null pseudo-hypotheses) on exposed Jaaks 2022 data and the exposed part of AZ2024.
   - Then open the AZ2024 413-context reserve once, under a frozen protocol, as both the confirmatory test and the first RSI arena.

## 2. Diagnosis

### 2.1 The estimator-novelty bar cannot be met on these assets

The loop accepts a core only if it meets two conditions:
- It is not equivalent to an ordinary estimator on the same information.
- It beats the strongest such estimator.

Under squared error with a fixed information set, the Bayes-optimal update is the posterior mean. In the regimes tested (tens to a few thousand units, near-Gaussian residuals), that is an ordinary conditioning, ridge or shrinkage estimator. A rule that beats it must either use different information or exploit a mis-specification that a stronger ordinary control would also fix.

The record shows this repeatedly:
- A 6.55e-15 Gaussian-equivalence check demoted the PTPC update to an ordinary joint-residual baseline.
- 29 affine stencil checks matched ordinary controlled structure.
- An optimised ordinary moment method matched the operator-renewal inference.
- The reusable-error rule lost to global shrinkage.
- The diagonal mixed-feedback law lost in 0 of 5 folds.

The owner's 10-22 advice adds why mechanistic-looking rules lose on predictive scores: structure is not prediction. With L = 2X and M = X + e (unit variances), the "true" structural predictor 2M has MSE 4, while the attenuated predictor M has MSE 2.

**Implication.**
- Close the estimator search on exposed small assets as `OPERATOR_NOVELTY_SEARCH_EXHAUSTED_ON_EXPOSED_SMALL_ASSETS`.
- Treat ordinary estimators as legitimate components.
- Look for novelty in the objective, in the information acquired, and in the interface between predictions and decisions.

### 2.2 A gate set above its headroom is not an experiment

A STOP carries almost no information in two situations:
- The margin a gate demands exceeds the task's oracle headroom.
- The minimal detectable effect (MDE) exceeds the plausible effect.

Section 1.3 lists five such cases in two days. The rule should be:
- Compute oracle headroom and MDE before any fit.
- If MDE > headroom, label the test `NOT_TESTABLE` and do not run it.
- Keep gate margins at or below half the headroom.
- Pool across assets when one asset cannot carry the power.

### 2.3 RSI cannot be tested without a task stream

The goal's own Section 6.2 requires cross-task, time-ordered evaluation on independent later tasks. Every study in this window is single-asset and single-shot, so Level 2 and Level 3 claims were untestable by construction. Two sources of ordered tasks are available:
- Public releases, which form natural ordered streams (Section 5.6).
- The AZ2024 reserve, which is large enough to split into successive campaigns.

### 2.4 Process overhead and evidence quality

- About two-thirds of phases ran no numerical test.
- Same-model debates produce critique, not evidence. Examples: 107,564 tokens across six API contexts in the CAF phase, and 79,906 tokens in the post-RNA phase. The independent recomputations are the valuable part.
- Each new source spawns its own small reserve, which fragments power. Twelve reserve definitions now exist, and most are too small to confirm anything on their own (Section 6).

### 2.5 What works is consistent

Across MAESTRO's history, two competences survive.

**Transfer priors.** A learned cross-item covariance or routed prior, conditioned on a few anchors in a new context:
- RKT on sealed GSE70138: 0.529 vs 0.287 for the generic prior.
- L1000 transfer to 421 sealed PRISM drugs: +0.116 on unseen classes.
- +17 unexpected hits in Block P (p = 0.00098, 10/10 lineages).
- The 10-22 and 10-23 conditioning gains in Section 1.1.

**Certification economics.**
- Design-based FDR and yield certificates: ALMANAC confirmatory FDR 0.043, yield coverage 0.958.
- Verify-hits allocation: +17.7% [13.1, 23.1], over five rounds.
- P2 stopping: same yield with 20% fewer measurements.
- Two-orientation history: +13.4%.
- Standardised endpoints: P(hit) rose from 55 to 77 of 141 on fresh GCP lines.
- The confirmed rule that predictions are not evidence.

The field shows the same pattern. In the 2025 Virtual Cell Challenge, models did not consistently beat naive baselines, and a regression model without pretraining reached the top of the leaderboard. Representation novelty is not the bottleneck; decision-level value is.

## 4. Proposed core mechanism: the evidence portfolio

### 4.1 Objective

Maximise certified discoveries per well, and per round, in new biological contexts.
- A discovery counts only when fresh measurements certify it under FDR control at level alpha.
- Predictions never count as evidence. They decide where wells go and how evidence is weighed.
- This formalises the confirmed R2 rule and puts the virtual cell where MAESTRO's evidence says it helps.

### 4.2 Mechanism

**1. Design-based e-processes.**
- Each candidate claim h in a new context gets its own e-process. Examples: "combination a+b exceeds the better single agent in line l", or "compound x selectively kills lineage L".
- Each confirmation read is a fresh well on a fresh plate, at a randomised position, with same-plate controls.
- Under the null, the treated read is exchangeable with its controls. A bet on its rank among k controls therefore has conditional null mean at most 1, whatever the noise distribution:
  - the multiplier is (k+1) f(rank), with f >= 0 and the f values summing to 1;
  - a bet on the sign of a paired difference works the same way.
- The product of per-read multipliers is a test supermartingale E(h,t).
- This needs only the randomised layout. It does not need Gaussian noise, which matters because the RSD study found heavy, compound-level tails. It does not need a correct model either.

**2. Predictable bets from a portfolio of experts.**
- Each read's betting function is a convex combination of bets proposed by experts j = 1..J:
  - the prior-free mixture bettor (the zero arm);
  - direct predictors;
  - world-model versions;
  - rules from fixed menus, evolutionary mutation, or deepseek-flash proposals.
- When hypothesis h starts, its initial weights pi(j,h) may come from any earlier information: other hypotheses, earlier campaigns, accumulated credit. The prior-free bettor keeps at least half the weight.
- Within h, the weights update by Bayes, in proportion to each expert's running wealth on h.

**3. Adaptive allocation, anytime stopping, e-BH.**
- The allocation policy chooses which hypotheses receive the next batch of wells, based only on the past.
- It includes a small randomisation (for example epsilon = 0.1) with logged propensities.
- At any stopping time, e-BH is applied to the stopped e-values. A stopping time can be the budget running out, the end of a round, or the agent's own decision.

**4. One ledger, two uses.**
- The per-read multipliers serve as the certificate: e-BH on the mixture wealth.
- They also credit improvements:
  - Every expert's counterfactual wealth on every observed read is computable exactly, with no extra wells.
  - Allocation rules are credited off-policy, by inverse-propensity weighting on the logged randomisation.

**5. Inheritance and an archive.**
- Between campaigns the system carries forward three things:
  - expert credit;
  - the refitted world model, trained on certified labels and on all randomised reads, with propensity weights to correct for adaptive selection;
  - an archive of certified discoveries and confirmed nulls, organised by niche (context cluster x mechanism class x assay).

```
new context --> experts (prior-free, direct, world-model versions, edited rules)
                  |  predictable bets + allocation (logged propensities)
                  v
          fresh randomised wells --> design-based e-processes, one per claim
                  |                           |
                  |                           v
                  |                 e-BH on mixture wealth --> certified discoveries
                  v                                                 |
   counterfactual wealth (bets), IPW value (allocation)             v
                  |                                    archive of discoveries + nulls
                  v                                                 |
   credit -> L1 refits, L2 rule edits, L3 editor budgets <----------+
```

### 4.3 Why validity survives any improver

1. Each expert's multiplier M_j(h,t) is non-negative with conditional null mean at most 1, by exchangeability under the randomised layout.
2. Any predictable convex combination sum_j q_j M_j(h,t) inherits that bound. So E(h,t) is a non-negative supermartingale under the null for h, however q was chosen. That holds whether q came from a gradient step, an evolutionary search or an LLM edit that saw all past data.
3. By optional stopping, E(h,tau) is an e-value at any stopping time tau. e-BH on the stopped values controls FDR at alpha under arbitrary dependence between hypotheses (Wang and Ramdas 2022; the adaptive-sampling form is Xu, Wang and Ramdas 2021).
4. **Regret.** With Bayes weights inside h, log E(h,t) >= log E_j(h,t) - log(1/pi(j,h)) for every expert j, on every path.
   - If the prior-free bettor keeps weight at least 1/2, the portfolio's evidence is never below half of the prior-free evidence.
   - The worst case therefore equals running the prior-free design at alpha/2, a cost fixed in advance.
   - A useful new expert gains weight geometrically in the evidence it earns, so it needs no gate.
5. **Goodhart resistance.** Under the null, any expert's expected wealth on fresh reads is at most 1, however it was produced. Overfitting cannot raise its credit; only correct bets on real effects can.

This is the property MAESTRO's gated studies lacked. Real but small improvements were refused because certifying the improvement itself needed more units than existed. Here only discoveries are certified. Improvements are made harmless and then credited.

### 4.4 The conversion law: how world-model error becomes wells

**The law.**
- For any likelihood-ratio bet with predictive distribution q, against a null p0, when the truth is p: expected log-evidence per read = KL(p || p0) - KL(p || q).
- For a Gaussian read in noise-SD units with true effect mu and a plug-in prediction mu_hat, this becomes mu^2/2 - (mu - mu_hat)^2/2.
- The same identity holds for the design-based rank bets, with q the predicted rank distribution.

**Consequences.**
- On true effects, the evidence lost per read is exactly half the squared prediction error in noise units.
- On nulls (mu = 0), the expected log-multiplier is -mu_hat^2/2 < 0. A wrong prediction makes the e-process decay faster, which wastes wells through allocation. It never creates a false discovery.

**Worked example.** e-BH with K = 1,000 tested hypotheses, alpha = 0.1 and k = 50 discoveries needs E >= K/(alpha k) = 200, that is 5.3 nats. Suppose the true effect is mu = 2 noise SD.

| Bettor | Evidence | Reads to reach 5.3 nats |
|---|---|---|
| Oracle bet | 2.0 nats per read | 2.65 |
| Prior-free normal mixture (scale 2 SD) | - | about 3.3 |
| Plug-in world model, error 1 SD on hits | 1.5 nats per read | about 3.5 (worse than no prior) |
| Same model as a calibrated mixture centre, N(mu_hat, 1) | - | about 3.0 |
| Calibrated model, error 0.5 SD | - | about 2.8 |

**Lessons.**
1. Score the world model on two things: expected log-evidence on true hits, and ranking quality for allocation. Global MSE or correlation is the wrong score, because most global MSE gains fall on null items, where they do not affect bets. This explains MAESTRO's recurring finding that MSE gains did not turn into decisions.
2. To help betting, a world model must be well under one noise SD on true hits, and its uncertainty must be calibrated. Most of its value will come through allocation and anchor choice.
3. Prior value is concentrated at 1-3 reads per hypothesis, which is the real confirmation regime. Gate 0 must be measured there.

### 4.5 How the two cores reinforce each other

**World model to agent.**
- Predictive distributions, which become bets.
- P(non-null) and expected evidence per well, which drive allocation.
- Anchor choice for a new context.
- Surprise estimates for exploration.

**Agent to world model.**
- Certified labels. These are far cleaner than primary-screen calls; O'Neil batch-3 primary hits re-called only 45%.
- Randomised reads with known propensities, which give unbiased refits despite adaptive selection.
- Confirmed nulls, which are negative knowledge most training sets lack.

**Both to the improvement process.** The ledger credits which world-model version, rule or editor earned evidence. That makes external validation feedback the single currency of co-evolution.

### 4.6 RSI levels and open-ended evolution inside the portfolio

**Level 1, model.**
- The world model is refitted after each campaign and the refit enters as a new expert.
- Rollback is automatic, because weight decays toward zero if the refit loses evidence.

**Level 2, procedure.**
- New bet, allocation and stopping rules enter as new experts.
- Bet rules are credited by exact counterfactual wealth. Allocation rules are credited by IPW value.

**Level 3, the improver.**
- Editors are the generators of Level 1 and Level 2 edits. Examples are refit schedules, mutation operators and deepseek-flash prompt templates.
- Each editor earns the discounted wealth share of its offspring.
- The next campaign's edit budget is reallocated by multiplicative weights (G1), against a fixed uniform budget (G0) from a common start.
- The Level 3 claim is that G1 finds credited improvements faster than G0 on later campaigns.

**Open-ended evolution.**
- Certified discoveries spawn child hypotheses: sibling contexts, mechanism neighbours, partners for certified single agents, and dose or time extensions.
- Each niche gets a minimum exploration allocation, so the search cannot collapse onto easy niches such as MEK inhibitors in BRAF-mutant lines.
- Progress is measured by two quantities:
  - Certified surprisal: the sum, over certified discoveries, of -log P_prev(non-null) under the previous world-model version.
  - Niche coverage.
- Neither can be inflated by false claims, because claims are certified, or by re-finding known effects, because those have low surprisal.

### 4.7 The goal's five questions

**1. Where is the innovation?**
- One evidence ledger both certifies discoveries and credits self-generated improvements. Recursive improvement therefore needs no statistical gate and cannot break validity.
- A randomisation null fits plate assays.
- The KL conversion law serves as the world-model contract.
- The components are classical. The architecture, and its test on release-ordered drug-screen data, are the contribution, subject to the novelty check in 4.8.

**2. Why a virtual cell?**
- It is needed only if world-model experts earn more evidence than direct-predictor experts on new contexts with the same anchors. The portfolio measures exactly that.
- Prior evidence is mixed:
  - RKT's 0.529 vs 0.287 favours transfer.
  - On AZ2024, a full direct model beat the residual world-model family.
  - On Beat AML, drug-specific ridge matched the RNA prior.
- If direct predictors match, report it and use them (question 5).

**3. Why an autonomous agent?**
- Adaptive allocation, stopping and expert generation must beat the fixed workflow at equal wells and rounds. The fixed workflow is top-k retest, verify-hits, P2 stopping and the current conformal-BH certificate.
- They must also beat a conventional ReAct agent at equal deepseek-flash calls.
- Block P showed a ReAct agent matching the MAESTRO loop, so this bar is real.

**4. Why RSI?**
- The five inheritance arms in Section 6.2 of the goal are simply different carry-forward rules for the portfolio:
  - no inheritance;
  - model state only;
  - experience only;
  - improved procedure;
  - improved improver.
- The test is the slope of wells per certified discovery across sealed campaigns.
- Expect Level 1 to carry most of the gain. Level 3 may be untestable at current sizes, and should be labelled so if it is.

**5. Could a simpler method do it?**
- The simplest competitor is a ridge or covariance prior, plus verify-hits, P2 stopping and conformal BH on replicate reads.
- If the portfolio does not beat it by the pre-registered margin, the simpler pipeline is the product.

### 4.8 Novelty: closest work and the claimed difference

**Adaptive sampling with e-processes and e-BH is established.**
- Xu, Wang and Ramdas, bandit multiple testing (NeurIPS 2021).
- Sample-efficient multiple testing with adaptive data collection (arXiv 2609.26651).
- Active multiple testing with proxy p-values and e-values (arXiv 2502.05715).
- Interactive conformal selection (arXiv 2507.15825).
- Sparse recovery with multiple data streams (Wang and Sun).

**Validity-gated self-modification is recent and close.**
- Scientific CI/CD for self-modifying discovery agents (ICML 2026).
- Self-evolving agents with anytime-valid certificates (arXiv 2607.00871).

**Claimed difference.** As far as titles and abstracts show, those systems certify each improvement before adopting it. Here, improvements are never certified: they are made harmless and then credited, while discoveries are certified. The difference matters exactly where MAESTRO operates, where true improvements are smaller than any gate's MDE.

**Before claiming novelty,** read in full:
- the ICML 2026 paper and arXiv 2607.00871;
- three items flagged in the earlier search: arXiv 2602.12112 (Mani, Vondrick, Zemel), AssayLoop and Arc's STATE.

If they already adopt improvements by mixture weight, the contribution narrows to the biological instantiation and the conversion-law contract.

### 4.9 Backup line: sparse anchor priors for selective responders

**Hypothesis.** Gaussian or covariance conditioning works on average (decryptE -45%, Beat AML -23%). But it shrinks selective responders, the items that become discoveries, toward the mean. A spike-and-slab or mixture prior conditioned on the same anchors should recover them.

**Metric.** The anchors-to-decision curve: anchors needed to reach a given share of oracle top-k hits, stratified by selectivity.

**Confirmation.** On the Emerald 16 backgrounds, the decryptE later drugs and the PTPC later backgrounds.

**Value.** Novelty is lower but practical value is high, and a better prior also becomes a stronger expert in the portfolio.

- LINCS GSE92742 replicate signatures.

**Measure.** Certified discoveries per 1,000 confirmation wells for:
- A0 and A1;
- an oracle that bets and allocates using all-replicate means as truth.

Measure at 1, 2 and 3 confirmation reads per hypothesis.

**Pass rule.** The oracle beats A1 by at least 20% (relative) within two rounds, on at least two of the three combination or viability assets.

**Fail.** `CERTIFIED_DISCOVERY_HEADROOM_INSUFFICIENT`, and move to the backup line.

**Feasibility check, same gate.** Using exposed MASTERs only, document the replicate and control structure of AZ `combo_well_stats`: fields, replicates per condition, controls per plate.

### 5.3 Gate 1: validity in replay

**Pseudo-hypotheses.** Build them from two sources, kept at their real plate positions so positional bias is caught:
- Control wells.
- Treatment wells of compounds inactive at their lowest dose.

**Pipeline.** Run them through the whole adaptive pipeline, including the self-improvement loop.

**Pass conditions.**
- The upper confidence limit of the mean stopped e-value is at most 1.
- Empirical FDR is at most alpha + 2 SE at every evaluated stopping time.

**Normalisation check.** Confirmation reads must never share normalisation with the reads used for allocation. This is the owner's -Sigma_M point: shared plate normalisation creates winner's-curse bias.

**Fail.** `VALIDITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED_IN_REPLAY`. Fix this before anything else.

### 5.4 Gate 2: component value and power

**Estimate on development data.** Each contrast in certified discoveries per 1,000 wells, paired by context:
- A2 - A1;
- A3 - A2;
- A4 - A3;
- A5 - A4;
- A4 - A6.

**MDE.** For the 413 confirmatory contexts, the MDE is about 2.5 x SD / sqrt(413), roughly 0.12 SD, at one-sided alpha = 0.05 and 80% power.

**Decision rule.**
- Carry a contrast into confirmation only if its development estimate is at least twice the MDE.
- Otherwise label it `NOT_TESTABLE`, not STOP.

### 5.5 Confirmatory run and first RSI arena: one opening of AZ2024 413

**Freeze.** Code, arms, endpoints, the stopping rule, and the hash order of the 413 contexts into four blocks of about 103. The blocks are treated as successive campaigns.

**Primary endpoint.** Certified discoveries per 1,000 confirmation wells, A4 vs A1, paired by context and pooled. One-sided alpha = 0.05.

**Key secondary endpoints.**
- A3 vs A2: world model vs direct predictor.
- A4 vs A6: framework vs conventional agent.

**RSI endpoints.**
- The across-block slope of wells per certified discovery for the five inheritance arms.
- Level 3 as G1 vs G0 from a common start.

**Validity endpoint.** The replication rate of certified hits in held-back replicate wells.

## 8. Steering the Codex goal loop

These are suggested edits to the goal objective. The owner decides; this review changed nothing there.

1. **Novelty criterion (Sections 4.2 and 13, question 1).**
   - Novelty may lie in the objective, in the information acquired, or in the prediction-to-decision interface.
   - A component being equivalent to an ordinary estimator is not disqualifying.
   - Failing to beat the strongest same-budget workflow is disqualifying.
2. **Pre-fit arithmetic (Section 8).**
   - Report oracle headroom, MDE at the planned size, and the gate margin before any fit.
   - If MDE > headroom, the test is `NOT_TESTABLE` and is not run.
   - Gate margins stay at or below half the headroom.
3. **Phase budget (Section 10).**
   - Every phase runs at least one numerical test on real data, or unblocks one scheduled within the next two phases.
   - Source qualification takes at most about 20% of phases.
4. **Debates (Section 7).**
   - Debates are design input. Independent recomputation is evidence.
   - Report debate token costs, but do not count them as progress.
5. **Focus.**
   - One primary line until Gates 0-2 decide: the evidence portfolio of Sections 4 and 5.
   - At most one side line: the backup in 4.9.
6. **Records.**
   - English decision records.
   - A fixed five-line header: question; pre-registered rule; result with interval; decision; what it does not show.
   - Index lines with spaces and units.
   - Either restore the English-only check for new day folders or record the policy change explicitly.
7. **Reserves.** Any opening needs a frozen protocol that names the reserve. Reserve status lives in `data/ASSETS.json`.

## 9. Risks, and what would change this recommendation

- **Little headroom.** If Gate 0 finds little headroom, prior-free certification is already efficient, so the world model's decision value for this objective is small. Move to the backup line.
- **Thin replicate structure.** If AZ and Jaaks replicates are thin (one or two reads, or shared normalisation), design-based e-processes will be weak, and prospective data will be needed. The first deliverable would then be a wet-lab pilot design: one or two plates per context across three to five contexts.
- **e-BH conservativeness.** e-BH is more conservative than BH. If the anytime-valid path loses more power than adaptivity gains, keep the one-shot conformal-BH certificate and use the portfolio only for allocation.
- **Prior art.** If the 2026 self-modification papers already adopt improvements by mixture weight, the novelty narrows to the biological instantiation and the conversion-law contract.
- **Selection effects.** Retrospective selection can leak. For example, PRISM secondary-screen membership was chosen from primary results. Restrict replay allocation to what the original design allowed, and say so.
