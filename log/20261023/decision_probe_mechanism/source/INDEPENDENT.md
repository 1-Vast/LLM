## What the authenticated drug task can actually observe

Beat AML source loader maps sample identifier, subject grouping, inhibitor and probit-fit AUC; RNA inputs are basal expression. One query is an archived single-inhibitor seven-dose AUC profile in that same sample, approximately seven treatment wells, not one arbitrary scalar wet assay. Lower AUC means greater ex vivo sensitivity within its defined dose range. Patient repeats require subject grouping. The published pool is incomplete; a released unavailable sample-drug profile cannot be fabricated by a world model. Historical qualification found time_of_read is not authenticated assay duration. This is not a longitudinal sequence-state library.

Therefore a legitimate offline test chooses which already measured inhibitor AUCs to reveal and which remaining measured inhibitor to nominate for the same sample. This tests a finite-pool replay decision. It does not identify outcomes for unseen combinations, drug order, treatment duration, new concentrations or clinical treatment policies. Known availability must be declared as part of the archival replay contract, not credited as a prospective assay-feasibility oracle. Selection using hidden AUC, response-derived toxicity masks or full-outcome ranking is prohibited. Same-subject outcomes cannot leak across source/target partitions.

## Ranking ambiguity is a decision objective, not identifying information

Only uncertainty capable of changing the selected action needs resolution for that decision. A shared additive offset to every drug score cannot change a ranking; a drug-dependent change can. The Beat AML historical shift audit already records this distinction: its global component cannot change ranking, while tested drug-family, potency and chronological slope explanations failed to account for the drug-dependent shift. That evidence does not certify every possible mechanism false, but it rules out presenting those existing explanations as newly supported biological components.

Choosing a query by expected reduction of final top-k regret is ordinary decision-aware active learning/value of information/best-arm identification. A probe with high predictive variance can be irrelevant if all plausible outcomes leave the winner unchanged. Conversely a modest-variance probe can be useful if it resolves a near tie and predicts another candidate. Neither observation adds new measured information. A selector trained only to predict disagreement flips can also chase wrong models; disagreement reduction is not true functional utility.

A common drift correction can reduce MSE without improving ranking; restricting updates to a ranking-relevant subspace may improve a decision metric while worsening calibration. Both consequences require true held-out endpoint scores, not a preservation or disagreement proxy. New feedback can legitimately change an old ranking; preserving it is not automatically safe biological behavior.

## Shared model component versus response/state vector

Suppose an admissible component model on a fixed inhibitor pool is y=mu+B c+epsilon, with fixed loadings B, component prior covariance C and noise R. After observing support S, ordinary conditioning induces

    yhat_Q = mu_Q + B_Q C B_S' (B_S C B_S' + R_SS)^(-1) (y_S-mu_S).

The direct finite-response model with Sigma=B C B' gives the same predictions and therefore the same rankings, query expectations and downstream measured decisions. This equality is conditional on that linear-Gaussian model and fixed pool; it does not claim all nonlinear component models are equivalent or all finite training costs identical. Keeping c rather than y can reduce storage/computation, but that must be measured against a competent direct implementation, and a computational advantage alone does not establish biological world-model necessity or RSI.

As a simple semantic example, y_A=c1, y_B=c2 and y_C=c1+c2 can be updated by changing c1 or by ordinary response covariance conditioning. Measuring A need not distinguish these implementations. The causal meaning of c1 is not supplied by naming it kinase activity. Drug annotations are generally not single-pathway interventions: dose-dependent potency, multi-target activity, cellular exposure and compensatory response can yield different viability effects despite shared nominal targets. The current AUC table contains downstream aggregate effects, not an authenticated selective target-perturbation/target-engagement bridge. No fresh biological mechanism claim is made without a new primary source; cached State methods establish context-conditioned prediction precedent, not identification of this drug mechanism.

A potentially distinguishable claim would specify a component transformation that predicts an additional real intervention outside the observed response pool under authenticated intervention semantics. It would need lawful auxiliary measurements or a selective perturbation/bridge that discriminates rival response explanations, plus a direct same-information model that cannot be dismissed by naming its representation differently. Those additional matched functional measurements and intervention semantics are not authenticated here. They are not universally necessary for all useful finite-pool prediction, only for the proposed extrapolative biological attribution. Merely updating shared parameters across patients is ordinary multi-task learning/meta-learning unless a distinct operation and benefit are established.

## Most concrete feasible diagnostic, explicitly not a new-core nomination

An exposed-development finite-pool test could ask whether decision-aware acquisition improves unseen-drug nomination more than ordinary variance acquisition or exploit-first under the same fixed direct conditional model. Before any new numeric access it would require source-only patient grouping, explicit known-availability contract, fixed candidate pool and assay budget, and no stopped low-rank component. All selectors receive the identical source drug-response covariance and the same revealed support values; they differ only in acquisition rule. Compare random, exploit-first, posterior variance, expected regret/value of information, and any proposed selective repair, charging all eight profile purchases and adaptation rounds. A direct model given the same purchased labels is mandatory for a component-update arm. Purchased drugs must be counted consistently in the final utility; otherwise probes can be falsely credited twice or their opportunity cost hidden.

Success for this small diagnostic would be a predeclared clinically modest but functional top-k AUC/regret benefit with patient-cluster uncertainty and no extra labels, alongside calibration and total-query accounting. This report does not invent a success margin, freeze a protocol or authorize the run; those need a concrete candidate after opposing review. Failure to beat ordinary decision-aware AL closes a selective-repair novelty claim. Equal predictions to direct conditioning close a component-necessity claim. Even a positive acquisition result remains ordinary agent/planning value, not world-model, inherited updater or improved-improver evidence. Independent later tasks and all requested direct/refit/CL/AL/mixture/equal-budget-search controls would still be required before a broader promotion.

The missing ingredient is not another asset list, bigger model, extra teacher or additional probes by default. It is a measured, decision-relevant error whose correction has a prospectively specified biological restriction or finite-cost operation not already matched by ordinary same-information prediction and decision-aware acquisition. Existing Beat AML supplies legal exploratory functional outcomes, but no such authenticated component-specific restriction. No model experiment is recommended solely to discover whether relabeling that uncertainty helps.
