## Specific shallow-model cost check: the bottleneck is not established

For the actual proposed scalar model f=b'h, h=tanh(u), u=A k, with p hidden coordinates and d key dimensions, squared support loss gives exact hidden loss gradient

    grad_u L = -e * b * (1-h*h).

The exact gradient update for A is therefore a p-by-d outer product using that vector and k; b uses e*h. Given the shared forward activation and residual, hidden-gradient formation is O(p). The candidate r=tanh(P[k;u;b]) needs an additional p-by-(d+2p) matrix-vector multiplication before the same rank-one A write. The common forward/write already costs O(pd); the teacher does not automatically remove a deep backward graph in this shallow setting. A competent exact implementation may be cheaper. This is operation counting under this stated architecture, not a benchmark or claim that all deep local teachers lack savings.

Consequently a better one/two-step query loss here could come from a useful source-learned bias/preconditioner, not established compute savings or a uniquely necessary synthetic gradient. Such finite-step statistical value is testable and allowed. But it needs a concrete hypothesis against exact gradient with source-learned rates, a comparable learned optimizer, head-only update and the strong inexpensive source-covariance/direct conditioning sentinel. Existing historical pilot training took seconds/tens of seconds locally and covariance was much cheaper; those records are not deployment profiling of this teacher and do not establish a general compute ceiling. Arbitrarily declaring two updates does not itself prove an application bottleneck.
