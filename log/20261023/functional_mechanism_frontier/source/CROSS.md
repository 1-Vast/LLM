## Constructive exchange: a return path is not a new update law

Constructive proposed the strongest restriction it could derive: matched measured endpoint states with different exposure histories followed by identical real rechallenge might test the sufficiency of the measured Markov state. It required fixed measurement tolerances, available history, matched drug/duration/live endpoint and controls. Source accepted but emphasized that this paper's matched late-module scores have explicitly different measured early-module scores: this does not establish identical full measured states with hidden memory. A history-aware or full-state direct predictor is a necessary opponent. Even a real benefit from history only establishes information value, not a new world-model updater or RSI.

Source challenged constructive's AlphaEdit analogy: a null-space update preserving old model responses can freeze old biological bias. It cannot certify old functional truth; an equal-budget projected optimizer implementing the same law is an exact operational comparator. Constructive accepted and added two concrete counterexamples. First, protecting late-only return keys merges states with different measured early modes and potentially different sensitivity. Second, even a richer apparently matching key could require a correct functional edit under changed history or engagement, which protection of its old prediction would prohibit. Source accepted the latter as a falsifiable hypothetical, not this paper's finding: the paper reports broadly recovered post-washout sensitivity.

Both agreed that a return-state functional protection target requires repeated measured functional calibration, a predefined error margin and explicit history/context. Unmeasured return-path function cannot become a free target/key. Even calibrated protection is scoped to its context and cannot be permanently frozen across drift. Full-rank or span-based edit infeasibility is operator geometry, not evidence of biological irreversibility. Constructive explicitly agreed that no distinct operator/legal feedback join is established and the current new executable restriction is NONE; no further asset search was recommended.

## Critic exchange: assay contracts and sparse feedback

Critic's strongest challenge matched source's distinction: similar late module with different early module can be explained by an ordinary full-state/history-aware direct model. Endpoint return of drug sensitivity cannot establish performance at intermediate unseen washout durations. Hypothetical terminal steady-state points in the author's ODE fit must not be treated as measurements; comparisons must either prohibit them or label them as identical explicit assumptions for every method. The variable-duration colony endpoint cannot be merged with a 72-hour ATP-based dose-response target. Source accepted all restrictions. Critic explicitly stated current new restriction = NONE.

Critic reported its independent reading of OnlineDFL: full true-cost vectors and a near-optimal oracle are not free in sparse cellular assays. It also distinguished SelectiveCRC's set-conditional coverage/risk from actual efficacy of a chosen treatment; a set containing a good action does not validate the selected action, and a hard assay cap needs its own selection contract. Source agreed these are comparator/feedback-contract restrictions, not evidence of a new biological update law or a GO for this project. Source did not independently retrieve these papers and does not claim to have reproduced their analyses.
