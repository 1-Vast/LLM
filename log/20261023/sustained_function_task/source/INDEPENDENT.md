## Actual functional experiment and useful distinction

The 2D assay cultures primary human NK cells with A549 tumor targets, includes cetuximab, and compares untreated control, low glucose, glutamine restriction and elevated lactate pretreatment. NK cells are pretreated for 24h, then washed before the killing assay. Therefore these are preconditioning contrasts, not continuous treatment switches within a tracked NK episode. Cells are imaged every 3min for 5h; roughly six NK cells occupy a well, so target-death assignment can involve shared effector context. At the end the cultures are fixed and stained for granzyme B. Endpoint granzyme content is a post-outcome correlate and cannot serve as lawful early support without leakage.

The primary reports automated dead-target/live-NK detection and manual individual-NK serial-killing analysis. Figure4 captions describe three independent experiments using three biological replicates; this does not establish nine independent donors. The exact donor/run identity mapping is not public in the authenticated metadata. A minimum analyzed-cell number is a figure-level inclusion count, not an independent task count or longitudinal table row count. The article reports that glutamine restriction affects the fraction of serial killers more than the fraction of cytotoxic cells, supporting a specific conditional early-versus-repeated functional distinction. This is not evidence of a learned transferable update rule.

The study has additional 3D spheroid assays with 16–20h imaging, 15min cadence and substantially more NK cells per well. Their functional assay is accumulated SYTOX fluorescence after subtracting baseline, with separate fixed 3D segmentation in some analyses. It is not automatically a single-cell serial-contact trajectory bank and must not be merged with the 2D event task. No spheroid intensity is converted into invented target-death times.

## Required schema and biological observation contract

A legal dynamic task would need explicit donor/preparation/experiment/run/chip/chamber/well/NK/target identities; treatment and pretreatment timing; acquisition timestamps; per-cell identity continuity; actual target and NK death annotations with uncertainty; contact start/end and post-death release; remaining live targets; simultaneous contacts; entry/exit/loss-to-follow-up; and selection/exclusion flags. The primary describes many measurements but the repository metadata supplies none of their column schemas or joins. Thus no public group count or support-query split can yet be frozen honestly.

The chosen future question, if those data were authenticated, would be whether early individual observed functional history improves remaining-window target-clearance prediction under a held-out donor/experiment/preconditioning context beyond early kill totals. The primary's roughly six NK cells per well requires accounting for shared target histories; a target dying after contacts with several NK cells is not a uniquely attributed kill without a validated rule. There is no repeated intervention schedule or direct NK-death intervention in the authenticated design.

The image-analysis methods state that some targets do not take up the death marker within the 5h assay; fluorescence plus bright-field morphology is used for segmentation. These are actual author annotations/assay definitions, not latent death inferred from net live-cell counts, but annotation uncertainty and cadence must be modeled. Their author local test accuracy is not a project validation. Dead NK cells are excluded in reported analysis; selected wells are matched on mean starting live-NK density. The resulting conditional population cannot establish unconditional clearance or preserved overall NK competence. A legal reanalysis needs either the full risk processes or an explicitly limited conditional estimand.

For the same authenticated early histories, a direct marked-event or trajectory predictor, ordinary multistate/semi-Markov survival fit, complete retraining, sequential sufficient-statistic/continual update and simple kill-versus-turnover mixture must see identical contact/death/target-availability inputs. A time-prefix query is real held-out later observation only after query access is sealed, not a new physical experiment. Fixed endpoint granzyme features cannot be given to a prefix model.

An ordinary transition updater uses observed event counts and exposure or the interval-censored event-history likelihood; this is a strong ordinary refit baseline, not itself a new model-update law.
