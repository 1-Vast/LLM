## Exact representation and update

Let H_t contain all legally available history at time t, including observed effector status, remaining targets and known future treatment/supply schedule. For fixed assay endpoint T, represent

    F_psi(H_t,t,T;z,w)=E[z^(K_T-K_t) w^(D_T-D_t) | H_t, schedule],

where K is cumulative correctly assigned target kills and D is effector death. This joint generating function represents sustained capacity and survival, rather than a full list of contact/kill/release hazards. A finite categorical representation can use bounded kill counts plus an explicitly overflowed tail and death state; do not silently truncate probability mass. No transition hazard or newly imagined molecular state is required.

For a genuine observed interval t to t', let DeltaK and DeltaD be its actual events. A frozen target network yields

    Y=z^DeltaK w^DeltaD * [1 if effector died or t'=T
                           else F_barpsi(H_t',t',T;z,w)].
    psi_new=psi-eta*grad_psi sum_j |F_psi(H_t,t,T;z_j,w_j)-stopgrad(Y_j)|².

The coefficient-distribution equivalent shifts future-count mass by DeltaK and absorbs on death/actual endpoint. A no-target interval is not terminal if future resupply remains possible. Cadence/interval censoring must preserve co-occurring deaths; unknown order cannot be resolved in our favor. Right-censoring is not an observed zero-future-kill terminal label. Full H permits the conditional tower identity without inventing a finite Markov state; compressing H adds an empirical sufficiency assumption.

This update trains future functional targets at intermediate actual transitions and may reuse short prefixes without estimating every hazard. It is not a fixed residual head added to a frozen predictor. Yet an ordinary distributional TD learner using the same histories, target network, horizon, representation, events and learning schedule executes exactly these targets/gradients. Calling F a functional cell world does not make it a new operator or demonstrate a world contribution.

## Finite-data hypothesis and necessary counterexamples

Potential finite-budget hypothesis: learning only a decision-relevant additional-kill/survival distribution may use scarce episodes more efficiently than estimating all contact/search/kill/release time distributions. This is an unmeasured bias/variance hypothesis, not a proof: full multistate likelihood can share parameters and use all event information, while bootstrap errors can compound. Direct Monte Carlo prediction of the same remaining endpoint distribution and ordinary distributional TD must receive identical source histories and outcomes.

Mean kills is insufficient for all shortlist criteria: always one kill and half zero/half two kills both have mean1 but probabilities of at least2kills0 versus1/2. This constructed distinction justifies a distributional endpoint if that decision criterion is real; it does not prove new learning or measured benefit. More critically, any bootstrap target can be self-consistent and wrong on histories with no eventual ground truth. Terminal/censored zeros or future target scarcity can create apparent effector exhaustion. Availability must be explicit and compared with a rich-history direct model, not gifted only to the world.

Observable supply/treatment interventions are needed to test transport rather than in-distribution forecasting. No supplied contrast means the strongest claim is forecasting under observed schedules; it does not invalidate all data utility. External resupply could restore kills without any change in effector competence, so a deficit based on elapsed time or first-kill count alone cannot be interpreted as a cell-state update. No new intervention is assumed available.
