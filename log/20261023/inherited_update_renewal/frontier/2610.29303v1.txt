Abstract
Evolutionary coding agents such as AlphaEvolve are one of the notable successes in using Large Language Models (LLMs) to build AI Scientists. These agents tackle open-ended scientific problems by iteratively improving and evolving programs, leveraging the prior knowledge and reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Despite their success, mutations over programs are mostly low-level upon surface heuristics, without a persistent unit of control. In contrast, science compounds through ideas. In this work, we propose ExpEvolve, which injects idea-level extraction and control into evolution: it discovers and leverages the high-level factors from programs that explain scoring differences among different programs. We characterize theoretical conditions on when discovery with idea factors improves sample efficiency. We also build a practical framework to show that the extracted high-level idea factors are helpful for guiding exploitation from experience and exploration when discovery comes to a plateau.
Across
11

open-ended mathematical tasks,
ExpEvolve
significantly improves the final performance and sample efficiency up to
5.0

. The improvements also transfer to
4

other LLMs and to
6
6
machine learning tasks from MLS-Bench. Analysis shows that
ExpEvolve
can discover idea-level factors to provide better exploration and exploitation for evolutionary discovery.

Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) are now widely used to build agents that operate on increasingly long-horizon tasks
(
Plaat et al., 2025
)
. One of the most ambitious applications is AI scientist agents
(
Zheng et al., 2025a
)
, in which LLMs automate the scientific discovery process, including literature surveys
(
Asai et al., 2026
)
, hypothesis generation
(
Wang et al., 2024
;
Yang et al., 2024
)
, data-driven analysis
(
Majumder et al., 2025
)
, experiment design
(
Li et al., 2025a
)
, or even the whole discovery workflow
(
Lu et al., 2024
;
Yamada et al., 2025
;
Gottweis et al., 2026
;
Mitchener et al., 2025
)
. LLM-driven discovery has shown strong results in a variety of domains, such as mathematics
(
OpenAI, 2026c
;
OpenAI, 2026b
)
, de novo protein design
(
Anthropic, 2026
)
, algorithm discovery
(
Novikov et al., 2025
;
Lange et al., 2026
)
, and the research that develops LLMs themselves, an early form of recursive self-improvement (RSI)
(
OpenAI, 2026a
;
Favaro and Clark, 2026
;
Lyu et al., 2026a
;
Karpathy, 2026
)
.
At the heart of the discovery loop is the
iteration over scientific hypotheses
, where scientific ideas compound throughout the evaluation of promising hypotheses
(
Wallace et al., 2026
)
.
Therefore, it is essential to
reuse the discovery experience
to generate high-quality hypotheses, e.g., avoiding getting stuck in repetitive mistakes or suboptimal solutions and encoding promising exploratory proposals.
In this work, we study experience reuse in open-ended discovery with evolutionary coding agents, where hypotheses are represented as
solution programs
to the underlying scientific problem, and experiences are accumulated as
evaluated programs and their scores
given by an explicit evaluator
(
Novikov et al., 2025
)
.
Typically, existing evolutionary coding agents such as
ShinkaEvolve
directly incorporate successful programs or rely on LLMs to summarize reusable insights and incorporate them as memory for future mutations with LLMs
(
Lange et al., 2026
;
Gideoni et al., 2026
;
Karpathy, 2026
)
.
However, program mutation contains an open-ended search space including low-level details such as hyperparameters and high-level insights such as optimization strategies. LLM-driven guidance is prone to focusing on low-level details
(
Karpathy, 2026
;
Pelleriti et al., 2026
)
, which may not bring genuine new scientific discoveries.
In contrast, human scientists tend to recognize the high-level contrasts among programs, accumulate and exploit scientific ideas from experience
(
Kuhn and Hawkins, 1963
;
Douven, 2025
)
.
The gap raises a challenging research question:
How can we reuse experience for evolutionary discovery like human scientists?
To tackle the question, we formulate evolutionary discovery as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process
(POMDP)
(
Kaelbling et al., 1998
)
(Sec.
3
).
In each turn, the agent needs to propose meaningful solutions to uncover the underlying causal mechanism for solving the scientific problem, i.e., maximizing the evaluation score
(
Wallace, 1981
;
Glymour,
)
.
Under the formulation, we introduce the notion of
ideas
as high-level mechanistic properties of the programs that determine the evaluation scores, e.g., the construction family or the optimization strategy (Def.
3.2
).
Without uncovering the ideas, the agent has to treat programs as unrelated and cannot tell whether a revision changes an idea or only a low-level detail, so discovery degenerates into exhaustive search (Thm.
3.1
).
Meanwhile, the identification of ideas also requires
sufficient exploration
to contrast programs with and without each idea (Thm.
3.3
). Hence, an effective discovery agent needs to extract ideas from experience, and keep exploring programs where the ideas vary.
Figure 1:
Comparison between experience reuse at program-level and idea-level. All programs solve the same grid task.
Left:
Experience reuse at program-level is prone to low-level edits. Program-level evolution derives new programs from a high-scoring parent, such as changing the heuristic weight or bounding the search loop. Both edited programs score lower.

lp = refine_lp(lp)
. Programs that contain the factor score higher than the program that omits it. This contrast singles out the factor, which is then inserted into new programs of different structures.
Motivated by the analysis, we propose
ExpEvolve
, which recognizes, structures, and leverages the high-level idea factors from discovery experience (Fig.
1
).
Specifically,
ExpEvolve
realizes the discovery of ideas via identifying high-level factors that help explain the program scoring differences. Built upon
COAT
(
Liu et al., 2025
)
, the discovered idea factors are expected to predict which program performs better given any pair of programs in the experience.
Then, the admitted idea factors will be incorporated as an action of a multi-armed bandit that controls the exploration and exploitation of different ideas during discovery.
To enable sufficient exploration,
ExpEvolve
also allocates slots for random exploration
(
Gideoni et al., 2026
)
, and idea-guided exploration when the evolution comes to a plateau. The idea-guided exploration enforces LLMs to explore diverse ideas other than those widely used in top-scoring programs.
The idea-based control in
ExpEvolve
allows efficient exploitation of promising ideas while continuing to explore programs with varied ideas.
We evaluate
ExpEvolve
on
11

open-ended mathematical discovery tasks with a budget of
200

evaluated programs, using
SCS
as the backbone and the main baseline (Sec.
5
). Under two configurations that split the budget into
20
20
generations of
10
10
programs and
10
10
generations of
20
20
programs,
ExpEvolve
improves the final performance over
SCS
and
ShinkaEvolve
in most settings with smaller budgets up to
5.0
×
5.0\times
.
Further analyses show that the discovered idea factors order the archived programs better than their code length on all
11
11
tasks. We further integrate
ExpEvolve
into
OpenRSI
(
Yang et al., 2026b
)
on six tasks from MLS-Bench
(
Lyu et al., 2026a
)
, where it improves the mean final best-so-far score of
OpenRSI
on all six tasks.

AI Scientist agents.
With the significant advancement in LLM capacity and the development of agentic systems, building LLM-based AI scientists has gained increasing attention

One research line automates part or the whole of the pipelines in scientific activities through agentic workflows

Evolutionary coding agents are one of the most representative AI scientist agents that integrate LLMs into evolutionary search over programs scored by an explicit evaluator

AI scientist agents, including evolutionary coding agents, have made impact in a variety of domains, such as mathematics
(

)
, materials science
(

)
, biology
(

;

;
Favaro and Clark, 2026
;

As generation scales up, validating the generated hypotheses becomes the main cost

(

The expanding paradigm of LLM-driven scientific discovery makes the reuse of experience

Experience reuse in evolutionary coding agents.
Existing evolutionary coding agents typically reuse experience via conditioning on evaluated programs
(
Novikov et al., 2025
;
Gideoni et al., 2026
)
or summarizing textual insights from programs into memory for future mutations
(
Lange et al., 2026
;

;

;

The useful experience can also be exploited for future search control, e.g., selection inspirations across diverse strategy clusters
(
Lange et al., 2026

Luo et al., 2026

Different from existing works that concentrate on the unit of experience as texts or programs,

discovers and exploits high-level idea factors to guide program mutations.

Discovering idea factors from unexplained pairs.
The idea discovery process in

is an iterative loop: each time the discovery is triggered,

fits a pairwise ranker on the annotated idea factors, and selects the pairs of archived programs with a large score gap that the current idea factors fail to explain. The unexplained pairs suggest the

that separate the programs in each pair.
For the selected pairs, a separate proposer LLM reads the programs to propose candidate idea factors that distinguish them. Each candidate is then annotated on the whole archive, and is admitted into the idea memory if each of its values is supported by enough programs and its values separate the scores.

4.2
Exploiting Idea Factors for Discovery
We build a two-level exploration and exploitation framework with a multi-armed bandit (MAB) to fully exploit discovered idea factors while exploring varying design spaces.
Exploration and MAB exploitation.
In each generation,

Exploration at plateaus.
Oftentimes, evolutionary discovery can concentrate around its best programs and a single strategy. Consequently, the ideas widely used in top-scoring programs stop varying, and their roles cannot be identified from the archive (Thm.

Conclusions
We introduced
ExpEvolve
, which structures the experience of evolutionary discovery into idea factors and reuses them to guide the search.
ExpEvolve
discovers idea factors from the pairs of programs that the current idea factors fail to explain, exploits them as the actions of a multi-armed bandit, and keeps exploring programs where the ideas vary, through random exploration and idea-guided exploration at plateaus. Our analysis shows that reusing a faithful idea representation reduces the number of evaluations from

⁡
(
K
)

⁡
(

⁡

)

, and that identifying ideas requires exploring programs where the ideas vary. On
11
11
mathematical discovery tasks and
6
6
tasks from MLS-Bench,
ExpEvolve
improves the final performance and the sample efficiency of evolutionary discovery under the same evaluation budget.

Budget and Resources
All methods evaluate each generated program once, and a failed program consumes the budget. The LLM calls of
ExpEvolve
for proposing, annotating, probing, and judging idea factors do not evaluate programs and do not consume the budget. Each run of
ShinkaEvolve
starts from
5
5
copies of the initial program, one for each island, and then generates
199
199
programs, so its final performance is reported at
199
199
generated programs. The evaluations of
ExpEvolve
and
SCS
are not limited in CPUs, while the runs of
ShinkaEvolve
on First autocorrelation, Second autocorrelation, Kissing, and Max–min distance ratio evaluate each program with at most
8
8
CPUs. The LLM is accessed either through the official API of DeepSeek or through OpenRouter, depending on the time of the run.

The strategy instruction of an action on a binary idea factor and on a categorical idea factor are as follows, with lines wrapped for display.

More Details of Experiments on Machine Learning Engineering
We further study whether the effectiveness of
ExpEvolve
transfers to machine learning engineering (MLE). We integrate its idea discovery and search control into OpenRSI
(
Yang et al., 2026b
)
and evaluate the resulting agent on MLS-Bench
(
Lyu et al., 2026a
)
. This setting examines the use of idea factors within a harness that supports evolution on ML algorithms towards a specific task objective.
Tasks and data.
MLS-Bench evaluates agents on improving components of ML algorithms under fixed task interfaces and evaluation protocols. Here we consider
6
tasks:
Online bandit
,
Anomaly detection
,
Clustering
,
Multi-objective optimization
,
Subgroup calibration shift
, and
Evolution strategy
.
Search leverages the scores and execution feedback exposed by each task.
Backbone and budget.
We use the evolutionary harness from OpenRSI, which organizes search through
Draft
,
Improve
,
Debug
, and
Crossover
operators.
For each task, we retain a fixed history containing 20 completed search slots from an earlier OpenRSI run. The OpenRSI baseline and OpenRSI equipped with
ExpEvolve
continue from this same history for
50
additional evaluator calls, with
3
seeds. Both methods use
5
search slots per generation.
All program generation uses Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.
The same checkpoint performs factor discovery and annotation in
ExpEvolve
.
Integrating idea factors with OpenRSI.
ExpEvolve
initializes its idea memory from the shared history and updates it with newly evaluated programs. Consistent with Sec.
4
, Discovery examines program pairs whose score differences are insufficiently explained by the current factors. The LLM reads their source and proposes reusable mechanisms.
Factors
z
z
enter the action catalog when both states have sufficient support and exhibit a score contrast in the archive.
Each generation reserves two slots for
Open
exploration and schedules three for
Guided
search.
Specifically, A guided slot first selects an action, such as
𝖠𝖣𝖣
⁡
(
z
)
\mathsf{ADD}(z)
or
𝖱𝖤𝖬𝖮𝖵𝖤
⁡
(
z
)
\mathsf{REMOVE}(z)
, and then the harness selects a high-scoring parent compatible with that change.
We use improvement over the best selected parent as action feedback, adapting the reward reference to OpenRSI’s parent–child structure.
When the cumulative best score remains unchanged within relative tolerance
10
−
4
10^{-4}
across three generation boundaries,
ExpEvolve
switches to idea-guided exploration at plateaus, aligning with Sec.
4.2
.
Evaluation and results.
We evaluate search performance using the best-so-far (BSF) score.
For each task and method, we average the final BSF score over three seeds, starting from the same 20-slot history and continuing for 50 additional evaluations. Fig.
9(a)
compares OpenRSI with OpenRSI+
ExpEvolve
across the six tasks. The reported configurations improve the mean final BSF score on all six tasks, with the largest gains on Online bandit and Anomaly detection.
