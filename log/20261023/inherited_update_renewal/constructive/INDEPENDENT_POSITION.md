## What the failures actually imply

There is evidence that feedback matters, but not yet that **the best way of converting feedback into improvement has an observable, transferable task dependence**. Calibration positives can come from instrument or batch shifts; source-duplicate tuning cannot demonstrate independent task variation; one feedback per drug cannot attribute an edge defect. These are distinct problems. A fixed average update can be useful without task-dependent update selection; conversely, a better selector requires stable variation in relative update utility. RSI cannot manufacture that variation by adding editor layers.

The missing scientific object can be stated without a new architecture. Let r_tau be latent support prediction error, r_tilde_tau=r_tau+epsilon_tau its legally observed noisy residual, and e_tau the error on the unobserved intervention menu. A common-start updater is a map U_psi:r_tilde_tau -> Delta predictions. If a prefeedback or sequentially acquired diagnostic q_tau predicts how r_tau relates to e_tau, an inherited update law can be useful. In a centered, conditionally linear-Gaussian approximation with independent measurement noise covariance R(q), a strong ordinary object is

    K(q) = Cov(e,r | q) [Cov(r,r | q)+R(q)]^{-1},
    Delta predictions = K(q) r_tilde.

Use cross-fitted conditional covariance, hierarchical empirical Bayes and context-conditioned direct prediction as strong baselines, not just scalar calibration. This is an ordinary conditional posterior rule; naming K a transport operator would not create a new mechanism. General nonlinear posterior inference can be stronger. The formula identifies exactly what prior experiments have not shown: a legal task diagnostic predicts the support-to-query correction relationship, independently of already-known context and nuisance correction. No such cross-task covariance structure is established in the current two-cell passage asset.
