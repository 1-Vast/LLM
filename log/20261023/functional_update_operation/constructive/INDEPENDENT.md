## The strongest construction considered, and why it is not nominated

A potentially useful biological restriction is **active capacity bottleneck revision**. Functional response may be limited by the least-capacity essential module, while a perturbation reveals spare capacity or compensatory routes. Support feedback could then correct the currently limiting capacities, changing predictions for previously unmeasured perturbations that share those capacities. This differs mathematically from a single signed mean direction, a Gaussian conditional-covariance correction, or an unconstrained context adapter: the active set changes at finite intervention magnitudes.

But an exact model class does not by itself specify a new updater. For example, let a legal, label-free state-to-capacity map give positive capacities c_j(z), let a perturbation have attenuation a_gj(z), and use F(c)=min_j c_j. The loss prediction is F(c)-F(a_g * c). A restriction to correcting only capacities that occur in support-active cuts is conceivable. It is NOT currently justified: an unobserved query can change the active cut, so a support-inactive capacity can become query-critical. Updating only support-active capacities silently assumes away the most important transfer failure.

Moreover, all three of c_j, a_gj, and the limiting-module assumption are currently unvalidated. Measured RNA is neither capacity nor biochemical flux, and a CRISPR state is not a drug-occupancy measurement. The exposed selected-target Chronos table supplies no independently authenticated module capacity or spare-capacity measurement. Adding learned maps merely moves the burden to labeled episodes; it does not verify the restriction.

The simplest special case makes every capacity proportional to one task-specific scalar. Then support calibrates that scalar and the query follows a fixed state-dependent ratio. This collapses to the frozen scalar calibration route excluded in the assignment, rather than providing a new mechanism. I therefore do not nominate it under a different name. Allowing arbitrary capacity shifts and learning their prior instead yields a standard structured nonlinear posterior/refit problem. A new *restriction* beyond that model class has not been supplied.

No new prior-art search was performed, so this report makes no novelty-clearance claim for capacity models, active-set adaptation, FBA, or metabolic control. Their mathematical familiarity is not the reason for NONE; lack of a validated update restriction and lack of a demonstrated separation from the informed controls are.

## A precise necessary information condition

Let I=(H,z_S,z_Q,g_S,g_Q,y_S) be everything lawfully available to an updater: historical tasks H, pre-feedback state/context, support/query identities and measured support function. Let Q denote the query function. For squared-error loss, the optimal unrestricted same-information direct/EB predictor is E[Q|I]. No updater can have lower expected loss on the true distribution. A restricted mechanistic update can still improve finite-data estimation, sample efficiency, robustness, interpretability, or compute relative to *learnable, budget-matched* estimates of that conditional mean. It needs a substantive sharing law for one of these benefits; the Bayes identity alone neither refutes empirical usefulness nor supplies a mechanism.

An update restriction must therefore say which alternatives in the conditional law of Q are ruled out by observable cell state and why. Historical support->query episodes can estimate this law only if their labels are not the new query's labels or reused aliases, and if the new task's information contract matches their state/assay timing.

## Minimal exact counterexample to support-active-cut sufficiency

This is a hand-derived mathematical check, not simulated or biological validation. There is no fitting or randomness.

Use two serial essential module capacities c=(c1,c2), F(c)=min(c1,c2), and intervention-induced function loss Y_g=F(c)-F(a_g * c). The support A has a_A=(1/2,1); query B has a_B=(1,1/2). Consider two possible unobserved capacity states:

| World | capacities | F(control) | F(A) | support Y_A | F(B) | query Y_B |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| U | (1,1) | 1 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| V | (1,2) | 1 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1 | 0 |

The legal transcriptomic/context observations may be identical in U and V unless a tested RNA->capacity relation explicitly excludes this. Both worlds agree on control function and exact support function. They disagree on query function by 1/2. Neither fitting support exactly nor preserving control resolves the query. With equal prior mass, E[Y_B|I]=1/4, and minimal conditional MSE is 1/16. For any deterministic point prediction p, max(p^2,(p-1/2)^2)>=1/16. Randomization cannot improve expected squared loss.

This refutes only the unsupported sufficiency claim: "observed support fit + its active cut uniquely tells us the needed query update." It does not refute capacity models or cases in which lawful state differentiates U and V. A noiseless state feature revealing c2 would resolve this example; it would have to be available identically to all controls. A strong direct predictor can use it as well. An informative intervention that halves capacity2 would also resolve the two worlds, but that is the query assay itself in this tiny example; it cannot be acquired for free and then counted as a held-out prediction.

## Minimal positive and negative falsification, if this restriction is revived

Positive prerequisite, not a promised algorithm advantage: in new independent tasks, a frozen label-free state statistic separates U-like from V-like spare-capacity states, and the resulting structured support update predicts disjoint functional queries better than an informed direct episodic predictor under equal total cost. Failure of separation alone is not proof that all state is useless; failure of the selected frozen statistic ends that fixed restriction.

Negative case: retain the same state/control/support but change the query-critical spare capacity as in the table. If the proposed rule asserts uniform exact transfer, the table falsifies it algebraically. If its claim is statistical, a finite independent functional test is necessary; the example identifies the mixture that the prior must represent and is not a fabricated empirical negative.

Neither case should be run by partitioning the already-exposed 16x100 labels and calling them a new confirmation. A genuinely unexposed independently grouped source or newly authenticated response endpoint is needed after a mechanism and assay contract have been frozen. Discovery-data development, if pursued, must be labeled exploration and cannot modify the old stopped result.

## Mandatory same-information competitors for any revised candidate

1. Strong direct episodic set predictor from (H,z_S,z_Q,g_S,g_Q,y_S), including interactions and nonlinear response to support residuals. It receives the same capacity annotations and intervention map if those are given to the mechanism.
2. Conditional Gaussian EB as an ordinary linear transfer baseline, PLUS a structured non-Gaussian EB/posterior predictor on the exact same capacity forward model and same historical episodes. The latter matters because a Gaussian-only comparator needlessly excludes active-set switching.
3. Meta-initialization with support adaptation, trained through held-out functional queries from historical tasks; same states, support count, parameter capacity and search budget. An untrained/linear first-step version is not enough.
4. Ordinary union refit/MAP of the same capacity forward model, with the same lawful historical and support likelihood and prior. Also a direct union refit on raw state/features. Restricting ordinary refit to transcript reconstruction would be unfair.
5. Same forward model, fixed updater versus learned updater; learned updater with shuffled historical support->query pairing; no support function; and measured-state direct model without intervention simulation. These separate model-class, history, feedback and true world-model contributions.

Count source acquisition, preprocessing, representation learning, hyperparameter/program trials and failed updates in total cost. If a structured posterior duplicates the proposed update exactly, it explains the benefit unless a separately testable computation/estimation property remains. Conversely, a posterior family label alone does not establish duplication: the exact likelihood, restrictions, history and inference budget must match.

## Agent, inheritance, and RSI boundary

Choosing an informative support assay could exploit capacity ambiguity, but a fixed optimal-design algorithm is a strong agent control. Transferring a state->capacity map is model/knowledge inheritance; transferring an empirically validated support->query update restriction is updater inheritance. Neither is Level3 RSI. A subsequent improved-improver claim would require a common-parent, same-history, equal-total-budget comparison of a changed edit-generation/evaluation strategy against its frozen predecessor on later independent task families, scoring descendant improvements rather than parent associations. No such mechanism or evidence is supplied here.

## Outcome and next discriminating question

The concrete constructive result is a sharper requirement: identify a lawful cell-state measurement that disambiguates *query-critical support-inactive mechanisms*, then specify why a restricted update uses it more efficiently or robustly than the informed direct/EB/refit controls. At present this measurement->update-law bridge is missing. No mathematical requirement for complete dynamics, exact physical occupancy, or perfect biological identifiability is imposed; the demand is only enough identifiable structure for the specific claimed functional-query gain.

No new core mechanism, virtual-cell necessity, agent gain, inheritance gain, joint gain or RSI gain is established. Full scientific objective remains active; this local NONE is not a global blocking claim.
