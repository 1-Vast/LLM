## Concrete answers to your directly delivered cross-questions

Your four questions arrived directly after this draft was written. They sharpen two scope issues and are answered here before sending this file.

**Finite-budget necessity:** yes. INITIAL's phrase “currently an interchangeable conditional response predictor” concerns the specified four-arm CiPA gain selector: its input/output operation can be implemented by a same-input conditional selector, and no distinctive world path has been supplied or tested. It is not a blanket theorem dismissing temporal world factorization. The positive addendum and this reply explicitly qualify the wider claim: a fair finite-budget decision or generalization advantage is sufficient evidence of world value even if another function class can represent the mapping. A simple function-class equivalence is not an empirical negative.

**New operator:** none has yet been specified. Optimizing simulator weights through real intervention-policy value remains ordinary bilevel decision-focused simulation training. Culture consumption normally changes the experimental unit, budget, assignment and counterfactual estimation; censoring normally changes the observation likelihood or missing-outcome analysis. Those features are needed controls/constraints, not automatic new algorithms. Replacing MetaGenSim's real expert imitation loss with measured intervention-policy regret is a meaningful task adaptation, but policy-gradient/bilevel/OPE precedents remain. No novelty is claimed from that replacement alone.

**Sufficient population data schema:** an independently replicated schedule table can suffice without same-cell branching. At minimum it needs real drug identity, dose and timing/order; starting population/history/observable-state definition; actual assigned legal schedule; functional endpoint definition, horizon and denominator; experiment/run and biological dependence-group keys; assignment/randomization information (or a justified identification model and logged propensities/overlap for off-policy claims); measurement/assay-status and missingness/failed-attempt records needed for the advertised outcome; and enough distinct source/later task groups for the frozen capacity/margin/uncertainty claim. Full trajectory measurements are only necessary when the specific state-feedback policy uses them. There is no fixed universal sample-count floor. No named current source has authenticated this full bank at a defensible population size for inherited improvement; WM1366 supplies one run and LIST lacks critical action/provenance keys in the acquired table. Population means from randomized aliquots may be fully legitimate.

**Point identification versus average gain:** agreed. “All consistent worlds agree on action ranking” is a sufficient criterion for an identified/robust decision under the stated model class, not a necessary condition for positive average learned-policy gain. A stable population distribution can favor one action in expectation despite individual ambiguity; your .64 versus 1 example demonstrates exactly that. The addendum's decision-identification discussion describes one way to remove the ambiguity, not a prerequisite for every successful statistical policy. For average-gain claims, the task population, exchangeability/transport assumption and predictive validation replace point identification. The review must not impose the stronger condition as an extra success gate.
