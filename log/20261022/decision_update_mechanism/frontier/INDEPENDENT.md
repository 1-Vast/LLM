## SelectTTRL's operation is precise, but correctness is not update value

The paper obtains a direction from an independent labeled calibration set, using normalized mean differences between hidden activations at teacher-forced correct versus incorrect answer positions:

    v = normalize(mean_i(h_i_correct - h_i_incorrect)).

For each target question it generates an initial response, computes cosine alignment c(q) with v, sorts all questions by c, and selects a fixed percentile interval. The configured interval is the ascending 25th–60th percentiles, retaining 35% of questions. It then applies **the same ordinary TTRL pseudo-label voting and policy update** on selected questions; excluded questions remain in evaluation. This is a real cheap selection operation before expensive rollout, not an added gradient formula.

The reported source of the direction is TruthfulQA; the calibrated direction is frozen. Each selected question uses 32 responses for pseudo-label voting and 16 for optimization, with Qwen backbones and four A800 GPUs in the reported setup. Random and perplexity subset controls retain the same 35%, with the same downstream pipeline and training hyperparameters. The full-set TTRL baseline uses the entire pool. Appendix C reports aggregate reductions of 18.38% generated tokens, 18.33% update-response tokens and 6.48% training-step time, plus 118.77 minutes/7.918 GPU-hours saved across the six reported benchmarks. Retaining 35% of questions is **not** proof of a 65% end-to-end saving. Initial response generation, direction calibration, selection and evaluation have costs; this audit does not independently certify the full accounting.

Theorem 4.5 establishes positive association between the score and a fresh answer's correctness under its stated latent-evidence conditions. It does not establish positive association with the **incremental final utility of training versus leaving the model unchanged**. Even perfect knowledge of pre-update correctness cannot determine that counterfactual difference. The middle-percentile rationale combines learning headroom and pseudo-label reliability, and its reported success is empirical; it is not a theorem that the interval is utility-optimal or safe for every question. Since policy updates share model parameters, evaluating all questions also matters: training on one question can affect other decisions.

For a drug-response learner, historical source assays could in principle label correct versus inaccurate forecasts. But a source-trained forecast-confidence direction alone does not say whether revising a model will improve the final experimental shortlist. Real functional labels are not equivalent to majority-voted pseudo-answer correctness, and no biologically certified relation between a hidden correctness axis and transferable update benefit is supplied here. Replacing c(q) with a learned utility score from common-start source episodes would be an ordinary update/data-selection learning problem; naming it decision-aware inheritance adds no distinct mechanism. The prior ALMANAC development truth cannot be used to choose such a gate. I do not propose this transplant.

## ReTTT changes the generator of future supervision, with a real control

ReTTT maintains explicit procedural entries z=(type,content,evidence) from the interaction history. The current adapted model generates the next lessons. Deterministic checks reject malformed, ungrounded or trajectory-inconsistent items. Same-type entries are matched by normalized-content Jaccard similarity; Appendix A.3 uses fixed thresholds .3 and .7 for insertion versus merge/replacement. All new/revised entries plus bounded uniform replay of unchanged entries form the training set. Target-token cross-entropy updates episode-local LoRA; the same adapted model then generates both actions and subsequent reflections. If no entry changes, there is no parameter update.

Thus the source supplies an operational loop affecting how later training material is produced. The frozen-reflection control retains action-policy adaptation and memory machinery but generates reflections with the initial policy. On the reported ALFWorld/Qwen3.5-9B experiment it obtains 56.43% success versus ReTTT-QA's 63.57%, a 7.14-point difference. Context-only memory reaches 53.57%, compared with the frozen agent's 47.14%. These are meaningful author comparisons; I do not dismiss them merely because the mechanism contains standard fine-tuning or because it is within one episode.

The boundary is equally concrete: adapter, optimizer state and procedural memory reset at every episode. Appendix A.2 explicitly lists **Cross-episode persistence: None**. Therefore this paper does not establish inherited improvement efficiency on independent later tasks. A shared adapted reflector is evidence for an in-episode component benefit, not proof that an inherited improver is better at producing the next generation of useful updates.

Its reported finite setup has two rank-4 LoRA components on the final attention q_proj, AdamW, memory capacity 12 entries and target-token budget 768. ReTTT-QA uses 7.14 gradient steps and 524.78 loss-bearing token exposures per episode versus aTTT's 12.93 and 573.69. However reflection adds approximately 27875.66 input and 1175.30 output tokens, and aggregate worker time is 2.955 times Base versus aTTT's 2.724—about **1.085 times aTTT**, not lower total runtime. The paper itself states this tradeoff. A drug-discovery adaptation must charge inference, screening, training and actual functional feedback, not only gradient count.

The deterministic validity checks use schema, referenced trajectory steps, string predicates and task-outcome consistency; there is no additional learned entailment model. This fits explicit action prerequisites and observed environment feedback. It cannot turn a single scalar growth response into a verified mechanistic explanation such as “pathway X caused resistance.” A source-rich functional campaign may support literal measured-outcome statements, but counterfactual drug lessons need stronger evidence than textual consistency. No such biological contract is newly authenticated here.

## One examined mechanism gap: evidence revision versus parametric revision

The only specific extension I considered is ensuring that a corrected or replaced lesson changes the learned state consistently, rather than merely editing the explicit memory. ReTTT revises memory and trains on new/revised entries, but the inspected update rule contains no operation that removes the replaced entry's earlier parameter effect. If an old entry produced phi_1=phi_0-eta*g_old(phi_0), then applying the corrected entry gives phi_2=phi_1-eta*g_new(phi_1), generally different from the corrected-history state phi_0-eta*g_new(phi_0). Intervening updates and AdamW state make exact undo more demanding. This is a mathematical distinction, not a claim that ReTTT promises exact unlearning or that its measured improvement is invalid.

A possible real functional use would be a campaign receiving a legitimately corrected assay measurement or revised control normalization, then revising subsequent intervention predictions without carrying a stale update. This is a meaningful prospective task only if the correction is independently valid and time-stamped, and later decision outcomes are separated. The current static ALMANAC diagnostic supplies no such revision stream; noisy evidence is not automatically erroneous evidence to delete. No corrected biological observation, new dataset or protected value was acquired.

The nearest faithful ordinary operations are concrete. Checkpointing model **and optimizer state**, then replaying the corrected suffix, reproduces the chosen training program under fixed data order/randomness and pays for that replay. In linear ridge or Gaussian sufficient-statistic models, subtracting the old observation's sufficient-statistic contribution and adding its replacement permits familiar matrix downdates/updates. Those are actual finite algorithms, not free converged nonlinear oracles. They must receive the same corrected evidence and their compute must be counted.

I have not supplied a new nonlinear update that preserves a declared corrected-history decision property with less cost than those operations. Simply subtracting an old gradient is not exact after later state-dependent/Adam updates; a low-rank sufficient-statistic design would be an ordinary convex-memory construction unless a further technical contribution were shown. Therefore this is **a gap description, not a qualified candidate**. It is not a reason to add a revision editor or reflection layer to MAESTRO now.

A falsifier for a future concrete proposal would be predeclared comparison against corrected-history checkpoint replay at matched legal observations: does the proposed cheap revision meet its claimed state/prediction tolerance and improve later functional selection at lower total cost, with source choices frozen before the independent campaign? Failure of the tolerance or claimed cost/decision benefit stops that specific proposal. A fresh-fit comparator must not be made free, and approximate useful forgetting must not be rejected solely because it differs from exact replay; it would need an explicit decision-risk claim. No such program or experiment is offered or executed here, and no new evidence justifies reopening old stops.

## Decision

These sources sharpen two distinct requirements: a signal correlated with current accuracy is not a guarantee of beneficial updating, and changing later training-material construction is an actual operation whose feedback validity, total cost and transfer boundaries must be established. The source mechanisms themselves should be credited in their reported domains. Transplanting their labels into drug discovery does not fill the current missing technical contribution.
