## Frontier: a fair conditional counterexample, not rejection of the paper

The explicit sensitivity rows produce the stated matrices. PlanA's two off-diagonal terms cancel upon pooling; PlanB duplicates its weak parameter direction. The rows have the same permitted norm cap; every segment has equal trace, determinant and eigenvalues, and each plan has four scalar readings. Thus unequal total sensitivity, singular segments and extra assay readings do not explain the Gaussian contrast/MSE result. The primary uses one theta throughout its complete trajectory and the same CMA-ES fitter across design methods; applying a fixed-global-parameter counterexample is relevant to that mathematical setup. It is not a secretly time-varying-parameter foil.

However, the author paper does not claim universal superiority. A synthetic counterexample to a universal risk surrogate does not by itself justify negative average transfer for a realistic nonlinear biology model or finite-budget nonlinear estimator. Local normalized separation could conceivably help when nominal sensitivities are inaccurate, different intervals have different unmodeled biases, or CMA-ES otherwise follows poor compensating fits. The present exact linear Gaussian experiment does not address those possibilities. None of them proves that a maximum-over-segments score is robust: optimistic selection of one contaminated segment could also fail. A specific supported misspecification class and a comparator using that same class would be required before any robustness claim.

There is a legitimate conditional positive certificate. For positive-definite matrices, S_i(F)=min_{z:z_i=1} z^T F z, so S_i(sum_s F_s)>=sum_s S_i(F_s)>=max_s S_i(F_s). Unnormalized local separation can therefore certify a lower bound on pooled information under the shared model. It is sufficient, not necessary, and the counterexample demonstrates how loose it can be. Normalization/logging/maximization does not turn that conservative bound into decision-risk optimality. This is standard variational information algebra, not a proposed MAESTRO contribution.
