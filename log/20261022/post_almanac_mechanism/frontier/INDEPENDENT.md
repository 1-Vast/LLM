## Newly authenticated primary mechanisms

1. **Biology-in-the-loop: Amortized Adaptive Hit Discovery in CRISPR Screens**, Edwards et al., arXiv:2609.11877v1, 2026-09-10. AssayFormer learns a history-conditioned acquisition policy from 1,349 historical screens; supervised masked-history prediction is followed by GRPO over full trajectories, with a context-delta reward relative to a context-free reference evaluated on the same remaining candidate pool. AssayLoop adds an LLM warm start; AssayLLM directly post-trains an LLM. The paper reports 1,389 total screens, temporal 1,349/20/20 splits, 10 rounds of 100 acquisitions and 5.67-fold enrichment / 27.7% recovered hits. These are author reports, not reproduced outcomes. This already implements a transferable rule for converting real functional hit feedback into future experiments. It is substantially closer than generic DGM to a proposal consisting of an inherited biological acquisition program.

2. **Amortizing Sequential Experimental Design in Reasoning LLMs**, Kriz et al., arXiv:2610.20832v1, 2026-10-14. The distinct source operation is to train reasoning plus action with information rewards computed from a specified external generative model and fixed contrastive hypothesis set, decoupling the policy's beliefs from the experiment model. It explicitly investigates expected versus realized information reward, prior misspecification and non-myopic rewards. Moving this to drug assays requires a calibrated biological likelihood and useful decision target; replacing the information target with assay utility is standard decision-focused amortized design unless another operation is supplied. Information acquisition under its simulator is not demonstrated measured drug utility.

3. **Deep Adaptive Bayesian Screening**, arXiv:2607.16927v1. DABS analytically integrates continuous effect sizes and noise in a conjugate spike-and-slab model, trains a discrete adaptive design policy on information about activity support, and performs posterior inference at deployment. Its strong-heredity prior makes an interaction active only when both main-effect indicators are active. Appendix B already tests weak-heredity mismatch; changing to a less restrictive interaction prior alone is not a distinct mechanism. The paper's general claim that strong heredity limits all joint support patterns to 2^p only holds in the deterministic-interaction special case: with 0 < pi_int < 1, each allowed interaction still has its own indicator. No claimed scale benefit is imported without checking this distinction.

## One concrete attempted design, and why it is not promoted

The most concrete design considered is **feedback-credit correction for finite candidate depletion**. It addresses a real structural problem: the value attributed to new functional feedback should not change merely because earlier actions remove candidates that a reference would have selected. For horizon T, define a learned world-based value Phi_t(h) as expected future measured hit utility from the current legal assay state, and train the acquisition policy with r'_t = y_t + Phi_{t+1}(h_{t+1}) - Phi_t(h_t), setting Phi_T = 0. The inherited object would be a calibrated value/advantage estimator trained on source campaigns; it could accelerate later acquisition learning.

This is a precise operation, not just calling a model a world model. However, the displayed rule is ordinary potential-based reward shaping, and learning its potential is ordinary value estimation. A faithful baseline can implement the same estimator and telescoping reward with the same source trajectories. A useful equal-budget gain over AssayFormer's stated reward would therefore not, by itself, establish an original MAESTRO algorithm. No novel version is claimed or promoted. The missing technical step is a demonstrably more sample-efficient way to estimate and inherit the *value of correcting a world-model update* under noisy, adaptively selected and physically bundled assay feedback, without giving the proposed policy privileged counterfactual labels. That estimator and its benefit are not supplied here.

This differs from stopped anchor transport: it concerns the objective used to learn an acquisition rule under candidate depletion, not adjusting response predictions with an anchor residual. It differs from the stopped continuation toy: the issue is a precise shaping bias in a newly authenticated source, not discovering a delayed compound edit. These distinctions make the analysis worthwhile, but do not make the conventional repair novel.

## Executed minimal check and falsifier

research/post_almanac_frontier_20261022/check_context_reward.py exhaustively searches small deterministic synthetic candidate pools. For the paper's stated reward, a fixed reference ranking chooses its highest-ranked remaining candidate, while only the actual acquisition removes a candidate. A four-candidate, three-step witness with labels [0,1,0,0] is sufficient:

- Actual sequence [0,2,1] finds one hit. Reference hits over the corresponding remaining pools are [0,1,1], so total context-delta reward is -1.
- Actual sequence [2,3,0] finds zero hits. The reference repeatedly selects the still-present non-hit 0, so total context-delta reward is 0.

Thus optimizing the stated sum of context-delta rewards can strictly prefer fewer discovered hits. The issue is not an action-dependent baseline at the current state alone: future reference rewards depend on prior actions and enter the remaining-return advantage. This is a synthetic restriction to batch size one and a deterministic reference, not a claim that the released stochastic implementation necessarily realizes this behavior or that author empirical gains are false. The code, exact result and scope are retained. No biological training or new assay was performed. Conventional terminal hit reward or a properly telescoping potential removes this particular mismatch; that supplies a faithful comparison, not a new mechanism.
