4.5 Training procedure Training proceeds in three stages: gene embedding initialization, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning fine-tuning. Gene embedding initialization. We initialize the gene embeddings v g ∈ ℝ d g v_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{g}} ( d g = 10 d_{g}=10 ) using a probit version of Bayesian Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (BPMF) [ 41 , 1 ] . Let Y i ​ j ∈ { 0 , 1 } Y_{ij}\in\{0,1\} indicate whether gene j j scored as a hit in training screen i i . We model Z i ​ j = U i ⊤ V g j + ε i ​ j , ε i ​ j ∼ 𝒩 ( 0 , 1 ) , Y i ​ j = 𝕀 [ Z i ​ j > 0 ] , Z_{ij}=U_{i}^{\top}V_{g_{j}}+\varepsilon_{ij},\quad\varepsilon_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),\qquad Y_{ij}=\mathbb{I}\left[Z_{ij}>0\right], (4) with isotropic Gaussian priors U i ∼ 𝒩 ⁡ ( 0 , σ u 2 ​ I d g ) U_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{u}^{2}I_{d_{g}}) on screen factors and V g j ∼ 𝒩 ⁡ ( 0 , σ v 2 ​ I d g ) V_{g_{j}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{v}^{2}I_{d_{g}}) on gene factors ( σ u = σ v = 1 \sigma_{u}=\sigma_{v}=1 ). Posterior distributions are estimated using Gibbs sampling with 2,000 iterations (1,000 burn-in, thinned by 2). Gene embeddings are set to the posterior mean v g = 𝔼 ⁡ [ V g ∣ Y ] v_{g}=\mathbb{E}[V_{g}\mid Y] . Genes absent from the factorization receive v g = 𝟎 v_{g}=\mathbf{0} and a static bias at their marginal hit frequency. Algorithmic details are provided in Appendix D.1 . This initialization is important for downstream performance, as discussed in Appendix B.1 . Supervised fine-tuning. The model is trained to predict hit labels for unobserved genes given simulated histories of varying length, using binary cross-entropy. For each training screen, a random subset 𝒪 \mathcal{O} of the screen’s genes (where | 𝒪 | ∼ 𝒰 ​ { 0 , 1024 } |\mathcal{O}|\sim\mathcal{U}\{0,1024\} ) is revealed as observed context. To ensure the model encounters informative histories, with probability 25% we resample 𝒪 \mathcal{O} to contain at least one hit: we sample n hits ∼ 𝒰 ⁡ { 1 , min ⁡ ( | H | − 1 , | 𝒪 | ) } n_{\text{hits}}\sim\mathcal{U}\{1,\min(|H|-1,|\mathcal{O}|)\} and replace n hits n_{\text{hits}} genes in 𝒪 \mathcal{O} with randomly drawn hits, capping at | H | − 1 |H|-1 to leave at least one hit unobserved. The model is trained on a balanced set 𝒪 ¯ \bar{\mathcal{O}} of unobserved genes (all unobserved hits plus an equal number of randomly sampled non-hits): ℒ SFT = 1 | 𝒪 ¯ | ​ ∑ g ∈ 𝒪 ¯ BCE ⁡ ( σ ⁡ ( a ϕ ​ ( g ∣ h t − 1 , c s ) ) , y g ) . \mathcal{L}_{\text{SFT}}\;=\;\frac{1}{|\bar{\mathcal{O}}|}\sum_{g\in\bar{\mathcal{O}}}\mathrm{BCE}\!\Big(\sigma\big(a_{\phi}(g\mid h_{t-1},c_{s})\big),\,y_{g}\Big). The supervised policy is greedy and does not account for the fact that decisions at one step can influence the information gained at future steps [ 37 ] . We therefore fine-tune the policy using GRPO [ 42 ] on full T T -step trajectory rollouts. For each training screen, we perform G = 8 G=8 rollouts. Since the policy network is deterministic, rollout diversity is produced via Gumbel-top- k k sampling [ 26 ] : B t = Top ​ - ​ b ⁡ ( { s ~ g : g ∉ h t − 1 } ) ; s ~ g = a ϕ ​ ( g ∣ h t − 1 , c s ) + τ ​ ϵ g , ϵ g ∼ Gumbel ​ ( 0 , 1 ) , B_{t}=\operatorname{Top\text{-}b}\big(\{\tilde{s}_{g}:g\notin h_{t-1}\}\big);\qquad\tilde{s}_{g}=a_{\phi}(g\mid h_{t-1},c_{s})+\tau\,\epsilon_{g},\quad\epsilon_{g}\sim\text{Gumbel}(0,1), where τ = 1 \tau=1 is the sampling temperature. Fresh noise is sampled at each acquisition step, so trajectories diverge as context accumulates. The reward uses a “context delta” approach. At each step t t , the reward is the difference in hits found by the context-conditioned policy relative to a frozen, context-free policy: r t = | { g ∈ B t π : y g = 1 } | − | { g ∈ B t π 0 : y g = 1 } | , r_{t}\;=\;\big|\{g\in B_{t}^{\pi}:y_{g}=1\}\big|\;-\;\big|\{g\in B_{t}^{\pi_{0}}:y_{g}=1\}\big|, where B t π B_{t}^{\pi} is the batch sampled by the current policy and B t π 0 B_{t}^{\pi_{0}} is sampled from the same set of unobserved genes by the frozen policy π 0 \pi_{0} without context history. π 0 \pi_{0} is initialized by the supervised fine-tuning phase. Optionally, we update the weights of π 0 \pi_{0} from π \pi every n n epochs. This reward function is aligned with the hit-discovery objective in Eq. 2 while explicitly encouraging the model to exploit the revealed context to outperform the static prior. Advantages are computed as remaining returns G t ( i ) = ∑ s ≥ t r s ( i ) G_{t}^{(i)}=\sum_{s\geq t}r_{s}^{(i)} , normalized across the group at each step. The policy is updated by the REINFORCE gradient of Plackett–Luce log-probabilities weighted by advantages, with an entropy bonus (coefficient 0.01). The fine-tuned policy π RL \pi_{\text{RL}} is sampled greedily at inference time. 4.6 LLM handoff strategy We combine the biological prior knowledge of LLMs with the adaptive capabilities of AssayFormer via a handoff strategy. For the first k k rounds, an LLM acts as the acquisition policy: given the screen description c s c_{s} and observed history h t − 1 h_{t-1} in context, it produces a list of b b genes, providing a biologically informed warm start. We then switch to the amortized acquisition policy π RL \pi_{\text{RL}} for the remaining T − k T-k rounds, allowing the learned policy to adapt to screen-specific feedback accumulated during the warm-start phase. We select k = 3 k=3 on the validation set. We refer to the resulting composite policy as AssayLoop . 4.7 AssayLLM AssayLLM is an LLM acquisition policy post-trained for adaptive hit discovery. At acquisition round t t , the model receives the screen description c s c_{s} and the observed history h t − 1 h_{t-1} as a multi-turn conversation, and returns a ranked list of b b genes defining the next acquisition batch B t B_{t} . We train AssayLLM for two deployment settings: as a standalone policy controlling all T T acquisition rounds, and as a warm-start policy controlling the first k k rounds before handing the accumulated history to AssayFormer . Both variants use the 27B-parameter Qwen 3.6 backbone and share a two-stage post-training procedure. We first perform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on acquisition trajectories and reasoning traces generated by GLM-5.1. This stage teaches the model to follow the sequential acquisition protocol, incorporate experimental feedback, avoid repeated selections, and reliably produce parseable gene batches.
