## The one selected application candidate

**Decision:** prioritize whether the next cefotaxime dose must occur on time to prevent evolutionary escape when a mixed susceptible/resistant bacterial population has already received its first dose. This differs materially from predicting static intermediate-dose alamarBlue from a separate high-dose profile: action is dose timing under changing population composition, and target is subsequent viable population suppression rather than a fixed drug-dose endpoint.

Primary source: King et al., *Fitness seascapes are necessary for realistic modeling of the evolutionary response to drug therapy*, Science Advances2025, DOI **10.1126/sciadv.adv1268**, PMCID **PMC12153978**, first-publication metadata2025-06-11. ### Actual measurements versus possible task counts

- All16combinations of four engineered beta-lactamase mutations in E.coli DH5alpha have measured cefotaxime OD dose-response growth curves. Methods report one plate per genotype, eight wells per concentration, OD every2h for20h. These are16genotypes from one combinatorial background, not16independent evolution histories.
- A fluorescence time-kill calibration uses resazurin/alamarBlue, approximately5h, ten time points, three technical replicates per condition. This is an empirical calibration for net growth/death, not a lineage-resolved genotype-frequency measurement.
- The genuinely repeated-dose experiment mixes **genotypes0,1,2**, with three arms: all three doses, missed second dose, missed third dose. Passage every8h; dose is adjusted at passage; AB estimates cell count. Figure6reportsN8per condition, while Methods describe technical-replicate wells. Do not call them eight independent donors, ancestors, genotype backgrounds or transfer tasks.
- Cells are not resuspended in fresh media; the authors explicitly note residual drug carry-over up to about1ug/ml. This is part of the intervention history, not evidence of a cell-intrinsic memory state. It must be included in a fair pharmacokinetic baseline.
- The many simulated patients, pharmacokinetic parameter combinations and1000simulations per nonadherence setting are **computational outputs**, not new observed experimental regimens. They cannot provide independent real test labels for a real-feedback inheritance or RSI claim.

The paired state/function needed for a genuinely learned transition rule remains incomplete: genotype-specific static growth curves exist, nominal initial mixture is known, and repeated-regimen total counts exist, but measured post-dose genotype composition or a broad bank of independently replicated mixture-by-schedule transitions is not authenticated. A16genotypestatic panel cannot be silently joined to three-mixture-regimen outcomes as if all16were tested under each schedule.

### Exact falsifiable scientific hypothesis and novelty status

A narrow useful hypothesis is: **the genotype-dependent switch between drug-free growth advantage and drug-exposed net-growth advantage predicts the sign of the second-dose timing effect on later viable count, beyond initial total count and a single effective IC50.** Its explicit operation is to update a genotype-mixture state during exposure and recovery using measured genotype-specific dose-response growth/death, then compare the same elapsed-time dose actions with residual-drug carry-over retained.

This is testable in principle, but **it is not a new algorithmic core**: the original paper already implements genotype-specific fitness seascapes in pharmacokinetic/population simulations and validates the importance of the second dose. Standard replicator/mutation-selection dynamics with PK, filtering of an unobserved mixture and conventional decision-focused fitting can implement the proposed operation. Repeating it, fitting its parameters after feedback or selecting further schedules by expected information does not exceed ordinary filtering/active learning. No additional identifiable operator, error guarantee or improved-improver procedure is supplied here. I therefore do not promote it on biological interest alone.
