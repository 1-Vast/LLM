Introduction
Latent world models let an agent plan in representation space. An encoder
compresses an observation into a latent representation, a predictor advances that latent
under an action, and a planner searches for an action sequence that carries the
latent close to the goal. Every part of the loop involves the latent,
and learning this representation is a central design choice
that varies across latent world models

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)

widely chosen for their simplicity: a JEPA predicts the target’s representation
from the context’s representation, and that prediction objective is what shapes
the representation. Left unconstrained, the representation collapses to a constant,
so JEPAs rely on a regularizer to keep the
embedding informative. Recent instances of JEPA use regularizers that encourage
the embedding to lie in a structured distribution, most commonly an isotropic
Gaussian or a variant of it

4

50
,

LeWorldModel (LeWM)

builds a latent world model on this recipe,
training an encoder and predictor end to end from pixels with a two-term loss:
next-latent prediction and SIGReg

embedding toward an isotropic Gaussian. Similar to many other latent world models

between the predicted terminal latent and the goal latent.
The simplicity of the cost, however, overlooks two sources of noise in the representation:
(i) the regularizer promotes variance in which noise fills the directions
carrying no signal; and (ii) the encoder, trained to represent observations,
retains content irrelevant to planning
[

measured partly along directions that carry no information about reaching the goal.
Ironically, the same regularizer that injects the noise also gives the
structure to repair it. The embedding is driven to be stationary under the
transition, and reading the latents as a Gaussian world model in the sense of
Klindt et al. [26]

the transition itself: fit the state dynamics and the covariance comes with it.
By projecting onto a subspace determined by the covariance, we obtain a
representation restricted to the directions that are certain with respect to
the state dynamics. In general, the subspace moves under the transition,
but we show that it is fixed under a normality constraint on the state
dynamics. All of
this is post hoc: the only thing we fit is a constrained linear function; the
encoder, the predictor, and the planner are untouched.

Planning in latent space.
Model-based planning on a learned latent has a long lineage

[

execute, replan. PlaNet
[

planning cost in representation space: DINO-WM

clean subspace that carries what matters, and that the distance restricted
to that subspace plans better.
Beyond the plain distance.
A recent line of work on latent MPC shares one premise: the latent
distance alone is a poor proxy for the reachability the planner needs.

linear function under normality constraints, and compute the cost on the
subspace it determines.
