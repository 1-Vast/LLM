## Concrete mathematical distinction, without another toyperformance claim

For a momentum matrixM, the exactpolaroperator P(M)=U*V^T and a fixedkNewtonSchulz recurrence are not generally the same finiteprogram. Even the scalardiagonal entryx0=1/4 under x_{t+1}=1.5*x_t−.5*x_t^3 remains strictlybetween0and1 at each finiteiteration, while its idealpolarsign is1. Hence an idealargminidentity says neither finiteiterateequality nor the cost of achieving a specifiederror. This is a direct symbolicobservation, not a newlyexecutedexperiment, newsolver or evidence of biologicalgain. Differentapproximations can also be worse; no finitecostadvantage follows merely from inequality.

Consequently a faithful ordinarycounterpart may use that SAME representation andobjective but must have an actual costed algorithm at matchedprecision. A hypothetical exactHessian, convergednonconvexsolve, fullunrollgradient or globaldiscreteoracle cannot be treated as free because it expresses the proposal. Conversely when candidate andcontrol execute identicalcheapsteps, as in literalCCIprojection or assaygradientrecombination, there is no remaining algorithmicgap to claim. Identicalpredictions also do not alone rule out a legitimate memory/communication/speedcontribution—but such a contribution must be measured or bounded at the actual end-to-endcostboundary.
