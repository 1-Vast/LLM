Conditional Flow Matching for Financial Price Forecasts Giancarlo Vercellino † † thanks: Contact: giancarlo.vercellino@gmail.com . October 21, 2026 Abstract Learning a forecasting model’s errors offers a modular way to improve its
predictive distribution while retaining its underlying dynamics. We study
this approach with Flowz, a conditional flow model of direct multi-step
price-level residuals fitted using random features and ridge regression.
Each EWMA, ARIMA, ARFIMA, and structural baseline is compared with a Gaussian
residual location–scale control and a conditional Flowz extension. The
experiment uses 24 US stocks selected across four liquidity–volatility
strata, 104 common forecast origins, horizons of 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, and 160
trading observations, and three fit seeds. Marginal CRPS is the primary score.
At farther horizons, Flowz reduces mean CRPS by 30.7% against EWMA and 24.0% against its Gaussian residual control.
The EWMA improvement holds at each fit seed and exceeds the Gaussian control for all 24 stocks in the far band. The ARIMA, ARFIMA, and structural extensions worsen both comparators. Conditional residual learning is therefore useful for the drifting EWMA specification in this experiment, with gains concentrated at longer horizons. Keywords: probabilistic forecasting; forecast errors;
conditional flow matching; residual learning; financial time series. 1 Introduction Financial forecasts must describe both future price levels and the uncertainty
surrounding them. A forecast can have a useful central estimate but poorly
represent the range of subsequent outcomes. Conversely, wide intervals can
cover observations while providing little precision. Proper scoring rules
evaluate the predictive distribution as a whole [ 6 ] . The question of how to improve these distributions is timely. Financial
stability analysis highlights the connection between uncertainty, market
functioning, and abrupt repricing [ 13 ] . At the same time, models such
as Chronos illustrate rapid advances in probabilistic time-series forecasting [ 12 ] . A complementary statistical route is to retain an existing
forecasting model and learn the structure of its errors. This route is
attractive when the baseline already captures useful dynamics and can be
updated economically as new observations arrive. Flowz implements this approach by learning joint vectors of future price-level
errors conditional on a local market state. A baseline supplies the forecast
mean and marginal scales. Historical errors are centered and standardized,
and a conditional flow maps Gaussian inputs into residual scenarios. Adding
those scenarios to the baseline produces a predictive distribution for future
levels. The statistical value of this construction depends on the information
remaining in the baseline’s errors. We ask three research questions: 1. Incremental value: Does conditional residual learning improve
the predictive distribution of the same underlying baseline? 2. Value beyond location and scale: Does Flowz improve a Gaussian
control with the same training-only residual centering and scaling? 3. Conditions of usefulness: How do these effects vary across
forecast horizons, baseline engines, liquidity, and volatility? The working hypothesis is that conditional residual learning improves CRPS,
particularly at longer horizons where baseline errors accumulate. We examine
this hypothesis through matched comparisons across four baseline engines and
a recent stock panel. The experiment is exploratory: all engines and all three
fit seeds enter the reported results. 2 Related methods ARIMA describes linear short-memory dynamics after integer differencing [ 2 ] ; ARFIMA extends this representation through fractional differencing [ 3 ] . EWMA and structural state-space models provide alternative
local descriptions. They form four starting points for evaluating the
incremental effect of conditional residual learning. Flow matching estimates velocity fields by regression along probability paths [ 4 ] . Flowz combines this construction with fixed random hidden
features and ridge regression, following the extreme learning machine approach [ 5 ] . FlowTime uses autoregressive flow matching for forecasting [ 7 ] ; FreqFlow also uses residual learning, with a frequency-domain
formulation [ 8 ] . Flowz instead models a complete univariate vector
of future level errors directly, conditioned on local innovations, drift,
volatility, and recent increments. The matched design connects this generative construction to a familiar
statistical question: how much predictive information remains after a baseline
has been fitted? A Gaussian location–scale control separates the effect of
unconditional residual centering and scaling from the additional conditional
distribution learned by the flow. 3 Conditional residual forecasting with Flowz 3.1 Target and local state Let P t > 0 P_{t}>0 be the split-adjusted closing price and y t = log ⁡ P t y_{t}=\log P_{t} .

The agreement in sign across all selected seeds makes the main engine-level pattern stable within this fitting experiment. 6 Discussion The results identify a useful setting for conditional residual learning:
the drifting EWMA baseline leaves errors that Flowz can improve beyond
unconditional centering and scaling. With λ μ = 0.90 \lambda_{\mu}=0.90 , the baseline
projects a short-memory drift estimate linearly over as many as 160
observations. One plausible explanation is that the conditional residual model
partly corrects this extrapolation. A conditional Gaussian or ridge residual
regression using the same state would help distinguish that mechanism from
the additional flexibility of the flow distribution. The parametric engines yield a different result. Their baseline forecasts
have lower absolute far-band CRPS than the EWMA augmentation, and both the
Gaussian correction and conditional Flowz worsen them. This pattern makes
the choice of baseline central to the method’s value. Examining which error
features persist across periods is a practical direction for selecting when
to apply a residual model. The panel is selected from currently surviving stocks, and the relative
liquidity–volatility strata also differ in sector and other characteristics.
Stale closes can affect both apparent volatility and predictability. Overlapping
targets and shared market shocks reduce the effective information in the
104 origins. The study uses one configuration, three fit seeds, and a frozen
historical residual model. Rolling refits, independent periods and markets,
and conditional residual controls are the next empirical extensions. 7 Conclusion Flowz learns a conditional distribution of direct multi-step baseline errors using random features and a fitted velocity field. Across three fits, it reduces far-band EWMA CRPS by 30.7% against the baseline and 24.0% against a Gaussian residual control with identical centering and scaling. The gain occurs at every selected seed and extends across all 24 stocks against that control. For ARIMA, ARFIMA, and structural baselines, the residual extension increases loss. The applied conclusion is that conditional residual learning can materially improve long-horizon forecasts when the baseline leaves predictable error structure; the implemented drifting EWMA provides the positive case in this panel. Two directions follow from these results. The first is to identify other baselines whose residual structure can be improved by Flowz, using the EWMA result as a starting point. The second is to adapt the conditional flow to the ARIMA, ARFIMA, and structural baselines that did not improve in this experiment. Baseline-specific choices of conditioning features, model capacity, regularization, and training windows are natural candidates for this investigation, with tuning evaluated on chronological validation data and performance assessed on a subsequent held-out period.
