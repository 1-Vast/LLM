## Decision

Accept the response's central admission: it supplies no defensible novel recursive-improvement core. Reject its claimed one-run finite example, the description of its baseline as fully optimized contextual, and its necessity claims about exact state sufficiency. Those statements are not validated by its admission of conventionality. The response is a proposal, not a performed experiment.

## Comparator and transfer failure

The first stated Q[o]=(successes+1)/(attempts+2) has no context input. It is an independent-arm Beta–Bernoulli bandit, not a contextual algorithm portfolio. The later toy switches to per-context tables without specifying that change. Even a per-context independent table is a deliberately restricted comparator for a proposed shared transition model; an optimized ordinary contextual model can share the same structural prior/features, fit the same transition kernel, or directly transfer expected utility. A Beta posterior also assumes a suitable binary likelihood; generic interventions, replicate-well actions and history-dependent outcomes do not automatically satisfy it.

The finite transfer claim does not follow from the observed information. At source state0 the two verified observations are A:0→1 and B:0→0. Both following deterministic worlds fit them exactly:

| World | A(0) | B(0) | A(1) | B(1) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Constant intervention maps |1|0|1|0|
| Context-dependent maps |1|0|0|1|

With reward1 for next state1, the preferred intervention in context1 reverses. A tabular T_o(s'|s,a) learned at s0 does not identify its s1 row. If the constant-map law is GIVEN as side information, the ordinary comparator must receive it and can pool or directly apply it too. If it is merely true but unknown, the proposed model does not earn certainty from two source transitions. The assertion that the ordinary comparator MUST try B at s1 is also false: an allowed policy might transfer the same side information or simply select A without that experiment. No universal one-run saving or matched-cost comparison is established. This is an explicit four-entry alternative-world argument, not a requirement that all biological mechanisms be uniquely identified before useful decisions.

## Intervention policy is not an improvement policy

The listed operators increase dose, switch combination, add an agent or replicate a well. Their T_o describes changes to cellular state under interventions, and the update changes that world model plus its action-value estimates. No operator that changes a research program or the rule for proposing/selecting future program improvements is defined. An inherited contextual proposal policy COULD meet operational Level3 under the requested common-fresh-start experiment, without self-editing code, but this requires defining the improvement action and measuring subsequent improvements. The present prescription establishes neither that operator nor the comparison. Model-based intervention selection alone remains model adaptation/planning.

The softmax pi(a|s) uses r_o and V_T_o(s,a) without a defined mapping between o and a. Its rollback refers to a lower bound on r_o+lambda V under the comparator posterior, although the comparator has only independent Bernoulli arm posteriors and no stated joint transition/value uncertainty model. Units, uncertainty propagation and the claimed confidence comparison are unspecified. These are gaps in the executable specification, not evidence that all related policies fail.

## Exact sufficiency is neither tested nor necessary

Checking whether ONE extra coordinate changes predictions does not establish Markov sufficiency, which concerns all relevant omitted history under the interventions of interest. Prediction invariance can reflect low power or an uninformative chosen coordinate. A detected change can refute that restricted predictive condition, but does not prove the candidate has no useful finite-budget value.

Similarly, early-to-later calibration tests one transport property; an imperfect or partially observed model can still improve functional decisions. Belief/history models, robust policies and ordinary contextual predictors can work without an exactly sufficient stationary two-number state. Thus 'if summary is not sufficient/stationary, autonomous choice adds nothing' is unsupported. The necessary empirical criterion is matched-budget decision value and reliability under a declared task scope, with state sufficiency as a model diagnostic/assumption where used. The original public-data qualification gaps remain real and cannot be waived by this correction.
