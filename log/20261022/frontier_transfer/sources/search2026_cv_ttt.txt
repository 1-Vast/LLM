Rethinking Video Autoencoders for Interactive World Models
2026-10-21T17:58:11Z
Video world models predict future frames from past frames and actions. They serve as simulators in which agents train, plan or are evaluated, and that humans can control interactively. They generate in the latent space of a video autoencoder, whose compression sets their cost per frame and how long each action waits to take effect. Yet world models often reuse the autoencoders of text-to-video generators, built for offline generation with no agent waiting and, without their training data and code, not retrainable at another compression. We therefore ask how, where and how much a world model should compress. We build CADRE, an open family of causal diffusion representation autoencoders spanning multiple spatio-temporal compressions. Its members differ only in a small bottleneck on frozen features and share a diffusion decoder self-distilled to one or a few steps. We train the same world model on each autoencoder in two domains, manipulation and navigation, with two environments each. CADRE forms most of the latency-quality frontier, especially with a moving camera. Our study also leads to four findings. At strong compression, a diffusion decoder is sharper than perceptual regression. In robot manipulation, temporal compression is more effective in the autoencoder than in the world model. Manipulation and navigation call for opposite compressions: temporal compression costs little when the camera is still, and spatial compression when it moves. Surprisingly, with a still camera, coarser spatial tokens hurt motion far more than image quality. Code, models and a live demo are at https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-10-21-cadre

PhysiCalWorld: In-Context Physics Calibration for World Models
2026-10-21T17:57:59Z
Existing world models typically perceive a scene through current visual frame or a narrow history window. However, these observations may not reveal the physics properties that determine transition dynamics, such as an object's mass distribution, joint damping, or surface friction. Without calibrating these physics factors for world models, predictions tend to regress toward an empirical prior and induce severe drift in novel test scenes. To this end, we propose PhysiCalWorld, which calibrates action-conditioned world models to the instance-specific dynamics via in-context prompting, without fine-tuning or analytical physics modeling. Our method encodes an exploratory interaction clip collected within the test scene into a compact physics descriptor token, which grounds subsequent predictions in the scene's actual physical behavior. PhysiCalWorld is trained end-to-end with the latent prediction loss alone, without physics labels. At inference time, the descriptor token is extracted once and can be reused across rollouts with negligible compute overhead. We evaluate PhysiCalWorld across four environments covering diverse physics variations and show that it substantially improves rollout prediction accuracy and visual planning success rate. The learned descriptors also form a physically structured latent space and generalize beyond the physics ranges seen during training.

ALPS: Visual State Encoders for World Models
2026-10-21T17:57:11Z
World models turn visual representations into predictions and actions. We argue that their visual foundation should combine physical-state information, native language alignment, and pixel reconstruction, so that a single representation can support prediction, instruction, and inspection. However, existing encoders typically specialize in only one or two of these capabilities; we therefore introduce ALPS, a pretrained visual state encoder that unifies all three in a single set of patch tokens. Using only still images and text, we pretrain a ViT-B/16 from scratch with language, reconstruction, and spatial correspondence objectives, and then refine it via cross-view self-distillation. In a unified evaluation across fifteen public encoders, ALPS achieves the lowest aggregate LIBERO state-probe error among ViT-B models (matching the 3.5x larger V-JEPA 2-L) while maintaining strong language alignment and accurate pixel reconstruction. Downstream, frozen ALPS tokens reach the highest mean policy success on LIBERO (85.5%) when paired with our native text tower, match or exceed leading encoders on world-model planning across PushT, Wall, and Granular, and allow predicted future tokens to be decoded directly into pixels for visual inspection. We hope this work helps establish visual pretraining as a shared foundation for understanding, prediction, and action in world models.

Looking Ahead: Modeling Future Evolution in Autoregressive Video Generation
2026-10-21T16:28:46Z
Autoregressive video diffusion models generate videos chunk by chunk, yet producing plausible continuations does not guarantee that they capture how scenes evolve. Under teacher forcing, strong temporal redundancy may allow local visual continuity to serve as a shortcut for current-chunk denoising, reducing the pressure to model visual evolution. We introduce Future Chunk Prediction (FCP), an auxiliary objective jointly optimized with teacher-forced denoising that predicts temporally separated chunks without access to the intervening visual context. Drawing supervision directly from training videos, FCP requires no additional pretrained visual encoder and leaves autoregressive inference unchanged. Our method achieves superior physical plausibility over standard teacher-forced autoregressive training with a lower overall training compute budget, highlighting the effectiveness and efficiency of future-chunk supervision for modeling visual evolution. Intriguingly, our visualizations suggest that FCP fosters a more structured representation space, which may be valuable for building autoregressive world models.

APED-CXR: Adaptive Privileged Evidence Distillation for Chest X-Ray Grounding with Image-Only Inference
2026-10-21T13:39:07Z
Compact chest X-ray (CXR) models that localize findings suit deployment, but box annotations are limited and report-aware multimodal models are expensive at inference. We introduce APED-CXR, which distills report-informed spatial evidence from a frozen multimodal teacher into a student with 11.1M parameters that predicts diseases and boxes from shared evidence maps and requires the image alone at test time. During training, the teacher uses the radiograph, report, and queried class to evaluate regions proposed from the student's current evidence maps, and its support scores serve as training targets for the student's pooled evidence scores within these regions. Trained on PadChest and evaluated on held-out PadChest-GR, refreshing the student's proposals for teacher evaluation improves box-level localization F1 from 0.302 with fixed warm-up proposals to 0.338 (IoU >= 0.5; gain 0.036, 95% CI [0.022, 0.049]), while maintaining diagnostic AUC. This gain depends on the teacher checking current proposals: with equally many random boxes, a fixed dense box set, teacher-generated boxes, or report rules instead of the teacher, F1 stays near the fixed-proposal level, and proposals two refreshes old recover only part of the gain. On NIH ChestX-ray14, a secondary test set, box recall also rises from 0.423 to 0.447.

FogTTA: Online Test-Time Adaptation for Robust Transformer-based Object Detection in Foggy Weather
2026-10-21T11:57:36Z
Object detection models for autonomous driving commonly experience substantial performance drops when deployed under adverse weather due to the domain shift between training data and real-world operating conditions. This degradation is especially evident when models trained on clear-weather images encounter foggy environments with reduced visibility and contrast. To address this challenge, we introduce FogTTA, an online test-time adaptation framework designed to improve the robustness of Transformer-based object detectors in fog. Using RF-DETR as the underlying object detector, FogTTA enables real-time adaptation to the streaming target domain without requiring source data or retraining. The framework follows a teacher-student design, where the deployed model serves as the teacher and generates pseudo labels from weakly augmented target inputs. These predictions are subsequently refined through non-maximum suppression and confidence filtering. The student model then learns from strongly augmented target sample using the Varifocal loss to mitigate pseudo-label noise. The teacher is updated via exponential moving averaging to ensure stable and continuous adaptation. Experiments show that FogTTA outperforms prior baselines, delivering improved detection accuracy and stability while maintaining real-time performance.

LiveAct: Towards Real-Time Multi-Agent World Models with Heterogeneous Action Interfaces
2026-10-21T08:34:03Z
Interactive video world models predict how an environment evolves under user actions, but most existing systems assume one controlled agent and one task-specific action interface. We study multi-agent video world modeling, where one model generates a joint visual future for multiple controlled agents while interpreting heterogeneous controls ranging from binary game buttons to continuous robot commands. This task presents two complementary challenges. First, every agent-indexed action stream must control its intended visual entity independently. Second, the model must adapt the meaning of each control variable across different tasks, where the same tensor dimension may denote an attack, steering command, or robot command. We introduce LiveAct, centered on a Heterogeneous Action Interface (HAI) whose two components mirror this decomposition. To support independent control, Agent-Aware Spatial Modulation uses language-driven spatial gates and agent-specific attention to bind each action stream to its intended visual agent. To accommodate heterogeneous controls, Schema-Guided Action Adaptation describes each control variable in language and modulates its semantic token with the synchronized numerical value. We further transfer the complete interface to a blockwise causal generator through teacher forcing and rollout-aware distribution matching, enabling efficient streaming generation while retaining agent-specific control and action semantics. We instantiate the formulation on six multi-agent sources spanning games and multi-robot manipulation, and evaluate visual quality, action following, agent attribution, and latency. Across games and robot manipulation, LiveAct reaches 0.898 and 0.942 agent-attribution accuracy without agent locations or identity crops, and the causal generator attains 17.70 FPS at $512\times 448$.

How Representational Priors Shape Generalization in World Action Models
2026-10-21T03:59:35Z
World action models (WAMs) are shown to substantially diverge under generalization performance based on their generative backbones. We investigate this gap through the representational geometry that WAMs inherit from their backbones and how policy training reorganizes it. Our central hypothesis is that a useful representational prior would structure geometry that separates nuisance information from the in-distribution manifold in advance of training as a policy. We quantify this with nuisance orthogonality; orthogonality of the feature under visual variation outside the empirical in-distribution manifold. Across various image and video backbones, we show that a higher orthogonality from the representational prior is associated with stronger robustness to visual variations after WAM training, enabling policy generalization measurable prospectively for world models. In addition, analyzing the resulting robust WAMs reveals nuisance suppression; relative suppression of feature displacement caused by such variations and robust action generation of the policy. Together, these findings provide a geometric perspective on how the representational prior of the world model shapes the generalization performance of WAMs.
