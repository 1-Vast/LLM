## Mechanism inventory

| Component | What the paper reports | Transferable operation (inference) | Boundary / missing check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PACE phenotype representation | 54 candidate gene sets were assessed across cohorts; ten cell-type-resolved, directionally scored modules form the immune-aging panel. The paper reports stronger held-out age benchmarking than five clocks in the 434-donor cohort. | Define an explicit, directional phenotype panel and hold out a cohort/background before tuning drug ranking. Record each module separately so an aggregate score can be challenged. | Its immune-aging genes, directions, and age-clock superiority are phenotype-specific. KARE is restricted; age association is not drug efficacy or functional rejuvenation. Exact module list and scoring pipeline need supplement/code. |
| CellQ representation and prediction | RVQ compresses each transcriptome to eight codes aligned to an LLM vocabulary; the paper says encoder training used over 100 million cells, then few-shot autoregressive prediction of drug-induced gene/module shifts. Tahoe-100M tests hold out cell lines or drugs; authors report best macro-F1 among named comparators. | A compact state representation and module-level prediction target may reduce prompt cost. Benchmark with whole-drug and whole-background splits and a direct non-LLM predictor under the same exposure. | Reusing CellQ itself needs verified weights/code and matched input schema. Eight tokens alone do not imply a cheap trainable model. Macro-F1 on labeled modules does not establish viable-dose hit selection or physical cell trajectories. Target's exact training leakage controls and numeric results were not inspected. |
| Inner screen | The reported fixed inner flow is candidate retrieval, CellQ response prediction, then PACE scoring. First-round approximately 5,000 candidates were narrowed to 110 by LLM-assisted categorization; CellQ reranking uses drug target/pathway, PBMC context, module direction, and labeled reference compounds. | Preserve a fixed candidate pool, deterministic score inputs, and provenance for every reference label. This is an auditable screen design. | Candidate-selection logs and costs are unavailable here; 110 is a selected panel, not a randomized prospective comparison against strongest direct or active-learning baselines. |
| Analyzer–Planner–Auditor | The outer roles read a round-N wet-lab response matrix, detect a mismatch, propose and approve a revised rule. The observed asymmetry was aged-down modules being easier to restore than aged-up modules. The initial `(D+U)/2` became `min(D,U)`, with viability and immune-function filters; the RVQ–LLM was not retrained for this update. | Treat the *ranking rule* as a falsifiable object: freeze candidate scores and external assay endpoint, state a revision before independent assessment, and retain the rejected/accepted alternatives and their evidence. | Role names and MIN are not by themselves a new learning algorithm. Better score correlation on 13 compounds does not imply a new hit, new donor transfer, a model update, or repeated autonomous improvement. Full logs and exact acceptance criteria remain unknown. |

## What transfers to a low-cost drug-screening virtual cell

| Candidate component | Minimal qualified use | What cannot be transferred as evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Directional phenotype panel | Predefine a few cell/context-specific axes with measured functional endpoints; retain axis-level labels and viability filters. | PACE's ten immune-aging axes or 434-donor age validation as generic cancer-drug endpoints. |
| Compact response model | Compare a compact representation against direct baselines using available public perturbation data and leave-drug/leave-background splits. | CellQ's published benchmark result as proof of affordable training, real assay hit gains, or causal cell dynamics. |
| Revision ledger | Version the scoring rule, triggering response matrix, acceptance criteria, and independent endpoint before each subsequent decision. | Treating an LLM's explanation or Auditor approval as experimental validation. |
| Budgeted selection | Compare equal experimental budgets using external function, with random, greedy, uncertainty, direct response prediction, and standard active-learning/VoI baselines. | Treating one-shot reranking, score correlation, or queried-hit enrichment as sequential learning benefit. |

**Practical access gate.** The target's 110-drug matrix is not presently downloadable through its cited official entry, and the held-out KARE cohort is restricted. Its reported cell-state/functional conclusions are therefore valuable as a design reference, but this paper does not currently supply a verified open, condition-level RNA–function table suitable for reproducing a low-cost closed-loop assay. A future dataset must expose drug, dose, time, cell/donor/batch, vehicle, independent replicate, RNA, viability, and functional values with an allowed split before any effect or cost claim is tested. This is an **inference about current reproducibility**, not a claim that the authors lack those data.
