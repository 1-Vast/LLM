## Alternative 1: joint susceptibility within a population

**Question/object.** Do two drugs affect the same cellular subpopulation or complementary subpopulations? The object is cross-drug alignment of cell fates, not another marginal rate or viability curve. A screening group choosing a two-drug follow-up can care about this even when each drug's full marginal response is known exactly.

**Solving principle.** Construct observationally equivalent monotherapy models by permuting the latent sensitivity labels for drug B while keeping their initial proportions and per-drug rates unchanged. A sequential A-then-B exposure to the *same surviving population* links those labels. A matched A-then-A arm controls total exposure time. Static heterogeneous sensitivities can produce a large A/B versus A/A difference even when A-then-B and B-then-A agree; order asymmetry is not required.

**Constructive route.** With n/2 sensitive and n/2 resistant cells, drug A kills one type at rate k. Drug B either kills that same type or the other type. All single-drug live-count path distributions agree between those two worlds, not just endpoint means. After equal pulses of length tau, the two A-then-B means are n(1+exp(-2k tau))/2 and n exp(-k tau). Their difference is n(1-exp(-k tau))²/2. This can reverse a population-depletion decision despite exact knowledge of every monotherapy curve.

**Risk/prior/resource.** Cross-resistance, collateral sensitivity, independent drug action, combination response and switched-system identification are serious prior families. No absence claim is supported. A direct continuous pair/schedule count assay at the same price is a mandatory and likely sufficient rival. HTS001 contains no such intervention; more monotherapy timestamps or repeats cannot create it. The promising deliverable may be a rigorous limit plus a minimal witness rather than an algorithm or a validated co-learning contribution.

## Alternative 2: treatment memory and order sensitivity

**Question/object.** Does a first drug create a future-response state that cannot be summarized by current total live count? This concerns treatment-induced functional memory rather than the static cross-drug coupling in alternative 1.

**Solving principle.** Compare two equal-duration histories that give the same current total count and then apply the same probe. In a simple two-state model, A converts S to R without changing total count and B kills S. A followed by B differs from B followed by A; the operator commutator can be seen through the common readout. If conversion rate is alpha and killing rate delta, both pulses length tau, the survival difference is (1-exp(-alpha tau))(1-exp(-delta tau)). This is a mathematical example, not evidence of a molecular state or clinical resistance.

**Prediction and confound.** The same probe response depends on history after matching count, dose and duration. Density, retained drug, media changes and delayed toxicity can also cause this, so order dependence alone does not identify induction versus selection. A functional predictive state may still be useful if those conditions are held fixed.

**Risk/prior/resource.** Predictive-state representations, controlled hidden-state models, scheduling/adaptive therapy and Wu's plasticity model are strong priors. The essential operation is standard state excitation and probing. The current resource has no switching/washout protocol or equal-count matched histories. No distinct transferable law or acquisition advantage has yet been derived, so this alternative is not developed into a candidate.

## Alternative 3: which kinetic decision survives the observation process

**Question/object.** Can a death/turnover ranking be distinguished from segmentation or imaging variability using the same live-count channel? The object is a set of decision-compatible process/observation decompositions, not a single fitted birth/death vector.

**Solving principle.** Under a homogeneous branching model and independent zero-mean measurement errors, residuals R_i = Y_(i+1) - a_i Y_i have adjacent covariance -a_(i+1) v_(i+1), with zero nonadjacent covariance. This enables a necessary model check and, under enough replicates and additional restrictions, separation of observation variance from innovation variance. It is ordinary state-space/measurement-error algebra; Wu already models the corresponding whole-trajectory covariance.

**Prediction and limitation.** Nonadjacent residual correlation falsifies that simple combined model, but does not identify a unique biological or imaging cause. Unknown correlated observation errors can mimic biological covariance. Treating technical wells or within-trajectory intervals as independent cultures would not fix this. Across-cell borrowing would itself require a tested shared observation law.

**Risk/prior/resource.** Root independently derived this relation in `../root_derivation.md`. It is at most a transparent assumption diagnostic on the 28 controls per cell background; it cannot validate drug-specific noise, drug death rates, or an agent advantage. HTS001's controls make a necessary check executable if the coordinator freezes it, but that convenience does not turn the diagnostic into the main contribution.
