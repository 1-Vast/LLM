# Public cellular target-engagement resources and overlap audit

**Search date:** 2026-10-30 (Asia/Shanghai)  
**Question:** Does a public resource provide cellular target engagement that can be joined to PRISM, DepMap, or GDSC at the same cell line, drug/structure, dose, exposure time, and replicate level, with emphasis on FLT3/kinases and 2025–2026 primary work?

## Scope and decision rule

I counted an engagement observation only when the source measures a compound–protein interaction in cells (for example, CETSA/TPP stabilization, live-cell probe competition, NanoBRET/FPCBA binding, or intracellular chemical-proteomics competition). Target/MOA labels, expression, phospho-response, and viability alone are not engagement. A **full match** requires all of: an unambiguous cell identifier, drug structure/identifier, concentration and unit, exposure time, replicate identity/count, and a machine-readable engagement value. A common cell line or common drug by itself is only a **potential overlap**.

No source below met the full-match rule together with an openly documented PRISM/DepMap/GDSC row. The report therefore does not claim an exact cross-dataset match.

## Official curated resource that is not direct engagement

**EUbOPEN/ChEMBL cellular affinity dataset (release 2025).** The official ChEMBL document [CHEMBL5723379 / DOI 10.6019/CHEMBL5723379](https://doi.org/10.6019/CHEMBL5723379), API record [document JSON](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/document/CHEMBL5723379.json) and [activity JSON](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/activity.json?document_chembl_id=CHEMBL5723379&limit=1000) contain 181 literature-derived “Affinity On-target Cellular” activities (cell descriptions, ChEMBL molecule/target IDs, IC50/pChEMBL values in nM, and source DOI links). The records are cellular functional inhibition/biomarker assays (for example MCF7/HEK293/IMR-32), generally without standardized exposure time, replicate identity, or cell-line registry IDs. They do not establish direct compound–protein engagement and therefore are not admitted to the engagement table; MOA annotations and expression are likewise excluded.

## Access failures and negative findings

- The 2025 diaPASEF paper is indexed in PMC but marked non-open-access; the Europe PMC full-text/supplement endpoint returned an access error, so only its abstract was used.
- The 2022 *Target Engagement Atlas* (DOI [10.1016/j.mcpro.2022.100327](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mcpro.2022.100327)) is marked open access by Crossref/Elsevier, but the accessible Elsevier API returned metadata without article body or dataset fields. It was not upgraded beyond access-failure status.
- Europe PMC returned temporary 503 responses for broad 2025–2026 compound queries, and Crossref returned 429 for some repeated searches. Independent Europe PMC title/DOI searches, open PMC XML, Crossref single-work metadata, PRIDE records, and the official ChEMBL API were used instead. No proxy, login bypass, or restricted download was attempted.
- No 2025–2026 FLT3 NanoBRET/TPP resource with a public, standardized PRISM/DepMap/GDSC cell × drug × dose × time × replicate table was found. Claims such as “directly engages FLT3” without a cellular engagement measurement were not counted.

## Field and mapping gaps that block an exact join

1. Engagement sources use protein/probe IDs (UniProt/kinase symbols, LFQ/TMT intensity, pIC50/Ki, ΔTm or soluble fraction), whereas PRISM uses compound and DepMap model registries and viability readouts; GDSC similarly uses its own drug/model identifiers.
2. Cell names such as MV4-11, Jurkat E6.1, HEK293T and HCT116 are not sufficient without a registry crosswalk and passage/engineering status (for example HC2 is OATP1B3-engineered).
3. Dose units and schedules differ (nM or µM; 30–60 min engagement, 1–2 h CETSA/probe incubation versus 72 h or five-day viability). A common nominal concentration is not an exposure match.
4. Replicates are often biological/technical summaries without shared preparation or plate IDs; raw PRIDE files require reprocessing to recover sample-level rows.
5. Most sources lack an orthogonal function endpoint measured on the same preparation. The LT-171-861 paper has 1 h CETSA and 72 h MTT in MV4-11/MOLM13, but the compound is not a verified PRISM/GDSC registry item and tables are not an analysis-ready crosswalk.

## Download budget and stop condition

The search budget was limited to public metadata, abstracts, open article XML and small API responses (target **≤50 MB** total); no PRIDE raw files, large supplementary XLSX files, or restricted full text were downloaded. Stop searching when either (a) a source supplies all six full-match fields plus a verified PRISM/DepMap/GDSC identifier, or (b) three independent search routes (Europe PMC/PMC, Crossref/DOI, and official PRIDE/ChEMBL records) produce no new full-match source. Condition (b) was reached on 2026-10-30. A future targeted metadata check should verify MV4-11–midostaurin and Jurkat–dasatinib registry/dose overlap before any data download; it must not be treated as an exact match until the row-level contract is satisfied.

## Data-gate consequence

This search does **not** change the existing `data_unavailable` decision. CellEKT and PXD047949 provide credible, public cellular-engagement candidates and useful fields for designing a future matched panel, but neither supplies a verified same-row PRISM/DepMap/GDSC join with structure, cell barcode, concentration, exposure time, replicate identity, engagement value, and orthogonal function. The coordinator should keep the current gate closed and avoid calling a retrospective overlap a target-mediated CDI result.

### Local identity check after the search

The coordinator checked the already downloaded PRISM metadata without fetching
new response or PRIDE files. The 24Q2 extended compound list names midostaurin,
dasatinib and gilteritinib, but their exact Broad IDs have zero rows in the
24Q2 treatment metadata snapshot. The 24Q2 cell metadata contains Jurkat and
MOLM13 rows but no MV4-11 or HEK293T rows. In the local 19Q4 treatment table,
midostaurin has 24 HTS002 rows, dasatinib has 24 HTS002 and 24 MTS010 rows,
and gilteritinib has 24 MTS006 rows; all 96 names are present in the response
header. The 489-row local secondary response index does not contain the
DepMap IDs for MV4-11, MOLM13 or Jurkat. These are row-presence observations
about the inspected snapshots, not global absence claims and not matched
engagement evidence. The deterministic counts are retained in
`raw/resource_metadata/engagement_overlap_identity_check.json`.
