## Central question and proposed analytical object

**One-sentence question:** Can an observed change in response after one cancer-treatment history revise a rule that correctly changes the choice of a later drug in an unmeasured cancer background or treatment history, using ordinary functional assays alone?

The smallest object is a conditional relation among *measured treatment histories and later functional responses*: for background x, history h, and challenge d, the function `F_x(h,d)` means the prespecified functional endpoint under the actual exposure/handling protocol. It is not a molecular cell state, proof of induction rather than selection, or an unconstrained counterfactual generated by a model. A reusable rule would state which history-to-challenge response relation survives a specified change of x, h, or d and which ordinary observation can invalidate it.

An Agent could choose a legal, informative functional comparison; a conventional virtual-cell response model could update the shared relation and its domain of validity. That loop is technically straightforward and has close active-design/model-revision precedents. To be a substantive result, the update must correctly change a previously unmeasured treatment decision because a verified biological relation transfers. A new module, changed wording, smaller posterior variance, or a better fitted source curve is insufficient.

At decision time the accessible inputs would be background identity, the actual previous drug(s), physical concentrations and durations, matched functional measurements and their preparation/technical-repeat identities. Molecular measurements may supply source explanation but are not assumed at deployment. Published source identities cannot substitute for missing dose and endpoint contracts. Historical adaptation labels are not treatment-independent features.

## What the actual human-oncology sources already establish

### P3: Transition between cell states of sensitivity reveals molecular vulnerability of drug-tolerant cells

Original JATS read: Sec3, Sec7, Sec12, Sec14, Sec15, Sec20; abstract and section inventory. Local original: `../2026-10-29-common-function-mechanism-renewal/evidence/p3_transition.xml`.

- Sec3 compares repeated, resting, and sustained TRAIL/TBQ/TCQ regimens in HeLa-RIPK3 and HT-29. Recovery of sensitivity during drug holiday does not automatically improve net population suppression because cells grow during the holiday. This is already the authors' explicit practical distinction.
- Sec7 finds that apoptotic treatment followed by necroptotic treatment can improve control, whereas the converse is less effective; TAK1 inhibition is also investigated for preventing or reversing resistance. Thus treatment-order specificity and pharmacological modification of later response are existing source contributions.
- Sec12 defines repeated treatment as fresh media/treatment after washing, resting as a drug-free interval, and sustained as the original treatment left in place. These are distinct exposure protocols; their nominal drug labels do not establish equal effective exposure.
- Sec15's primary density endpoint is occupied dish area. It is not an absolute viable-cell or entry-cell-descendant count. The raw-density normalization and starting-burden issue cannot be bypassed by calling it survival.
- Sec20 already calibrates compartment-model topologies with penalized least squares and compares their trajectories to data. Its existence is a strong mechanistic-dynamics rival, not a claim that every possible transfer relation has been solved.

P3 therefore directly overlaps initial alternatives about recovery windows and reversible treatment opportunities. A proposed new rule must add a real transferable relation beyond the studied death-modality/TAK1 examples.

### P4: Exploiting collateral sensitivity in the evolution of resistance to tyrosine kinase inhibitors in soft tissue sarcomas

Original JATS read: Sec6, Sec7, Sec11, Sec12, Sec16, Sec17, Sec18; abstract and section inventory. Local original: `../2026-10-29-common-function-mechanism-renewal/evidence/p4_collateral_sarcoma.xml`.

- Sec6 reports an A204 panel of four mTKI pretreatments and 58 subsequent inhibitors after 13 days, plus screens of terminal resistant sublines. The collateral-response patterns differ by pretreatment although terminal resistance shares important features. This directly warns against identifying treatment opportunity from a generic resistant label or drug class alone.
- Sec7 validates sitravatinib-to-infigratinib switching and reports a similar phenotype in G402. It also studies A204 pretreatment durations of 3, 6, 13, 20 and 27 days. Those timing and cross-background examples belong to the authors; rediscovering them is not new validation.
- A second FGFR inhibitor, pemigatinib, shows a related trend that was not statistically significant. The source does not establish universal interchangeability within the target class. Nonsignificance does not prove absence of an effect either.
- The 14-cell-line baseline panel does not become a 14-background sequential panel. The repeated time-course is not a four-primer by 58-challenge by five-time by many-background factorial resource. Combining those distinct experiments into that tensor would fabricate support.
- Sec16 says the 13-day temporal pretreatment uses 1 micromolar mTKI, whereas Fig4B in Sec6 labels it 0.5 micromolar. The downstream inhibitor panel is separately specified as 0.5 micromolar for 72 h. The pretreatment discrepancy must remain unresolved until an authoritative source clarifies it.
- Sec11 terminal resistant sublines arise through adaptive dose escalation with drug/background-specific maintained concentrations; they are not simply later samples of the fixed-dose 13-day experiment.
- Sec12/16/18 challenge assays reseed cells and use CellTiter-Glo after 72 h. Sec17 schedule assays instead use fixed-plate Hoechst direct counts and stop counting at confluence. Consequently, a per-reseeded-cell sensitivity shift cannot be silently equated with total cancer-population reduction from the beginning of treatment.

P4 thus supplies an important **published reason to retain history-specific susceptibility**, but not a new shared rule for the present project. Its two experimentally followed backgrounds do not establish generic transfer; its source data have not been qualified here.

## Constructive enabling case and its failure case

The following is a reasoning example only. It was not simulated and is not claimed to describe either source.

Suppose historical functional data establish two reproducible classes of treatment history. In class U, later challenges B and C each leave functional burden 0.2; in class V, each leaves 0.8. The decision is whether burden is at most 0.3. On a new background, one measured B response can identify U versus V and correctly change the previously unmeasured C decision **if the B-to-C relation transports** and noise can distinguish the values. The observation need not reveal any molecular mechanism. An ordinary joint-response model or even a lookup table implements this useful capability.

The exact failure case keeps every acquired observation unchanged but makes new-background C equal to 0.8 when B equals 0.2. Without a justified invariant relation or a direct C observation, the learner cannot distinguish these cases. Better fitting of B, extra language reasoning, or a rule name does not supply the missing fact. P4's primer-specific patterns and unestablished same-class substitution make this a real kind of uncertainty, although this numerical table is only illustrative.

This enabling case is deliberately **not promoted as a new candidate**: absent a source-grounded relation it is the archived functional-index/conditional-response construction with treatment histories replacing monotherapy labels. For a single desired C decision, direct C measurement remains an equal-price allowed rival and can dominate acquiring a surrogate. Multiple pending decisions can create amortization, but paid training, all controls, and calibration must be included; no saving follows just from writing a shared table.

## Consequential decision and strongest ordinary rivals

The practical user would be an oncology screening team deciding whether to retain a history-to-drug sequence, choose a different following treatment, or discard a sequence whose per-survivor sensitization does not produce sufficient total population control. The result would be a research-screen decision under a specified functional endpoint, not a clinical recommendation.

The strongest simple comparator is to measure the pending target schedule directly, using the same functional endpoint and total purchased conditions. For a menu of decisions, use an ordinary history-by-drug interaction model with full refitting and the same acquisition policy; include drug-class pooling, nearest functional signatures, and the source's compartmental model where its assumptions match. The Agent should receive no extra history, source-derived target label, or free evaluation outcome.

The scientific gain would be a stable, actionable history-response relation that these methods can implement. If they exploit the same relation equally well, that removes an exclusive Agent/algorithm claim but does not erase a real biological law. Here, however, that law itself has not yet been identified beyond source-owned examples.
