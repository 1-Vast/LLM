## Alternative questions actually considered

1. **Reusable cycle:** Does a treatment sequence that succeeds once remain effective when applied to the resulting survivor population? This can change whether a screening team reuses a schedule, measures a later cycle, or abandons a one-cycle hit. P3's four-day observed alternation versus longer modeled extinction makes the capability consequential. Develop this candidate below.
2. **Recovery clock:** Does functional recovery transfer across cancer backgrounds after matching elapsed time or after matching drug-free net population growth? This would change timing of a repeated challenge using cheap density curves. However, net growth is not division count when birth and death coexist; the prior functional-observation episode already prevents assuming an exponential mean law. The inspected P3 schedules do not cross multiple recovery durations and independent growth rates sufficiently to separate these clocks. A rescaled-time curve with no independently tested biological relation would repeat the archived shared-functional-deformation problem. Do not infer a molecular dilution clock or demand a new experiment to rescue this lead.
3. **Return-map convergence:** Can repeated application of the same sequence make initially different treatment histories functionally interchangeable, even if early cycle gains differ? A contracting history-to-future-response map would support reuse after a transient. This is distinct from a constant gain, and an ordinary state-space/Floquet model could implement it. But the current source lacks several separately prepared initial histories followed by the same cycle and common later probes. One observed trajectory cannot demonstrate contraction between histories. P3's coupled sensitivity-state model already contains the relevant dynamic relaxation mechanism; the finite public table does not establish a new shared contraction law.

## Developed finite-cycle candidate

**Central question:** Can a first complete treatment cycle predict the observed response to its next repetition well enough to justify treating that cycle as a reusable action in a functional virtual-cell model?

This is about one measured repetition, not indefinite renewal, eradication, independence of past history, or induction rather than selection. It can be stated without molecular inputs. Let `Y_0`, `Y_1`, `Y_2` be the same positive functional burden measurement at the same phase of a fixed schedule, before treatment, after its first complete cycle, and after its second complete cycle. A *candidate constant-gain rule* is

`Y_2(predicted) = Y_1^2 / Y_0`, equivalently `Y_2/Y_1 = Y_1/Y_0`.

For P3's 24 h TRAIL then 24 h TBQ sequence, the endpoints would be 0, 48 and 96 h if the source entries genuinely correspond to them. With source-defined fractional density changes `p_j` across each of the four consecutive 24 h phases, the corresponding gain is `g_j = 1 + p_j/100`, and the same claim becomes `g_3*g_4 = g_1*g_2`. This transformation is valid only if each p_j uses the immediately preceding phase as its denominator under the same schedule; a bar labeled treated versus control, an average of unrelated preparations, or a difference in percentage points is not interchangeable.

An Agent could compare the predicted next-cycle outcome with a declared decision threshold and decide whether to acquire another cycle's functional observation. The virtual cell would update or reject the recurrence using the returned observation. Standard likelihood inference and ordinary sequential design are sufficient implementations. That loop is not itself the candidate scientific contribution.

### Constructive enabling and failure cases

Reasoning examples only, not simulations or biological results. In a restricted setting with stable measurement scale and reproducible preparation, observe `Y_0=1` and `Y_1=0.4`. A genuinely reusable finite cycle gives `Y_2=0.16`. If the screening threshold at the second-cycle endpoint is 0.2, the first-cycle recurrence correctly retains the schedule.

An alternative population can produce exactly the same first-cycle observation, then give `Y_2=0.36`: the first reduction mainly removes a susceptible component, while the remaining population responds weakly to repetition. The recurrence incorrectly retains it. A direct second-cycle measurement distinguishes these finite outcomes. No caps on hidden-type amplification, independent cell descendants or selection-mechanism identification are used in this example; it is an input-output prediction counterexample.

Even the result 0.16 does not establish cycle 3: another admissible process has `Y_3=0.15`, whereas recurrence predicts 0.064. Two successful cycles therefore cannot license arbitrary long-term prediction. Conversely, a failure of the recurrence could reflect density dependence, changing drug activity, timing/handling or measurement changes as well as a changing cell population. Those are competing explanations, not automatically biological memory.

### Strong ordinary rival and exact comparison

The mandatory rival is P3's already-developed coupled phenotypic-state dynamics, or a simple ordinary history-dependent response model supplied with the same protocol information and first-cycle observations. A constant multiplier is a restrictive response law, not a privileged Agent mechanism. A fair prediction comparison cannot let the state model use the complete four-day outcome while scoring recurrence as though that outcome were withheld. Conversely, calibrating a flexible state model from a single cycle may be underidentified; arbitrary fitted parameters cannot serve as a fair strong forecast.

For the actual screening decision, directly observing the next cycle is an admissible paid action. If two continuations with the same first-cycle output imply different retain/reject decisions, first-cycle data alone do not justify skipping that observation without a separately supported relation. The practical value would have to be a demonstrated transfer of the recurrence or a richer simple rule across genuinely held-out schedules/backgrounds, including the cost of learning and auditing that rule.

The occupied-area Y available in P3 can define an assay-specific recurrence. It must not be relabeled as an absolute cell-count contraction or total descendant survival. Data near the detection floor require interval treatment; treating a reported zero as an exact log-compatible value would be invalid.

## Primary-source result: why this is not currently a new main mechanism

Additional original reading in this pass: P3 Sec8 and Sec9 in full. These passages materially limit the candidate:

- The authors couple their single-treatment models, select coupled topologies, and estimate reset speed and sensitization rate from the alternating-treatment observations. The model explicitly carries treatment-dependent state and memory variables.
- The authors say alternation produces similar cell-density efficacy to the first TRAIL treatment and directly show four days/two cycles. A returning drug's finite efficacy is already part of their observed comparison, not an unasked source question.
- The reported twelve-day extinction is a model simulation. It is not an observed twelve-day validation. This is already stated in the source; preserving the distinction is good evidence handling, not a new biological result.
- The authors infer from their fitted dynamics that sensitization tracks drug dynamics and lacks long-term memory in a specified compartment. They also explicitly discuss continued proliferation and state switching. A recurrence failure would be compatible with this richer model rather than a discovery that the source overlooked history.

The source does not establish the constant-gain law or its transfer beyond selected experiments. Nor does its use of an ordinary state model refute a possible new law. The present issue is that there is no independent biological reason to favor this specific recurrence, and the available test would mostly check a simplifying assumption that the source already replaces with dynamic states. Calling that assumption renewal does not supply the missing scientific difference.

**Current recommendation: do not spend the remaining scientific-test quota merely to evaluate this constant-gain null.** A rejected null would justify retaining a history-dependent model, which is already the source's approach. A non-rejection, especially with few source repeats, would not justify durable cycling or omitting later-cycle measurements in new backgrounds. The current project has not adopted this recurrence as a consequential model assumption that must be audited. If that concrete deployment/modeling choice is later made, the same finite check can be a meaningful internal adequacy test, clearly separated from innovation or transfer validation.
