# Independent challenge

## Initial assessment (recorded before consulting episode notes or source extracts)

The inherited mathematical idea is established: representing an incompletely observed system by predictions of interventions, then compressing those predictions for future decisions, is closely aligned with predictive-state and task-oriented representations. Functional response panels and early response-to-later-response prediction also have obvious biological prior overlap. On the two supplied documents alone I see no established methodological novelty and no empirical evidence of a transferable biological state.

A potentially significant biological question survives: do history-induced differences in future drug susceptibility become observable through a small, standardized panel of nonterminal functional challenges on sister cultures, and does this measurement transfer to genuinely unseen histories and chemotypes at equal assay cost? This is conditional and narrower than discovering a universal cell state. The important contrast is against a strong, ordinary response-panel predictor using the identical information and budget.

My preliminary stopping rule is that a low-dimensional prediction map, a successful reconstruction of a fixed drug panel, or a literature connection to predictive states is not itself a discovery. The experiment must demonstrate a reproducible history-specific blind spot of cheaper measurements and a panel that resolves it prospectively. Unknown future actions require additional support or structure.

## Original-source checks and novelty verdict

I independently read selected primary-source text, rather than accepting the scouts' conclusions:

- Singh et al., **Predictive State Representations: A New Theory for Modeling Dynamical Systems**, UAI 2004, arXiv:1207.4167, PDF pp.4-5. `control_sources/selected_passages.md` reproduces the original: state is predictions for core action-observation tests, and a rank-k system admits k linearly independent core tests. Source identity/hash is in `control_sources/reuse_manifest.json`. The conceptual proposal to measure state by how a system responds to interventions is inherited. A biological probe response is a noisy measurement on a sample; it is not automatically the exact test probability or a spanning core test.
- **Reconciling Rewards with Predictive State Representations**, arXiv:2106.03926, PDF pp.3-5, read directly from `control_sources/arxiv-2106.03926.txt`. Proposition 1 says observation-predictive state need not determine reward. Section 4 constructs R-PSRs from expected rewards of hypothetical futures, including core intents and rank. Directly supervising the susceptibility representation by future function/decision utility addresses this known distinction; it does not establish a new mathematical principle. These finite-POMDP constructions do not prove a finite biological challenge panel sufficient in an open, changing population.
- Svensson et al., **Back to basics: Observed statistics are sufficient to predict drug responses**, DOI `10.64898/2026.06.09.731197`, original bioRxiv HTML in `../2026-10-30-drug-discovery-capability/sources/root10_rhaister_article_redirect.body`. I read the abstract, panel-prediction description, and methods paragraph specifying ridge prediction of an unseen perturbation as a combination of measured panel perturbations, plus additive fallback. Equations embedded as images were not independently rederived. A prepared history can simply be another context in this formulation. Predicting later outcomes instead of contemporaneous molecular summaries changes the data collection but need not change the statistical model. Merely naming a response-panel embedding a predictive state does not distinguish it.
- Original 2025 ALK dynamic-BH3 study, DOI `10.1038/s41419-025-07513-3`, selected introduction/results/methods in `causal/bh3_reading.txt`, derived from the original XML at `../2026-10-31-functional-state-drug-response/challenge/sources/alk_dbp_original.xml` (identity/hash in `causal/local_receipts.json`). The work measures BIM priming after 16 h ALK-inhibitor exposure, compares it with 96 h death, profiles BAD/HRK/MS1 dependencies, and tests sequential BH3-mimetic treatment. Thus early functional challenge, history-induced adaptation, and later treatment guidance already coexist in biological prior art. The method permeabilizes cells with digitonin and fixes them: sister-culture prediction is appropriate; the same assayed cell is not followed. This paper does not establish transfer across arbitrary history chemotypes or that DBP is a universal sufficient state.

**Verdict:** no established methodological novelty. A potentially valuable biological measurement/transport result remains conditional. I cannot assert that the narrow prospective transfer experiment below is novel: targeted reading is not an exhaustive closest-prior search, and foundational DBP papers, other functional precision-medicine platforms, and longitudinal drug-adaptation studies were not systematically checked. The 2025 original makes a broad novelty claim especially unsafe.

## Strongest simple rivals

1. Use each preparation history as a context and train ordinary multivariate ridge from the exact same measured probes to the complete later action-outcome vector. Additive/mean fallback and shrinkage covariance conditioning are important low-data baselines. Match normalization, training contexts, missingness policy, hyperparameter budget, and test observations.
2. Directly predict from baseline, actual history, dose/time and probes without a bottleneck. Include supervised reduced-rank regression/PLS. If compression wins only by finite-sample regularization, report that explanation; do not infer extra information or a novel biological state.
3. For apoptosis-driven outcomes, use an ordinary DBP panel and a fixed DBP-guided treatment rule or ridge. Generic viability, growth rate, cell-cycle composition and early target-drug response can be stronger cheap rivals than expensive passive omics. The candidate must not win by giving these rivals fewer real measurements.
4. Compare fixed expert-selected, random and learned probes at identical total sample/reagent/instrument/time cost. A learned panel needs source-only selection; target outcomes cannot be used to choose probes or the validation histories.

The early response to the very target action is a distinct information regime. It is a legitimate comparator if measured and costed equally; it must not be silently mixed with the claim that a reusable target-independent panel predicts future treatment choices.

## Biological question worth preserving

Can a reusable functional panel reveal a **history-induced reversal in which later treatment is preferable**, when baseline growth/viability and recorded preparation alone fail to predict that reversal in a new independently prepared history? The useful discovery would identify the specific challenge and the bounded history/action regime in which it reveals susceptibility, including reproducible failures. A general claim of cellular state sufficiency, identification of a latent mechanism from prediction, or a zero-shot prediction of unsupported new actions should stop.

There are two distinct outcomes. If full-panel ridge and the compressed map perform equally, a useful panel/measurement finding may survive, but there is no demonstrated representation advantage. If the panel beats cheaper passive measurements but equals existing DBP, this may be an application or replication, not a new functional-state principle. If compression preserves performance using the same measurements, dimension reduction alone has not reduced assay cost.

## Minimum distinguishing experiment

Start with a **fixed later action menu**, not an unseen-action claim. A concrete small feasibility design is one authenticated parental cell model; vehicle plus four development preparation histories; two genuinely held-out preparation histories chosen without their future outcomes; three candidate later drugs plus vehicle at fixed physical dose/time; four prespecified functional probes plus assay controls. Keep history exposures, washout/residual-drug checks, cell number, elapsed time and endpoint fixed. Preparation histories must include more than different wells from the same original exposure. At least three independent preparation blocks are a feasibility floor, not a powered efficacy study; use those blocks to estimate the replication requirement for a later confirmatory experiment.

At the decision time split each preparation into randomized sister cultures. Measure the probe panel and baseline/growth controls on diagnostic sisters. Independently assign every later action to endpoint sisters, ensuring a complete action contrast for each history. Use a viable-cell endpoint with growth-rate correction where appropriate and a death endpoint if claiming cytotoxic susceptibility. For an apoptosis setting include a matched DBP arm. Destructive probe measurements do not supply longitudinal measurements of the same cells.

Freeze panel selection, fitting and thresholds using development histories only. Give ridge, direct history-plus-panel prediction, reduced-rank/PLS and the candidate exactly the same source observations and optimization budget. Predict each held-out history before opening its future endpoint. Measure action-selection regret and assay cost; retain uncertainty by independent preparation block. A primary gain needs a predeclared practical margin and compatible uncertainty, not a tiny point-estimate lead. A cost-reduction claim requires physically assaying fewer probes, not retrospectively deleting coordinates.

The first decisive observation is a reproducible history-by-action preference reversal in independent preparations, plus an admissible panel measurement that predicts its direction in the held-out histories beyond the cheapest prespecified rival. The second, separate observation is that the proposed compression/panel selection improves on equally informed panel ridge or preserves a prespecified quality level at lower actual assay cost. Failure at the first gate removes the proposed biological use case; failure only at the second removes the special representation claim.

A fixed panel cannot be declared sufficient merely because no residual history effect reached significance. Test same-action residual differences across matched/overlapping representations with a prespecified tolerance and measurement-error model; lack of power or lack of overlap is inconclusive. Conditional-mean prediction can miss changes in variance or tails relevant to decisions. Different actions producing different outcomes at the same z are expected and are not a counterexample.

**Chemotype scope must be explicit.** Holding out the drugs used to prepare histories tests transfer across preparation chemistry while keeping later actions supported. Holding out the later action chemotype tests another, harder extrapolation. The latter requires a source-trained action representation/structural support and a separate whole-chemotype test; a fixed-menu decoder cannot answer it. Do not claim both from the first experiment. No suitable complete public history/probe/future-action table has been qualified in this review. Exposed L1000/PRISM arrays cannot substitute for these unmeasured sequential outcomes. This experiment is a data-acquisition requirement, not an authorized fitting plan.

## Addendum: action-contrast sufficiency (requested after initial review)

I read the specific proposal and qualifications in `causal_sources.md` after saving the assessment above. This does not revise the initial novelty judgment.

The restriction `E[Y(1)-Y(0) | S=s,H=h] = tau(s)` is a coherent, weaker scientific target than full outcome-distribution or even action-specific conditional-mean sufficiency. For two actions it permits `E[Y(a)|S,H] = b(S,H) + a*tau(S)`. History-specific common baseline shifts cancel when choosing the action maximizing the expected value of this one endpoint. Discovering a small measurable S that preserves an important drug-choice contrast across specified adaptation histories would be a useful biological result if demonstrated. The equality is ordinary absence of residual history effect modification on a chosen outcome scale, or a conditional effect-invariance/transport restriction. It is not a new causal method or architecture. Nothing read establishes that such a functional S exists or is novel in this biological regime.

A **two-history by two-randomized-action design** is the correct minimum factorial skeleton to challenge this restriction. Early state must precede A, overlap across histories, and be measured with a common assay; independently prepared blocks and randomized/exchangeable diagnostic/endpoint siblings are essential. Randomizing A identifies eligible-preparation conditional next-action effects, not the total causal effect of prior H or effects in cells killed before eligibility. A terminal viability assay cannot retrospectively identify that original-population effect.

Two histories alone can test a contrast difference/equivalence in their overlap; they cannot establish generalization across arbitrary unseen histories. For held-history transport, use a source-frozen mapping and an independent target history/preparation, and do not fit or adjust that mapping using target outcomes before scoring. If the same held-history outcomes are first used to establish contrast equivalence, then used to advertise successful transport, they supply an internal consistency check rather than independent prospective confirmation. Use disjoint preparation blocks for admission/equivalence and prediction validation, or predeclare joint testing and account for selection. Representation learning itself must be source-only; common support cannot be created by a collapsing encoder.

The test requires a prespecified biologically consequential equivalence margin for `tau(s,h1)-tau(s,h2)`, a defined outcome scale and overlap region, and adequate precision. Failing to detect an H-by-A interaction is not equivalence. With continuous, learned, noisy S, estimating a contrast at each exact s is unrealistic in a tiny design; either prespecify coarse clinically/biologically meaningful strata or a small contrast model with its assumptions. Quantify overlap, measurement error and uncertainty. If only the sign of tau is needed, sign preservation may be weaker still than magnitude equivalence, but near-zero contrasts need a declared abstention/tie rule.

The cancellation does **not** preserve absolute-response or safety decisions. A history can shift survival/death under both actions while preserving their contrast; one history may then breach a minimum efficacy or maximum toxicity threshold. Selecting between drug and no treatment for an expected net benefit can use a contrast only when that endpoint and utility fully encode harms/costs and there is no separate absolute constraint. Chance constraints, tail toxicity, nonlinear utility, multiple endpoints or different cost weights require additional conditional means/distributions or calibrated history terms. For more than two actions, invariance must hold for every relevant contrast against a reference, on the declared decision scale. One invariant pair does not certify a whole treatment menu.

Therefore retain this as the sharper **bounded biological question** and a cheaper initial falsification gate. Stop claims of full state sufficiency, universal transfer, or safety calibration from this gate. Keep the same strong ordinary contrast-regression/DBP rivals and the later matched-cost prospective decision test. The 2025 DBP source is close prior; its reported 16 h profiling should not be equated with a demonstrated 16 h drug switch. The causal scout specifically notes incomplete timing/washout detail for several in-vitro sequences, and a separately documented 24 h lorlatinib-to-next-action schedule. I did not newly re-read that latter section and therefore treat it as a source-located lead, not my independently audited timing finding.
