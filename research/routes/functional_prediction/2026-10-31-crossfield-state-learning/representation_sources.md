## Answer that changes the research direction

**The general idea is established elsewhere:** discard observation details while preserving the quantities required for a declared prediction or decision problem. VIB formalizes target-relevant compression; MuZero trains latent action dynamics only through reward/value/policy; proper value equivalence formalizes when an incomplete model is enough for optimal planning; TD-JEPA makes long-horizon, policy-conditioned representation learning possible using offline one-step transitions. Naming a biological embedding “functional state”, predicting its future embedding, or removing a reconstruction decoder is therefore insufficient novelty.

**Their guarantee is conditional, not a way to recover missing information.** The task family, action support, measured transitions/outcomes, and sufficient observed state determine what can be preserved. Zero-shot new reward/task in the same dynamics is different from an unseen drug with unknown effects or a changed cell system. Off-policy and reward-free do not mean action-free, transition-free, or assumption-free. These methods do not turn an unmatched RNA signature and a viability table into longitudinal cellular transitions.

## What “retain future response” must mean

For a reusable state encoder `z=f(x)`, declare an action set A0 and endpoint family Y0. A meaningful desired property is that `p(y | x,a) ≈ p(y | z,a)` across that declared domain. For intervention language replace the observational conditional only when the experiment/design justifies it. A compact state may be sufficient for viability at one time/dose yet discard information needed for toxicity, delayed relapse, a different dose or a different target family.

A direct diagnostic counterexample: two cell contexts have identical compressed z and indistinguishable outcomes for every training drug; under a held-out drug their outcomes differ. Training evidence cannot decide whether to separate those contexts. Penalizing information, fitting a latent future, or using an LLM to name the latent cannot identify the missing drug response. Action descriptors can supply a shared-effects assumption, but that assumption needs validation on genuinely held-out drug/target families. Protein, epigenetic or history measurements may resolve the ambiguity; RNA alone may not.

“Predict the future embedding” has another failure: a self-defined target can erase outcome-relevant biology while being easy to predict, or collapse. TD-JEPA's policy-dependent targets, successor-feature link, covariance control and assumptions address a specified RL setting; transferring only its latent MSE objective omits the reason the latent supports decisions.

## Smallest useful biological pilot: endpoint first

This is a proposed, unrun design, not an admitted dataset or an instruction to execute existing protected outcomes. Use an **independent, openly available pharmacogenomic panel with pre-treatment expression and measured drug-response endpoints on overlapping cell lines** (GDSC-type design; current availability, version, license and actual join keys still require a metadata audit). Freeze one assay endpoint, dose/time definition, and compound identity mapping. No L1000-to-PRISM pairing by analogy, pseudotime transitions, cross-panel response imputation as truth, or new wet-lab work is needed.

1. **Admission and frozen task.** Establish that baseline expression precedes exposure, outcomes refer to the same named cell context and tested compound, and replicate/batch handling is known. Specify the finite measured action panel. If these facts cannot be established, stop at the metadata gate.
2. **Representation comparison.** Candidate: small action-conditioned outcome bottleneck, `NLL[y | f(x),a] + β KL[q(z|x),p(z)]`, with the state encoder depending on x alone so it can be reused across drugs. This is a conditional extension of the VIB principle, not a method claimed by the VIB paper. Compare fixed latent sizes and a frozen small β grid using training/validation data only. First use a deterministic supervised reduced-rank baseline; introduce stochastic VIB only if it adds a testable advantage.
3. **Stronger simple rivals.** (a) drug mean + cell/context mean + low-rank interaction; (b) PCA plus ridge using the same action features and parameter budget; (c) PLS/supervised reduced-rank regression; (d) full-expression regularized conditional predictor with no bottleneck; (e) nearest-context response-profile transfer using only training outcomes. An autoencoder is a useful reconstruction control but is not the strongest comparator. Separate a drug-only predictor to expose compound-identity shortcuts.
4. **Two explicit transfer tests.** Hold out whole cell contexts for generalization to a new baseline; independently hold out whole drug/target families for the **new-action** claim. Keep replicate, compound aliases and close analogues together. The second test needs meaningful held-out-action descriptors; an unseen one-hot drug ID cannot be predicted by construction. Report interpolation and each holdout separately.
5. **Decision outcome.** Rank the assayed actions within each held-out context and compute regret/utility against the measured endpoint, alongside prediction error and ranking. Define the decision rule and endpoint orientation before viewing test outcomes. A lower viability endpoint is an assay objective, not proof of selective therapeutic benefit. Cluster uncertainty by held-out cell context and drug family rather than treating all wells as independent.
6. **Stop condition.** If a compact supervised linear/rank model matches the bottleneck's predictive and decision results within the preregistered practical margin, the result supports a simple task representation, not a new deep-state method. If the bottleneck improves in-panel error but fails drug-family holdout, restrict its claim to the tested action panel. If decision utility does not improve, do not claim improved drug choice from prettier latents or lower reconstruction loss.

The additional scientific question would be **which modest extra measurement separates contexts that are predictively aliased for a specified drug family**, and whether it lowers held-out decision regret beyond baseline expression and stronger simple rivals. That question needs measured ambiguity and independently tested utility; these four papers do not supply the missing biological evidence.
