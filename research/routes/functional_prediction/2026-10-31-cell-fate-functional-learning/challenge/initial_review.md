# Independent initial review of probe question v1

Saved 2026-10-31 before receiving a proposer response or reading developer notes, investigator conclusions, PROCESS.md, record.md, or root interpretive discussion. This assesses the frozen qualification question, not admission of a numerical model or a claim of completed validation.

## Finding

The question has a coherent, potentially useful experimental meaning: can an affordable observation, taken before a specified next cancer-cell challenge, replace otherwise necessary history-specific calibration when predicting that challenge's measured consequence? A supported answer would add knowledge about the conditions under which a measured state is predictively sufficient. A negative result could identify where history still matters. Neither contribution requires a new architecture.

The supplied resources do not yet authenticate an informative numerical test of that transport claim. They support the biological motivation, already published reporter–response associations, and examples of ordered treatment effects. They do not presently establish a joint, prospectively timed, preparation-indexed observation/outcome resource spanning the needed histories. This is a resource-qualification limit, not evidence against the hypothesis.

The scientific difference is narrow. Predicting cancer-cell fate from a compact response state, using a shared threshold across conditions, and explaining treatment sequences with sensitivity-state models are already target-field capabilities. What could differ here is a demonstrated **pre-next-challenge**, fixed-information, cross-history/preparation restriction with no target-outcome recalibration. Merely fitting an ordinary conditional model to the published representative experiment would not establish that difference. The present packet supplies no new learning rule, numerical benefit, biological mediation result, or validated intervention-selection capability.

## Strongest prior and ordinary alternatives

Peyre2025 is the closest biological prior, not merely motivation. Original XML Sec3 compares repeated, resting, and sustained regimens using population density; Sec6/Fig4 already relates reporter abundance in surviving/replated cells to susceptibility and death timing after the next treatment; Sec7 studies ordered alternation; Sec4/Sec8/Sec20 fit sensitivity compartments and drug-history/memory dynamics. Recovering the known alternation result or the reported RIPK3 association would reproduce source-owned findings. The PSM2D description explicitly selects a topology and estimates additional rates using relevant observations (Sec8), so its fit is not evidence of the proposed no-target-calibration transfer.

Roux2015 is a stronger capability prior than “an older dataset.” Its original Results subsection “A cell fate boundary is set by the rate and duration of C8 activation” tests a shared threshold across agonist doses and classes. “Predicting and confirming three-agent effects” reports intervention predictions and experimental confirmation. The original Methods “Determining the value θ” distinguishes a threshold fitted on TRAIL data and used for other agonist/co-drug analyses from an ABT-specific threshold optimized using replicate combination experiments. The manuscript therefore already treats portability, useful prediction, and necessary changes in a shared fate relation. This does **not** establish the present pre-challenge history-transfer question: its activation trajectories are measured after the tested agonist is added. “Modulating the position of the cell fate boundary” also provides an important boundary case: changing Bcl-2 regulation changes fate without a corresponding change in the measured C8 dynamics. A compact state is condition-specific, not automatically universally sufficient.

Fiandaca2026 extends this same Roux2015 data lineage with inferred effective state coordinates and a dose-dependent decision surface. Physical PDF pages19–20 fit individual trajectories over up to ten hours, using outcome-specific weights and exclusions. Calling fitted initial concentrations “effective pre-treatment variables” does not turn their estimates into independent pre-treatment measurements. This is neither a new 2026 cancer cohort nor independent validation of Peyre histories. Its original physical page23/reference20 and Roux DOI metadata authenticate the 2015 ancestry.

The strongest ordinary statistical rival is a common probe-specific conditional regression or survival model with the same baseline observations, compared with a model that also permits history terms/interactions. The proposed invariant map is a restriction of this ordinary family; a history-aware model contains it as a special case. Appropriate history-dependent compartment/state-space models are also substantive target alternatives. If the invariant restriction holds with useful precision, the scientific result would be the supported restriction and measurement economy, even if ordinary regression implements it. A lower prediction error alone would not identify cellular mediation or a new state-abstraction principle.

## What the existing resources can and cannot support

Peyre supplies the most relevant pre-next-challenge experimental structure. Sec16 makes baseline Cherry a plausible observable. Fig4D may, after allowed authentication, support a narrow reporter-versus-fixed-horizon-fate analysis: two outcome-labeled columns can encode useful joint information if the cells, timing, and denominator are documented. I cannot infer absence of such information from the header. However, neither that potential pairing nor a large number of cells demonstrates multiple preparation/history units, transferable support, or prospective normalization. Fig4C's separate history distributions do not establish that its rows join Fig4D/F; equal row positions would not establish a join.

The Fig1/Fig5 density measurements have an ordered-history interpretation, but destructive staining means the measurements at different times are separate-well observations. They must not be reinterpreted as repeated measurements of one unchanged culture, nor joined to Fig4 single cells by treatment name alone. These population records and Fig4 cells answer different estimands unless a valid hierarchical pairing/acquisition design is supplied.

The Fiandaca archive inventory establishes the presence of outcome-grouped FRET files, fitted parameters and classification files. It does not establish their matrix semantics, complete risk sets, biological preparation identities or raw prospective preprocessing. No matrix was opened. An honest reanalysis of its selected trajectories could be scientifically useful under a narrower task, but cannot be treated as an untouched cohort or prechallenge observation merely by renaming inferred coordinates.

JBC2026 original Results sec1.1, Experimental procedures sec3.2/sec3.7 and Discussion support a concrete functional-response issue: a net-growth measurement can obscure different contributions of growth inhibition and killing, and GRADE/MEDUSA are established approaches. FLICK uses dead-cell observations plus start/end total-cell measurements; these procedures do not authenticate a complete live-burden time series, recovery endpoint or history/probe/state matrix in the present packet. Its different NSCLC experiments cannot supply missing paired outcomes for Peyre HeLa-RIPK3 cells. If the endpoint is net burden, net burden is a legitimate directly measured endpoint; its inability to identify death/growth mechanisms does not automatically invalidate that prediction task.

## Smallest decisive next qualification

Request or locate metadata documenting joint observations for one explicitly chosen probe and endpoint across independently prepared cultures and multiple histories, including the exact baseline timing and survivor/replating rule. Stop before viewing response values if this metadata cannot establish the needed unit, pairing, preparation split and common support. This is a qualification request; it does not authorize new data-value inspection or execution.

If that resource exists, a subsequent separately frozen numerical study can compare a common state-only map against an equal-information history-aware rival and source-relevant ordinary models. The informative contrast is reproducible history-dependent error at overlapping measured states and meaningful precision in the held-out preparation/history. A reproducible residual rejects the chosen state restriction. Good common-map performance with adequate precision supports it only for the specified probe, endpoint and support. Non-overlap, too few independent preparations or imprecise results leave the restriction undecided. No numerical threshold is fabricated here.
