## Initial biological framing, saved before reading the scout

Inputs at this point: the parent task and episode `record.md` only. The following is a question framing, not source verification.

The drug-discovery object should be the capacity of a living treated population to regenerate disease-relevant progeny and to remain susceptible to a subsequent intervention. Current viable count conflates loss of reproductive capacity, reversible arrest, and delayed recovery. This creates a concrete decision: continue a drug, allow a recovery interval and rechallenge, or apply a different challenge. A useful Virtual Cell restriction would let limited observations choose between those actions in an unseen cellular preparation or exposure history. Predicting a published fate label or reconstructing a trajectory is insufficient by itself.

Three candidate biological framings deserve separate examination before selection:

1. **Commitment and recovery.** An intervention can change the fraction irreversibly committed to death and the rate at which surviving arrested cells regain reproductive capacity. The hoped-for restriction is an experimentally transportable relationship between recovery and the benefit of a second challenge. This is stronger than fitting separate growth and death rates, and may be false if recovery and drug vulnerability are distinct processes.
2. **Challenge-defined cellular memory.** Two exposure histories with equal counts and growth can leave different future susceptibility. Rather than infer a latent molecular coordinate, use an observed functional response to a standardized challenge as a test of what memory matters. The scientific question is whether a small measured response profile is sufficient for a useful next intervention across histories. A finite-state label or generic partially observed model would not establish that sufficiency.
3. **Lineage replacement.** Equal current survivors can yield different numbers and timings of future proliferative lineages. A useful restriction may concern lineage founders or recovery-time distributions rather than single-cell survival. This must be distinguished from ordinary clonogenic survival, branching-process growth, and standard time-to-event models.

Initial necessary distinctions: observations from the same tracked cell versus independently sampled marginal populations; viable survival versus demonstrated reproductive recovery; pretreatment inputs versus trajectory information that arrives only after the decision; a real second challenge versus an inferred transition; independently repeated preparations versus many related cells. Cross-species or cross-mechanism transport is not assumed.

Candidate success criterion: specify actual use-time observations, a causal intervention rule, a prediction that ordinary aggregate growth/death or unconstrained history models do not already entail, and the smallest economical observation that can discriminate it. Failure to have that observation is an identification/data boundary, not biological falsification. No fitted experiment is admitted by a good question alone.
