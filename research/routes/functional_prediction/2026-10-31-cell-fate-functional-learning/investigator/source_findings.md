Original: **Survival of ampicillin-treated uropathogenic Escherichia coli is independent of single-cell growth rates** (2026; DOI10.1038/s44259-025-00180-6; PMC12864937). This is an authenticated compact source-data bundle, not an authenticated rectangular benchmark or complete sampling universe. No workbook fetched or parsed.

Drug-discovery need: distinguish a transiently intact cell from one able to regrow after treatment withdrawal; avoid progressing an antibiotic regimen on a misleading growth or lysis proxy. Same-cell microfluidic tracking explicitly observes before/during/after treatment, washout and resumption of division. Clinical UPEC CFT073 is the principal strain, ampicillin the principal drug, with LB/M9/M9+alphaMG environmental contexts and limited additional strain checks. Main LB lysis design has three independent experiments. Other reporter designs have two repeats, so cells are not independent preparations. Methods select25–35 chambers with1–5 starting cells; imaging every4min over24h in LB and5–10min in M9. Projected area is fit exponentially for growth; lysis rate counts events per currently observed population. The underlying observation definitions can change with fragmentation/L-forms. Survival explicitly requires regrowth after washout, while death can occur after washout. Rechallenge is shown for selected survivors, not a complete all-cell intervention-order matrix.

Load-bearing negative evidence: ordinary slow growth is neither necessary nor sufficient for ampicillin survival in this clinical isolate. Published data describe growing survivors, non-growing cells that die, overlapping pre-drug growth distributions, and morphology changes shared by eventual survivors and nonsurvivors. An alphaMG population shift cannot be interpreted as a universal single-cell growth-to-survival rule. CFU plating and MPN/microscopy give different survival assessments because wash/recovery media and fragile cell states change what is observed. See lines12–18 and25–28. These original findings make a universal dormant=persister restriction untenable; they do not supply a replacement invariant.

Useful learning object, if data qualify: **context-dependent recovery competence**, with lysis and post-washout regrowth kept distinct. A practical discriminating task could predict later regrowing burden from a fixed early non-destructive observation across independently prepared cultures, with a target decision such as whether a regimen leaves a recoverable population. This is currently only a proposed question. With one principal antibiotic and few contexts, the source cannot establish unseen-drug generalization. Source data organized around published figures may be selected survivors or pooled quantities, rather than every cell and censoring event. Biological proof of the observation distinction is stronger than present evidence for a transferable restriction.

Ordinary rivals must include: per-context empirical survival; growth-only or growth-plus-time logistic/survival prediction; one-/two-phase exponential kill curves (the original already fits two-phase decay with kfast≥1.3kslow); a standard birth/death model with context-specific rates; and direct post-washout measurement at the same cost. A flexible hazard model that adds washout phase and medium can reproduce apparent state dependence without a new theory. For cheap observations, distinguish low computational/storage cost (processed XLSX) from acquisition cost:24h microfluidic imaging and manual tracing are not equivalent to one plate-reader well. No cost advantage is established.

Specific next evidence: adopt a strict header whitelist before any values; inspect sheet/table schemas of Fig2_S2,Fig4 andFig5 to ask whether lineage/experiment IDs, all-cell denominators, exposure and washout boundaries, tracking/censoring flags, eventual regrowth observation windows and contexts coexist. Stop if only selected survivors or figure-aggregated values are present. Need a genuinely broader multi-drug/context, independent-preparation functional panel to support intervention learning. ## Cancer functional opening and closest ordinary prior

Original: **Genome-wide profiling identifies the genetic dependencies of cell death following EGFR inhibition** (JBC2026; DOI10.1016/j.jbc.2026.111414; PMC13133947). Full XML saved. Read egfr_primary_text.txt lines9–13,19–22,42–53,62–63. Concrete therapeutic need is distinguishing EGFR-inhibitor cytostasis from actual cell killing to select backgrounds/combination interventions. Six NSCLC backgrounds, two main EGFR inhibitors, eight-dose designs and three independent biological replicates are explicit in Fig1 design. These are assay-specific findings; immune-mediated death and microenvironment are absent (line43).

FLICK is economical functional measurement: dead-cell SYTOX fluorescence is read longitudinally; separate T0 plate and destructive end lysis provide starting/terminal total fluorescence. Live fluorescence is inferred from total and dead measurements. This is a repeated population observation, not a complete same-cell fate trajectory; endpoint totals do not by themselves identify live counts at every intermediate time. No functional washout/recovery experiment is authenticated here. Original source lists RNA-seq and chemo-genetic supplementary workbooks, but does not authenticate a complete processed time-resolved functional matrix. It therefore does not yet pass the resource gate for our proposed task.

Especially strong prior: MEDUSA already calibrates population growth/death with FLICK and simulates knockout effects to infer growth/death from chemo-genetic fold changes (line53). GRADE jointly assesses growth and lethal fraction (line13); ordinary GR, lethal-fraction kinetics and GRADE/MEDUSA are mandatory comparators. Merely separating proliferation and death or naming a latent process is not an innovation. This investigator did not fetch RNA/chemo-genetic workbooks because they would not resolve missing direct functional recovery labels. The2025 FLICK protocol (DOI10.3791/67768) and older GRADE2020 original were located through source references but EPMC fulltext attempts returned500; those originals remain unread and must not be cited as newly verified full texts.
