## Scientific decision and practical meaning

For a cancer-screening team, a state measurement would be useful if it helped triage a specified next intervention after an initial treatment. The proposed restriction is a common, probe-specific map `f_a(S)`: observe `S` before the next challenge `a`, learn the map from allowed histories, and predict the later response in a held-out history/preparation without history-specific target-outcome calibration. The falsifying observation would be reproducible history-dependent response differences at overlapping measured states. Agreement would support only the stated probe, endpoint, horizon and population; low power or lack of state overlap would remain inconclusive.

This is ordinary conditional predictive sufficiency, with controlled state abstraction and predictive-state methods as relevant mathematical precedents. Common regression or survival models can implement the restriction. A history-aware conditional model contains the common map as a special case and is a mandatory rival. A useful biological restriction need not require a new architecture, but merely naming one a Virtual Cell does not establish a contribution.

*Graphical Modeling from Marginal Information via Optimal Transport*, [arXiv2610.40070v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40070v1), dated2026-10-29, distinguishes a regularized choice of a coupling from identification of a generating transition law. Its special conditional-covariance calibration requires the prescribed graph/Gaussian/cost family. Generic paired timepoints do not identify arbitrary cellular birth/death/switch rates. *Separable Effects in Four-Arm and Two-Arm Designs*, [arXiv2605.05984v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05984v1), dated2026-05-07, distinguishes actual independently assigned treatment components from two outputs of a model. A history/probe factorial can estimate regimen contrasts under the relevant assumptions; it does not automatically separate proliferation and death mechanisms or identify mediation.
