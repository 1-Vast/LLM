# Independent closest-prior and counterexample challenge

Date: 2026-10-31 (Asia/Shanghai)  
Scope: action-contrast sufficiency; small functional probes across treatment histories; “easy-input” virtual-cell claims.  
Boundary: targeted source reading only. No model fitting, response-matrix inspection, protected-outcome access, code changes, or claim of priority/firstness.

## Finding

The broad idea is already occupied in pieces that compose into a strong rival: dynamic BH3 profiling supplies an early functional perturbation and later death readout; functional precision medicine supplies the treatment-selection framing; Rhaister (2026 preprint) predicts unmeasured perturbation responses from a few measured perturbations in a new context; causal transport theory supplies the conditions for moving a conditional action effect across populations; and active system identification supplies the rule “choose an executable probe that separates competing response models.” Predictive-state and value-equivalence work supplies the mathematical language for action-conditioned sufficiency.

Therefore, “a small probe makes an easy-input virtual cell,” “a functional panel predicts a later treatment,” or “a compact state is sufficient for future actions” is not a defensible novelty claim from the present evidence. A conditional biological result could still be useful: a prespecified, inexpensive probe might preserve one next-action contrast across independently prepared, genuinely held-out histories and chemical families. That would be a bounded response law, not a new general architecture or a claim that the probe reconstructs a cell.

## Strongest rival

The strongest practical rival is **DBP or another fixed functional panel plus ordinary response-panel reconstruction with known-history and history-by-action terms**. Give the rival the same measured probe responses, dose/time and endpoint labels. Fit a simple ridge/covariance or response-surface model and allow a history interaction. A proposed compact “virtual-cell state” must improve a prespecified held-history/action decision at equal assay cost, or achieve non-inferior calibrated decisions with fewer assays. Comparing against a molecular-only baseline or an unmeasured full-input model is insufficient and can manufacture an apparent gain.

## Decisive falsification test

Freeze two prior histories, one common early probe panel `S`, two randomized next actions `A`, a fixed later endpoint/denominator `Y`, and independent preparation blocks. Restrict analysis to a declared region of overlapping measured-state support. The candidate restriction is:

`E[Y(1)-Y(0) | S=s, H=h] = tau(s)`.

The decisive falsifier is a reproducible history-by-action contrast difference at the same measured `S`, exceeding a prospectively justified consequential-error tolerance across independent preparations. This falsifies the measured-state contrast law. Different absolute outcomes under the same action are not enough to reject an action-contrast law; differences under different actions are not enough either. A nonsignificant interaction is not equivalence. If the state assay is destructive, use randomized/exchangeable sister cultures and retain survival/reseeding/denominator history. If the next action is observational, sequential exchangeability, positivity, consistency and censoring/eligibility assumptions must be explicit; balancing or an embedding cannot repair absent support.

To claim a positive result, require (i) equivalence of action contrasts within the declared margin with adequate precision, (ii) a held-history and held-preparation prediction/choice test, and (iii) superiority or non-inferiority to the strongest ordinary panel/history rival under matched measured information and cost. A result confined to one probe, one endpoint, one lineage or one action menu must be labelled that way.

## Residual uncertainty and stop conditions

- The search was targeted, not exhaustive. The inspected 2025–2026 sources strongly block a broad novelty claim but cannot prove that no closer paper exists.
- Rhaister’s HTML abstract was available locally; equations and complete methods were not independently rederived here. Treat its claims as source-reported preprint results, not as verified biological benefit.
- The 2026 BH3 review is a review, not a new prospective validation. Its clinical-translation warnings make a universal easy-input state less credible but do not quantify assay cost or transport failure.
- The ALK DBP study demonstrates early priming-to-later-death association and sequential therapy, but does not supply the matched cross-history/action table needed for the proposed contrast law.
- Active-system-identification evidence is a transfer principle from robotics, not biological evidence. Its parameter-compensation failure is a warning against mechanism claims, not a direct falsification of a functional predictor.
- No current MAESTRO numerical result was used to support novelty or transport. No model was fitted in this challenge.

Stop the novelty line if the matched ordinary panel/history model reaches the same held-out action decision; if state support is absent; if the probe consumes nearly the full assay budget; if contrast equivalence fails; or if the result disappears under independent preparation, endpoint or chemistry holdout. A negative closure result remains scientifically useful when it identifies where history or a missing measurable component matters.
