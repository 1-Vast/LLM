## What the source makes available and already owns

The controlled sequence library makes an RNA intervention question possible without treating endogenous observational promoter associations as interventions. The assay varies promoter context, cis sequence, and (under pEF1alpha) transcription-drug conditions. It measures mature RNA abundance, including cryptic isoforms. It does not directly measure transcription speed, promoter-specific factor occupancy, nascent splicing order, or therapeutic response.

The source already owns promoter-specific inclusion, promoter-dependent motif activity and reversals, prediction of promoter-response clusters from sequence/splice/structure features, exon-length dependence, upstream-intron effects, and weak-donor fidelity rescue by an added upstream intron. It has checked alternative PSI/mutation-effect scales. These are neither new mechanisms nor fresh validation targets merely because a different learner is used.

Most relevant opening: Sec8, paragraphs 2-4 associates altered donor choice with whether an upstream intron is retained, and the added-intron experiment changes weak donor fidelity. This raises a distinction between (a) promoter action on the abundance of different splicing histories and (b) promoter action on recognition within the same history. The paper discusses timing, recruitment, and processivity as alternatives, rather than identifying one. Final mature isoform identity is not a measurement of chronological splice history: a donor can be recognized before or after an upstream intron is excised, and transient commitments are invisible. This distinction is load-bearing.

## Initial alternatives and developing question

1. A promoter/drug functional fingerprint with a low-dimensional context encoder could improve response prediction but reduces to ordinary multi-task regression or matrix completion. Its architecture is not a biological mechanism. It only becomes a substantive empirical claim if a specified measured fingerprint is sufficient for a new intervention response, survives same-information ordinary rivals, and exposes a useful acquisition saving. No such sufficiency is established by response clustering.

2. Treating promoter effects as an elongation-rate knob is already challenged by the source: promoter differences often exceed and do not reproduce the tested drug effects. Transcriptional drugs are pleiotropic and the actual experiment does not provide all promoter-by-drug crosses. A latent rate inferred from mature PSI cannot separately identify initiation, elongation, recruitment, and isoform stability. Calling that state kinetics would overclaim.

3. A more consequential question is whether a local cis change and a context change obey a transport law after accounting for the actual competing isoform pathways. Potential capability: a target-family wild-type isoform profile under a target context, plus a measured cis-edit effect in a reference context, predicts the edited target-context profile, including cryptic/retained outcomes. All calibration responses must be paid and disclosed; holding out only the final edited-context cell is not unseen-gene zero-shot prediction. The intended output is a reporter RNA distribution, not binding, drug efficacy, or an ASO effect.

The physical premise for option 3 would be modular perturbation of local splice recognition while other recognition relations remain invariant. Algebraically, this is a constrained log-linear/competing-outcome model. The empirical biological restriction, not a graph or softmax, would carry the claim. A generic weighted redistribution of all outcome odds is ordinary exponential tilting and is not new. A valid mechanism needs to say which physically defined outcomes share an edit multiplier, why they should share it, and where promoter-dependent recruitment violates it.

## Constructive thought experiment and immediate failure

These are symbolic reasoning cases, not simulations. Suppose a donor-site-strength edit changes only recognition of one donor and every final isoform can be assigned unambiguously to use/nonuse of that donor. If it multiplies the relevant recognition odds by one factor independent of promoter and upstream history, a measured factor in one context and the full wild-type target profile determine the edited target profile by reweighting donor-using outcomes and renormalizing. Isoform identity supplies the incidence channel that scalar PSI loses. A source-context perturbation effect plus target WT PSI alone cannot distinguish rescue of canonical inclusion from diversion into a cryptic donor product.

But the same three measured corners of a WT/edit by reference/target rectangle can be generated by arbitrary promoter-specific recruitment interactions; its fourth corner can then differ freely. Even the full WT isoform distributions need not identify those interactions. If the local edit changes an RBP motif, secondary structure, splice timing, or decay, a universal donor multiplier is not justified. Source-owned motif-direction reversals directly contradict the broadest context-invariant edit claim. Thus a successful aggregate fit would not identify a molecular mechanism; a deliberately constrained splice-site edit class and prospective interaction challenge are needed.

## More biologically specific branch

A possible refinement is a measured dependence of a local donor edit on prior upstream splicing: one effect law when a prior splice event has occurred, another when it has not. This could predict portability or failure of a splice-restoring intervention in promoter backgrounds with differing upstream processing. It has a real biological object, but the current endpoint table does not observe the state before donor choice. Conditioning on final upstream retention may be collider selection and cannot establish a causal history gate. A successful conditional probability model would demonstrate a portable endpoint relation at most.

The physically distinct channel that could separate this branch from ordinary pathway mixing is nascent or temporally resolved joint measurement of upstream excision and downstream splice commitment, ideally coupled to a controlled splice-history perturbation and a promoter-by-drug cross. It is not present in the known table schema. An upstream-intron addition is already in the paper, and may change timing, RNA sequence, structure, and factor recruitment together; simply repeating its rescue is not new.
