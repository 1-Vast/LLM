## Application and proposed restriction

A human oncology screening laboratory has reference-support dose curves for several drugs. It must decide which hits remain useful on a second controlled support before measuring all drug/context entries. In one qualified cell background, the candidate says that drugs/doses isoeffective on the reference support remain isoeffective on the changed support, within an independently specified family of load-generating drugs.

An enabling model, proposed rather than observed, is

`y(d,c,s)=F(q_d(c)-a_s)`.

The endpoint increases with functional injury. F is one susceptibility/response function for the fixed population/interval. q is a drug/dose injury, invariant under the support change. a is a protective margin determined by support, invariant to drug identity within the family. On a one-to-one part of F, equality `y(d,c_d,s0)=y(r,c_r,s0)` implies equal injury and hence `y(d,c_d,s1)=y(r,c_r,s1)`. A reference drug measured on both supports thus supplies a common map `T_s1<-s0` to predict an unmeasured target/support entry.

Different dose-to-injury functions are allowed; equal old IC50s/slopes are not required. Exact plateau equality does not identify injury. Finite noise requires matching intervals and calibration uncertainty; old-response support overlap is necessary. The family and reference must be assigned without looking at target-context results. Cross-background transport is a separate unestablished claim.

## Observable distinction, failure cases and ordinary alternatives

The actual restriction is equality across distinct drugs/doses that independently attain the same old effect, then cross a new support. A good one-drug curve fit or compositional consistency of invertible maps alone is insufficient. A prespecified hit threshold makes such a prediction relevant to advance/reject decisions.

One analytic possibility: drugs generate different doses of the same injury, while extracellular support only shifts a shared antioxidant protection margin; matched old injury then remains matched. No simulation or biological effect is claimed. A concrete failure: one drug produces injury while another blocks support-induced defense. They can match on s0 and separate on s1 because support changes their actions differently. Erastin is a possible defense-perturbing challenge, not automatically a valid load reference. Other alternatives are support-dependent uptake, state distributions, drug-triggered NRF2, timing/cycling and assay metabolism.

Strong rivals: direct assays of target/support entries; ordinary flexible joint drug/dose/history/context response; shared-potency/monotone calibration with exactly the same observations; controlled ecological/PK–PD/state models where relevant. Compare all paid calibration, controls, repeats, timing and failed wells. A prospective acquisition-policy advantage needs its own matched-budget evidence; it does not follow from adding an Agent. No argument here depends on absolute non-expressibility by a Bayesian model.
