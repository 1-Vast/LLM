## Actionable findings

### 1. Qualify when a response-sensitivity bound is required

Location: “Frozen relation, prediction and boundary,” paragraph beginning “A reference drug measured on both supports…”

Current wording: “Finite noisy matching needs propagated calibration uncertainty and an estimated regularity/response-sensitivity bound: a small old discrepancy alone does not bound the new discrepancy.”

The final clause is correct. The preceding “needs … and … bound” is too categorical. A quantitative sensitivity bound is one way to convert approximate old matching into a guarantee on transported discrepancy. It is not a universal prerequisite if a qualified calibration directly supplies an uncertainty-aware image of the old-response matching interval, or if the cross-drug prediction is assessed with the calibration and matching uncertainty explicitly included. Those intervals may be too wide to make a decision, but that yields an inconclusive prediction rather than mathematical inadmissibility for lack of a separate smoothness bound.

Suggested replacement: “Finite noisy matching requires uncertainty-aware transport of the old matching interval through the calibrated map. A small old discrepancy alone does not bound the new discrepancy; a quantitative closeness guarantee needs a justified response-sensitivity bound or an equivalent empirically calibrated interval.”

This sharpens the report's distinction between a scientifically meaningful test and a test that is sufficiently precise to support an advance/reject decision. It does not remove any current biological or resource qualification gap.

### 2. Restore the developer's explicit amortization boundary

Location: practical-value paragraph following the frozen relation; relevant owning text: `mechanism/candidate_v1.md`, “Agent decision and strongest ordinary rivals.”

The report correctly counts calibration/control/repeat cost and requires direct-assay rivals. It omits the developer's particularly useful warning that, for one new drug at one dose, direct measurement can be at least as natural as purchasing a reference curve. Any plausible saving depends on multiple target decisions reusing an already qualified family/context map, or on a concrete separately demonstrated cost advantage. The opening “one reference curve” use case can otherwise sound cheaper before the number of decisions is stated.

Suggested addition: “For a single target/dose decision, purchasing a reference curve may cost more than direct testing; possible savings depend on amortizing a qualified map over multiple target decisions.”

This is a practical clarification, not evidence against the relation and not a demand to invent a cost model or run an experiment in this continuation.
