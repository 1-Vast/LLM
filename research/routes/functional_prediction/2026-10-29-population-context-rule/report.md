## Frozen relation, prediction and boundary

The [developer's frozen object](mechanism/candidate_v1.md) proposes an enabling model

`y(d,c,s)=F(q_d(c)-a_s)`.

Here y increases with functional injury, d/c denote drug/dose, s is controlled support, q is a support-invariant injury and a is a drug-invariant protective margin. F is one response/susceptibility function for the fixed population and interval. These are substantive assumptions, not definitions inferred from one drug's response.

On an injective old-response range, equal old effects imply equal q. Therefore the drugs remain equal on s1, with common map

`T_1<-0(u)=F(F^{-1}(u)+a_0-a_1)`.

A reference drug measured on both supports could estimate T only over the nonsaturated old-response range overlapping the targets. Different slopes and potencies are allowed. Old plateau equality does not identify injury; a new plateau can preserve equality while preventing reverse inference. Finite noisy matching requires uncertainty-aware transport of the old matching interval through the calibrated map. A small old discrepancy alone does not bound the new discrepancy; a quantitative closeness guarantee needs a justified response-sensitivity bound or an equivalent empirically calibrated interval. A wide interval can be scientifically valid but inconclusive for a decision.

The analytic enabling case is two different dose-to-injury functions acting on common thresholds while support only changes protection. A meaningful failure is an injury-generating drug versus a defense-blocking drug: they may match on s0 and separate on s1. Support-dependent uptake, cycling, state distributions, drug-triggered defense and observation metabolism are other rivals. These are thought constructions/conditions, not simulations, observed effects or newly discovered laws.

The scientific prediction is **cross-drug equality on new support after independent old-effect matching**. A successful fit of one reference curve or composition of invertible maps is not that test. Even a validated observable T would not uniquely identify F/q/a or establish antioxidant-margin causality. A biological contribution remains possible if a useful independently specified family obeys the relation; ordinary monotone calibration can implement it. The shared-map idea and its implementation are not new algorithms here.

Practical value would be an advance/reject decision on an unmeasured target/support entry, with a fixed hit threshold and total calibration/control/repeat cost. For a single target/dose decision, purchasing a reference curve may cost more than direct testing; possible savings depend on amortizing a qualified map over multiple target decisions, or on another demonstrated cost advantage. Mandatory rivals include direct assays, ordinary shared-map calibration and flexible joint drug/dose/context/history models with equal information and budgets. Agent acquisition or policy-learning advantage requires a separate comparison. One fixed background does not establish unseen-background transfer or the full requested co-learning capability.
