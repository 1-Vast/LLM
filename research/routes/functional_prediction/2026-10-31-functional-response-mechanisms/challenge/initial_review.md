## Claim A: derivation, exact restrictions and escape routes

Let Omega be the connected open domain of F in R^2. For arbitrary p=(x,y), q=(u,v) in Omega, openness supplies a neighborhood of zero in which both p+h and q+h remain in Omega. If the stated contrast invariance holds for all such independent h=(a,b), differentiating at h=0 gives grad F(p)=grad F(q). Thus grad F is a single constant vector and F-alpha*x-beta*y is constant on connected Omega. Continuous differentiability is unnecessary: differentiability at each point suffices. Connectedness joins local constants; independent two-dimensional shifts give both directional constraints. In fact the all-pairs assumption itself forces the same slope even across components, while component-specific intercepts could remain without connectedness. The frozen sufficient conditions are sound, if stronger than minimally necessary.

This conclusion is specific to one shared, pointwise F of two scalar uncalibrated input folds and invariance of its between-line output contrasts under independently variable condition offsets. It does not identify alpha,beta from the invariance condition. To get alpha=beta=1,gamma=0 requires additional justified identities such as F(x,0)=x and F(0,y)=y over suitable nontrivial axis ranges; a measured zero response is not automatically a no-exposure identity. It does not force an actual combination response to be additive or reveal its missing condition-specific output scale.

Independent offsets are consequential. Under only a common one-dimensional shift a=b, F(x,y)=c*(x+y)+phi(x-y) obeys contrast invariance for arbitrary differentiable nonlinear phi. More generally unconstrained directions permit nonlinear behavior. A finite observed support without a neighborhood of shifts cannot support the derivative theorem. Dependence on known drug/dose labels, external anchors, full cross-line contrast vectors or measured states changes the admissible function class and permits nonlinear invariant predictions. For example a nonlinear function of the two already computed line contrasts is offset-invariant. Calibration of each input condition resolves the particular input-gauge ambiguity, not the dynamic question or missing combination calibration. These escapes should remain visible in any report.

## Claim B: an explicit nonnegative, pooled-observable witness

Write x(t)=integral(u), y(t)=integral(v). From zero initial state,

z(T)=a*integral_0^T y(t)u(t)dt+b*integral_0^T x(t)v(t)dt.

For constant u,v this is (a+b)*u*v*T^2/2, while singles give zero. Therefore any amount of exact dose/time data generated by constant controls from this same initial state identifies at most a+b in this family. It does not identify a-b. The same is true for proportional controls u(t)=U*w(t),v(t)=V*w(t): both cross integrals equal U*V/2 when integral(w)=1. Conversely, varying relative timing can change these integrals and supply the missing direction.

For A only then B only, the first cross integral is zero and the second is U*V; for reverse order they exchange. Thus z_AB=b*U*V and z_BA=a*U*V. The frozen two worlds with nonnegative (a,b)=(0,2) versus (2,0) in c and (1,1) in d give equal complete concurrent states and equal positive absolute folds g=2^(-z), hence equal pooled measurements under the same recovery mapping. At U=V=1 the AB log-fold contrast is -1 versus +1 as claimed. The sign reversal persists for the invariant within-schedule contrast D and is not erased by an unknown common pool offset. If other lines exist, keep their dynamics and recovery identical in the two worlds. All folds remain in (0,1] for nonnegative controls/parameters. Equal-duration staging is possible; controls can be adjusted to keep total exposure and observation time matched.

The vector fields are f=(1,0,a*y),g=(0,1,b*x), with LaValle's bracket convention [f,g]=(0,0,b-a). The classical a=-1,b=1 integrator is prior art; the nonnegative family and fixed-sum derivation are an elementary witness, not a new control mechanism. No inverse/negative drug controls are required for the two forward-order endpoints, although the book's commutator-loop controllability demonstration uses inverse controls. One must not import that physical controllability interpretation into cells.

The fixed initial state and observation family are essential. At arbitrary known x0,y0, constant-control z' contains a*u*y0+b*v*x0, which can separate a and b. Sequentially resetting or knowing varied intermediate states is new information. Inside the stipulated two-parameter family, one nonzero ordered endpoint plus known a+b can identify the remaining coefficient if its absolute readout is calibrated; a single pooled contrast across lines identifies only the corresponding coefficient difference unless additional anchors/parameter restrictions are supplied. Ordered endpoints in general do not identify arbitrary vector fields, hidden state, or biological mediation.

This is a general identification counterexample for a richer authenticated constant-control design, not a model of the actual source. The summary lacks fields authenticating either constant/concurrent control or ordered control. Absence of order fields proves that this summary cannot authenticate order; it does not prove what the cultures actually received. Tuple order supplies no schedule evidence.

## Constructive measured-state law and closure counterexample

For q in (0,1), U_A(q)=q+theta*q*(1-q) stays in (0,1) for theta in [-1,1]: the endpoint maps are q^2 and 2*q-q^2. One exact interior pre/post transition identifies theta=(q_after-q_before)/(q_before*(1-q_before)) within this stipulated one-parameter map. This is algebraic identification under a correct specified map, not evidence of cheap biological sample complexity or of a shared theta across backgrounds. Noisy/near-boundary q can make estimation poor.

With ell_B(q)=-beta*(1-q), a matched conditional B contrast at two distinct known states identifies beta via Delta ell_B=beta*Delta q, provided these are genuinely comparable B-only increments after their corresponding A-only schedule controls. Pooled contrasts cancel a shared condition offset under stable recovery; they do not automatically cancel unmatched history/control offsets. A total AB endpoint cannot be silently substituted for the conditional B increment.

For two backgrounds, the predicted conditioned contrast is beta*(q_c-q_d)*[1+theta*(1-q_c-q_d)]. Thus equal initial differences can yield different conditioned contrasts when their sums differ and beta*theta is nonzero. This supports the toy possibility. It is fully representable by ordinary nonlinear regression given q and the same transition information. beta*theta is simply an interaction coefficient unless independent biological evidence gives it a mechanistic interpretation.

The hidden-state objection needs a carefully observationally equivalent construction. Merely adding rho*(r_c-r_d) could change observed training B challenges and cease to match the data. A valid alternative sets the hidden contribution to zero on all observed source transitions/challenges, while allowing it at a held-out background/history or subsequent action. For example r is an unmeasured persistent history marker, absent in every training history, that changes B susceptibility after a novel A history without changing measured q or its transitions. Two models then agree on every legal observation and disagree at the requested target. If the target challenge itself is observed with a correct contrast and controls, that particular endpoint ceases to be unobserved; ambiguity may remain elsewhere. This is the precise closure boundary, not a universal untestability claim.

A same-measured-q/different-history B challenge is a useful falsification attempt when q and B increments are measured precisely and timing, dose, washout/carryover, selection, viability and preparations are controlled. A difference refutes deterministic q-only sufficiency under those controls. Agreement at finitely many histories fails to prove closure everywhere. For stochastic biology, equality must concern the relevant conditional response distribution, not just means. A population mean q need not close dynamics because mixture distributions or selection can differ at equal means. Manipulating q independently of A/history would strengthen a mediation claim; observational prediction success by itself does not establish q as causal mediator.
