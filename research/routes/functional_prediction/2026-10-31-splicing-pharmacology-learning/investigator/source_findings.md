## Finding and practical question

The supported biological restriction is **conditional targetability**, not a motif-only target list: a recognizable A−1 bulge, sufficient U1–splice-site complementarity and a permissive cellular splicing complex jointly constrain compound responsiveness. Local pairing can be altered without altering the compound, changing target space. This is already the Lenkeit source's contribution. It does not establish a new MAESTRO mechanism.

The material unresolved practical question is whether an RNA-drug team can predict, at a declared exposure, **which desired and unintended exon responses change when the same human cell's regulatory background changes**, beyond ordinary baseline splicing, expression/coverage and dose-response effects. A useful output would distinguish conserved response, loss of attainable response, a concentration shift and newly unmasked exon response, with explicit unmeasured-condition uncertainty. Such prediction could guide selectivity experiments; clinical benefit, killing and useful therapeutic co-perturbation are not established.

A directly relevant 2026 original found in the bounded live check already measures U1C knockdown × branaplam/risdiplam dose response. It contains both general loss of responsiveness and exceptions becoming drug responsive only after U1C depletion. Therefore neither 'drug plus regulatory perturbation' nor 'regulatory state can unmask drug targets' is an unmet observation or novel principle. What remains open here is a *qualified predictive/identification problem*, not the existence of that interaction.

## Alternatives, ordinary equivalent and distinguishing evidence

1. **Sequence-complementarity sufficiency:** predict response directly from local U1 sequence/pairing and fixed dose. Already P001's framework. Strong contrary source is P002's U1C/Helix-H dependence and conditional effects. A new sequence encoder is an implementation variant unless it establishes useful unmeasured-condition capability beyond that equivalent.
2. **Baseline/state geometry:** a regulatory perturbation shifts basal inclusion, and a shared drug effect on inclusion odds produces different raw PSI changes. Strong ordinary rival: baseline-conditioned ordinary regression or P003's context-strength/dose-response model, with the same sequence, expression and measured source-response inputs. This must be compared before calling changes a new conditional drug mechanism.
3. **Genuine context-dependent drug action:** U1 perturbation changes an attainable response or concentration dependence after accounting for baseline and support. P002 reports such source observations. A learned general rule requires held-out exon/gene backgrounds and direct combination measurements; retrospective recovery of source-selected classes is not independent mechanism discovery.
4. **Measurement/ascertainment:** expression, decay, coverage, selection on treated discovery, absent rows and shared DMSO error can imitate context selectivity. Matched support and independent predictor-baseline/label-baseline replicates help, but cannot restore an unknown universe or prove error independence.

The smallest meaningful distinguishing observation is matched drug/DMSO measurements in **both** regulatory backgrounds over a predeclared exposure series, with an independently fixed reference/control-supported event set and per-sample inclusion/exclusion counts. Fit baseline/context-strength and equally informed flexible ordinary response alternatives on the same observations; hold whole genes out when the task is a new gene and hold the intended condition out when that is the claim. Test whether regulatory context predicts a reproducible response-shape or maximal-response change beyond baseline alone, while explicitly permitting 'only a baseline shift' or inconclusive results. A single endpoint and thresholded yes/no inducibility cannot separate these mechanisms. Actual rate claims require time-resolved or direct binding/residence measurements, not only endpoint RNA-seq/EC50.
