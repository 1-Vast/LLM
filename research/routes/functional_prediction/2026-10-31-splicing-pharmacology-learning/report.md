## Why this entrance matters

An RNA-drug discovery team needs to anticipate desired and unintended exon modulation as cellular regulatory conditions change. Current alternatives include direct selectivity measurements, local-sequence/baseline prediction and empirical dose-response models. A useful conditional rule could reduce measurements for new combinations of previously measured choices, but the proposed rule still requires source vehicle/drug and target vehicle measurements. It is not sequence-only prediction, unseen-compound prediction or a free baseline readout. Any eventual savings and experimental value require comparison at equal information and measurement cost. Splicing is a molecular functional endpoint, not evidence of killing or clinical efficacy.

## Constructive prediction, equivalence and failure condition

For a fixed supported choice set, let p/p' be source untreated/treated distributions and q the target untreated distribution. If relative drug multipliers remain invariant, then

`q'_j = (p'_j/p_j) q_j / sum_k[(p'_k/p_k) q_k]`.

With one drug-responsive choice T and equal reference multipliers for alternatives A/B, the rule predicts equal proportional change of A/B and preservation of their ratio. Different baseline T can predict different suppression of A despite equal baseline A. This is the extra joint prediction preserved by the development analysis. It is a symbolic construction, not a simulation or observation.

Suppression here means relative usage within the declared choice set. It does not identify absolute transcript abundance, whole-transcript isoform frequency or total exon inclusion outside that conditional denominator. Those outputs would require additional observation assumptions or measurements.

With two actual choices, the rule is exactly a context-strength/log-odds shift. Aggregating several alternatives preserves that shift when those alternatives share a common drug multiplier; differing multipliers can make the effective reference change with their composition. For several choices the full rule is a standard shared multinomial model or density-ratio reweighting. Any positive source pair admits fitted multipliers; source fit has no validation force. For any positive target treated vector, freely changing target multipliers can reproduce it. The restriction only has predictive content if the same relative factors are transported without target-response adjustment.

U1C depletion can change a target's drug-compatible complex directly, providing a substantive reason to challenge presumed invariance. Another choice may acquire direct drug responsiveness. Different RNA survival, transcript production or observation/denominator composition can also alter measured mature-RNA proportions. Untreated frequencies alone cannot distinguish these cases. Unsupported source probabilities leave multipliers unidentified; zero fill or arbitrary pseudocounts do not repair missing identification.

The closest fair rivals are the source-owned isolated-event model, its exact shared-multinomial equivalent and a flexible ordinary predictor receiving the same **entire choice-set information** and source responses. A gain against a predictor that sees only one exon's baseline would not establish a new biological law. An ordinary implementation could still support a meaningful biological result, but no applicable intervention class or successful withheld prediction is established here.

The exact shared multinomial implementation makes the same point predictions and is not a comparator the formula should outperform. Fresh review also separates the ordinal scalar null from the vector restriction: either can fail without providing the other's test. Changing competitor composition can alter an aggregated margin even when branch-relative drug factors remain fixed. No ordinal reversal or vector-transport failure was measured here.
