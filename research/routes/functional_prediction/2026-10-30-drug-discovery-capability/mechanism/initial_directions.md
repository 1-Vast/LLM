# Initial capability hypotheses, before preferred-candidate review

No new source acquisition, model call, dataset/outcome access, experiment or code execution informs these hypotheses.

The objective is a useful drug-discovery capability combining a virtual cell, an Agent and cheap accessible data. An ordinary mechanistic model can be adequate if the scientific relation is consequential. A virtual cell here predicts a functional consequence of a drug intervention in a represented cell/background. An Agent changes an assay, compound, combination or model assumption after observing evidence. Neither label establishes a contribution or an advantage.

## Independent openings

These are alternative scientific objects, not six modules for one system. Resource examples are **eligibility questions**, not claims that suitable public data exist.

| Lead | Question and proposed capability | Operative hypothesis and virtual cell | Agent's substantive decision | Critical unknown and strongest simple rival |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1. Transferable reversal of acquired resistance | Can distinct initiating resistance lesions be grouped by the intervention that restores drug sensitivity, so a screen identifies one rescue strategy across resistant backgrounds? | A resistance state is described by a causal bypass/dependency and the dose response to the original therapy; a targeted gene or drug intervention changes that state and predicts restored functional response. A shared causal bypass may imply transferable rescue even when baseline molecular similarity does not. | Observe resistance and a small set of mechanistically distinct perturbation responses; revise the proposed bypass and select a rescue intervention or reject transfer to the new background. | Requires matched parental/resistant backgrounds and measured rescue outcomes, preferably with different initiating lesions. A genotype/pathway rule, direct rescue screen, or ordinary hierarchical interaction model could suffice. This is not yet distinguishable from established functional precision oncology or synthetic-lethal screening. |
| D2. Recoverable suppression versus durable loss | Can a cheap early observation distinguish a compound that merely pauses cells from one that eliminates the capacity to repopulate, sufficiently to choose follow-up duration or regimen? | The virtual cell tracks viable proliferating, reversibly arrested and nonrecovering populations. A drug changes transition/birth/death rates; a washout or delayed observation reveals whether short-term suppression persists. | After an early response, choose the next observation time or washout challenge; revise arrest versus durable-loss attribution and prioritize a regimen for durable control. | Aggregate counts generally cannot identify all transitions. Public matched exposure/washout/recovery series are essential. A directly measured recovery assay, GR/DIP analysis, or a simple growth-death model is a strong rival. The important claim would be a reproducible predictive relation to later recovery, not inventing a state model. |
| D3. Context-induced reversal of drug action | Can a screen determine when changing a second population or its abundance changes which therapy actually controls the target population? | The virtual cell represents the target population and a distinct interacting population, with drug effects on each and a limited interaction term. Context can change the functional consequence even at equal target-cell exposure. | Observe a drug response in one context; select a second abundance/composition or compartment-selective intervention that distinguishes direct target sensitivity from an interaction-mediated effect; revise the recommendation. | Needs cell-type-resolved outcomes, independently varied compositions and genuine intervention replication. A target-only monoculture screen, simple density correction, mixture model or directly measured coculture screen may explain the result. A useful relation must transfer across held-out drug/background families, not merely fit a few coculture examples. |
| D4. Assay-mediated false selectivity | Can a screening researcher determine when apparent cancer-selective efficacy is a change in the assay signal per cell, and recover a functional selectivity conclusion using a cheap orthogonal observation? | The virtual cell separates viable cell number/state from the observation equation of ATP, fluorescence or metabolic reporters. A drug can change both cell state and reporter yield. Paired imaging/count and reporter observations may identify when a rank reversal is biological or measurement-mediated. | On disagreement between predicted state and assay readout, acquire or consult an orthogonal count/fate measurement; revise the observation model and compound advancement decision. | Requires paired raw measurements on the same conditions and an independent functional endpoint. Direct cell counting and standard orthogonal hit confirmation are extremely strong rivals. A useful contribution would be a validated transport boundary or correction rule, not a generic call for orthogonal assays. |
| D5. Selective protection as a discovery target | Can a drug protect a normal cell state from a subsequent cytotoxic intervention while preserving tumor killing, and can the protection mechanism transfer to a new tumor/normal pair? | The virtual cell represents cycling/arrested state and susceptibility to a second drug. A first intervention may move normal cells into a reversible protected state while failing to protect a relevant tumor state. The output is a therapeutic-window change, not an ordinary tumor-only synergy score. | Observe selective arrest and damage/recovery; revise the protection hypothesis and choose pretreatment order, delay or a different protector. | Needs paired tumor/normal measurements, both treatment orders and recovery, with independent pairs. Existing cyclotherapy, schedule optimization and direct dose/schedule screens may already own the key relation. A cheap dataset with all required outcomes may be scarce. |
| D6. Drug mechanism that survives a changed cellular background | Can one infer which part of a compound's functional effect is mediated by the intended target and which part cannot transfer with that target dependence, improving target or lead selection? | The virtual cell includes a target/pathway dependency state and a drug intervention. Matched genetic target perturbation and multiple chemically distinct inhibitors separate a target-mediated response from compound-specific action, under explicit genetic-pharmacological mismatch assumptions. | Observe disagreement between genetic and chemical responses; revise the mechanism or select a chemically distinct perturbation that separates target dependence from an off-target explanation. | Requires independently controlled target perturbations, actual compound responses and multiple chemotypes at meaningful exposures. Target triangulation, chemical-genetic epistasis and a simple causal interaction model are close rivals. Genetic loss and acute inhibition are not equivalent; target rescue or resistant alleles may be necessary. |

## Preliminary reasoning, not a selection result

D1 and D6 ask for a mechanistic relation that could change which rescue or lead is pursued. They are attractive only if their matching measurements are actually available; joining unrelated databases does not create matched intervention evidence. D3 changes the unit from a cell line alone to interacting populations and could expose a useful context boundary, but has a serious replication burden. D2 and D5 concern a consequential functional outcome absent from a static viability score; their temporal and recovery data requirements may dominate feasibility. D4 may be the cheapest analytical correction, but ordinary orthogonal confirmation could remove both its claimed novelty and any Agent cost advantage.

No winner is selected at this stage. The next archive read will ask whether these mechanisms repeat an already failed premise, whether a materially new data contract reopens one, and whether an archived negative suggests a different useful object. Subsequent factual source packets must be distinguished from these untested initial hypotheses.

## Shared comparison and exposure contract

For any developed line, record the represented state, intervention, outcome and exact screening decision; the observation that changes the Agent's action; the strongest simpler model/policy and a direct-assay rival; and a prediction they do not all make. Charge all arms for initialization, calibration, failed/orthogonal assays, elapsed biological time and outcome feedback, with the same available observations at each decision. Hold out the biological or mechanistic family required by the claim. A new random split of familiar labels or a second database with shared experimental origins is not independent transfer. Inspect resource metadata first; do not open protected or confirmation outcomes. A standalone scientific/data-analysis finding and a demonstrated benefit of adaptive Agent action remain separate claims.
