One narrower question remains distinctly testable from the inspected author claims: **does a resistance state change only the single-drug dose-response coordinates, or also the functional relation governing their combination?** In particular, after matching each component's single-agent effect between parental and resistant HPAC, does the parental daraxonrasib–zoldonrasib response surface predict the resistant pair response? This is an empirical restriction about portability of a combination relationship, not a new response-surface algorithm. It is not yet known to hold, to be novel, or to produce useful screening savings.

The new opening is the source-owned **parent/resistant joint-intervention matrix**, which changes an observation prerequisite; it is not a fresh algorithm merely because the map now has two dose coordinates. ## The restriction and the useful decision

**Research user and output.** A screening team has an existing parental combination matrix and obtains a resistant derivative. It must decide whether to reuse that matrix after calibrating the two new single-agent curves, or measure the resistant combination anew. The potentially useful output is a restricted response relation that predicts which resistant dose pairs should advance, plus an empirical boundary beyond which that reuse fails. A separate retrospective use is to determine whether a reported reduction in combination benefit remains after differences in the component dose-response curves are accounted for.

**Virtual-cell state, intervention and outcome.** The minimal functional virtual cell represents the specified parental/resistant state and treatment history, each component's single-agent response, and the pair-response relation. Intervention is a specified simultaneous daraxonrasib×zoldonrasib dose pair for the five-day HPAC assay. Outcome is the **same source-qualified CTG response estimand** in both states, with its baseline/control normalization retained. It is not automatically viable-cell number, survival probability, durable clearance or a clinical outcome. A molecular state interpretation would require additional evidence. Calling this a virtual cell does not make its ordinary response model a research contribution; the proposed transferable relation is the contribution to investigate.

Let `Y_b(d,a)` denote that response for background/state `b=P,R`; larger values conventionally indicate more remaining endpoint signal. Define the single-agent curves `m_b(d)=Y_b(d,0)` and `n_b(a)=Y_b(0,a)`. On predeclared monotone, nonsaturated ranges with overlap between states, define

`H_b(u,v) = Y_b(m_b^(-1)(u), n_b^(-1)(v))`.

The proposed restriction is

`H_R(u,v) = H_P(u,v)`

over a specified common effect domain, within a predeclared practical tolerance and propagated calibration/replicate uncertainty. Equivalently, use the two single-agent curves to map a resistant dose pair to parental doses with matched component effects, then predict with the parental combination surface. A new variable name does not prove invariance: each state's surface can always be re-expressed this way when inversion is valid; **equality between states is the falsifiable content**.

For finite doses and noisy data, inverse points can be intervals or nonunique. Plateaus and nonmonotonicity do not permit arbitrary branch selection. No extrapolation beyond supported parental and resistant ranges is licensed. If those intervals are too broad to change the screening decision, the prediction is inconclusive. A pointwise comparison that ignores the error from fitting both single-agent curves is inadequate.

**Why the restriction might hold.** A sufficient mechanistic picture is that resistance changes each drug's effective dose/access coordinate while leaving a shared response function and joint-action relation unchanged. More explicitly, `Y_b(d,a)=F(K(q_b,D(d),q_b,A(a)))`, with the same `F` and `K` in both states, `K(x,0)=x`, `K(0,y)=y`, and injective `F` on the relevant range. Matching single effects then matches the effective coordinates and predicts matched combinations. These are load-bearing biological assumptions, not a consequence of observing KRAS amplification. An ordinary joint model can implement them.

**Where it breaks.** KRAS amplification may change mutant/WT contributions, feedback and downstream dependence; the additional MAP2K1/PIK3CA mutations in HPAC may also matter. A partner can alter the other drug's uptake, target availability or payload delivery. Hidden population composition can change joint susceptibility while preserving each marginal response, as the archived common-screen co-learning analysis already establishes. These give coherent failures of the observable restriction. Importantly, failure does **not uniquely prove molecular rewiring**: unchanged biochemical interaction combined with a changed cellular response function or assay behavior can also break it. Conversely, equality does not identify a particular molecular mechanism.

This question has a clearer source foothold than the original broad rescue-equivalence claim because the required **joint** responses are actually described in the same parental/resistant assay. It does not inherit empirical support from the source merely because those matrices exist.

## What an Agent would actually do

1. Observe the resistant single-agent calibration curves and propose predictions from the already measured parental surface within their qualified overlap.
2. Select a small number of real combination measurements capable of changing the advance/reject decision or rejecting portability; retain all calibration, control and failed-assay costs.
3. If an observed discrepancy exceeds the predeclared joint uncertainty/tolerance, withdraw that pair/state's reuse claim and collect a specifically needed resistant response. Otherwise retain only the tested domain and uncertainty, without declaring universal transfer.

The revision is of a biological/functional restriction, not merely an LLM explanation. However, an ordinary sequential design program can perform the same steps. **No distinctive Agent advantage is presently established.** A fixed diagnostic combination panel and a standard adaptive design policy must be compared on exactly the same observations, timings and costs. Allowing the Agent to fit a free resistant surface after seeing every combination would erase the proposed prediction and make the feedback loop vacuous.

For one requested combination, directly measuring it can be cheaper than calibrating two single-agent curves. A possible benefit requires amortization across enough genuine decisions, previously available parental data, or a demonstrated timing advantage; none is assumed here. Reusing published animal data can be computationally cheap, while regenerating the corresponding evidence is not. A free table and a cheap deployment are different claims.

## Strong ordinary rivals and the observation that distinguishes them

- **Author-informed cause rule:** KRAS/RAS hyperactivation motivates a multi-selective plus mutant-selective RAS pair; DDR dependence motivates a DDR inhibitor; RTK activity or ADC delivery motivates the corresponding partner. It receives the same molecular information and history. Reproducing those choices is not a new discovery.
- **Single-agent-only response predictions:** HSA, Bliss, Loewe or appropriate related dose-equivalence rules where their response-scale assumptions hold, plus a flexible ordinary model using the same single-agent curves. A survival-probability formula must not be silently applied to a signed baseline-corrected growth signal. These models may make the parent matrix unnecessary.
- **Ordinary response-surface transfer:** shared or state-specific interaction parameters in a hierarchical surface model, including potency/efficacy/interaction decompositions such as the MuSyC family. Exact closest-prior equivalence remains unsearched here. A simple interpolated parental `H` is itself an ordinary baseline, not a new algorithm.
- **Direct measurement and ordinary policy:** a prespecified small resistant combination panel, or the actual desired pair measured directly, with the same calibration, repeats, elapsed biological time and historical labels charged. A standard sequential uncertainty/design policy gets the same opportunity to revise.

The **biological prediction** is equality of parental/resistant combination responses after independent matching of the two component effects, not equal behavior at the same nominal concentrations. The **predictive-capability claim** is that the parental pair surface adds useful withheld resistant-combination information beyond the strongest single-agent-only rule. If the inherited surface is indistinguishable from an ordinary additive/null surface, its acquisition may buy no additional predictive content. The **Agent claim** is separate: adaptive decisions must beat fixed or ordinary adaptive alternatives at equal total costs. A negative result at one level does not automatically refute the others.

A surviving relation could be scientifically useful even when an established model implements it. Conversely, simply rejecting an invented invariance null would not by itself deliver a useful drug-discovery capability. Do not spend the remaining experiment allowance just to obtain that rejection. The follow-up must have a real reuse/decision objective, informative overlap and strong rivals fixed in advance.

## Disposition

Retain **conditional reuse of a paired combination relationship after single-agent recalibration** as the useful source-led opening. It is more specific than the original generic rescue sentence and can be investigated using irregular but real matched observations. The source already owns the biological combination rationale; no new cause-specific therapy is nominated. Metadata qualification, a decision-relevant distinction from ordinary response-surface rules, and fresh independent challenge must precede any test admission. No positive result, novel method, Agent benefit or ready cheap benchmark is claimed.
