## What is being assumed and tested

The scientific content is equality of the two effect-coordinate surfaces on a qualified common domain, within a prespecified decision-relevant error tolerance. Merely constructing each H is an identity and supplies no evidence of portability. The equality is a restriction on all included resistant joint outcomes and can fail while both single-agent curves remain perfectly predictable.

For the actual prediction at a resistant target dose pair `(d,a)`, one can use `u=m_R(d)`, `v=n_R(a)`, then map to parental doses `m_P^{-1}(u)`, `n_P^{-1}(v)` and read the parental combination surface. This needs reliable parental inverses and resistant forward curves at the targets; it need not numerically invert the resistant curves merely to generate predictions. The formal two-surface comparison still needs well-defined coordinates. Small slopes, saturation and finite grid interpolation can destroy accuracy even on formally monotone ranges, so overlap must be defined with uncertainty and interpolation support, not just matching nominal minimum/maximum effects.

The hypothetical common-F/common-K construction is sufficient only with the intended zero-dose latent anchors, compatible baselines, accessible common ranges and the stated injectivity/axis conditions. It is not evidence that those conditions hold in HPAC. Nor does scalar effect matching identify equal target occupancy, signaling, growth state, cellular mixture or response time course. Five-day effects can match while the temporal or cellular composition of those effects differs. Conversely, such differences need not preclude useful local endpoint prediction.

The important practical assumptions are: qualified compound identity and exposure; the same justified endpoint definition across states; adequate single-agent and parental joint support; a fixed interpolation/calibration procedure; and assay uncertainty small enough to decide the intended action. Population mixtures, different untreated growth rates, altered drug-conditioned exposure and ATP per cell can all change the measured combination surface without proving a changed molecular interaction. Baseline normalization can also mask biologically different states. These are interpretation limits, not grounds for automatically discarding a predictive endpoint claim.

## Simplest counterexample and strongest rivals

On an illustrative viability scale with untreated reference 1, take `H_P(u,v)=uv` and `H_R(u,v)=uv+c*u*(1-u)*v*(1-v)` for a small nonzero c. Both surfaces have exactly the same two single-agent axes (`H(u,1)=u`, `H(1,v)=v`) but different interior outcomes. Small c can preserve smoothness, monotonicity and bounded responses on this illustrative domain. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a fit or scientific test and not an assertion that the source endpoint is a probability. More generally a state-specific interior perturbation vanishing on the axes remains invisible to all single-agent measurements. KRAS copy-number knowledge cannot identify that perturbation by itself.

Ordinary rivals must receive the same information and uncertainty treatment:

- Single-agent-only prediction rules, including appropriate null models on their explicitly justified response scales. A retained parental surface is useful only if it adds something beyond what the axes already predict.
- Deterministic parental-surface interpolation after component-effect matching. This is the direct implementation of the frozen restriction; calling an Agent around it is not an additional scientific model.
- Shared-interaction and state-specific/hierarchical parametric surfaces, including MuSyC-type decompositions where applicable. A shared model expressed in the same coordinates may be the very same restriction, not an independent rival. A finite-parameter potency-shift model is not automatically identical to arbitrary empirical monotone remapping; exact equivalence must be checked rather than assumed.
- Author-informed DDR-dominance/cause rules, an ordinary fixed diagnostic panel, ordinary adaptive design, and direct assay of the desired resistant combination.

The published DDR direction gives a plausible challenge to portability, but it cannot be counted as an unseen discovery. It also cannot settle portability without effect-matched comparison. Same-pathway RAS doublets may be particularly close to ordinary dose-equivalence explanations. Neither proximity to known mathematics nor expressibility by algebra suffices to reject an empirically useful conditional restriction; it does limit algorithmic novelty claims.

## Practical decision and four-core fit

For one desired resistant pair, measuring it directly can dominate calibrating full curves and diagnostics. Reuse becomes plausible when a parental matrix already exists and several resistant combination decisions share the calibration burden. The relevant endpoint is useful resistant prediction or decision error at total cost, including repeat/failed assays, calibration, diagnostic combinations, historical matrix cost under a declared amortization convention and biological waiting time. A retrospective masked grid cannot demonstrate real laboratory savings by itself.

The functional predictor is a narrow virtual cell: a state-conditioned assay-response emulator. An Agent could select informative resistant entries, update confidence in reuse and abstain when uncertainty is too large. That satisfies a plausible system role, but agentic reasoning is unnecessary to test the scientific restriction. Agent value requires a separate equal-budget comparison to fixed and ordinary adaptive policies over enough decisions to be informative. This HPAC example alone cannot establish a general Agent advantage or broad drug-discovery capability.

Cheap accessible data is currently an unverified opportunity. A public-labelled source ZIP and existing experiment do not ensure raw wells, independent validation or inexpensive future biological work. If useful coverage and metadata exist, a local paired-state reanalysis can still be worthwhile without a global factorial or a clean causal intervention. Broad resistance-cause/background transfer needs additional genuinely relevant state pairs.

## Data admission, independence and exposure contract

Before any outcome test, establish the archive manifest and mapping to exact panels, drug labels, doses/units, states, time points and endpoint fields. Verify whether zero-dose axes, DMSO/T0/T5 controls, technical wells, experiment IDs, plates/batches and both experiments are present. Determine whether single-agent curves are the same matrix axes or separate plates. Do not assume a displayed curve and matrix supply independent measurements, or that every paired parental/resistant experiment was run together. An averaged figure table may support a descriptive local comparison but not all uncertainty or independence claims.

Declare the estimand from actual metadata; if normalization cannot be resolved, report precisely the deposited endpoint and narrow the claim. Do not algebraically “repair” ambiguous printed normalization without source justification. If only means/SD are deposited, do not reconstruct fictitious raw wells. Technical duplicates are not biological-state replicates. Two experiments, if actually available, remain two experiments rather than dozens of independent dose-level samples.

Freeze the prediction procedure, admissible effect/dose domain, error tolerance, comparator set and allocation rule before revealing held-out resistant interior outcomes. Account for uncertainty in both axes and parental interpolation. Resistant diagnostic entries consumed by the policy are training/feedback, not evaluation. Exclude or separately report calibration axes and diagnostics from final predictive error. If both experiments are available, their identities provide a stronger separation than random wells; their independence and intended pairing still need source verification. Only held-out combinations from this known pair would be local outcome evaluation, not external biological transfer.

The article directions and this hypothesis are already exposed. A later holdout can protect numerical outcome evaluation from further tuning, but cannot make this an outcome-naive biological hypothesis or erase article-guided selection. More than one drug pair in the same HPAC derivative is not independent background/cause validation. If extensive exposure has already occurred elsewhere in the project, label the reanalysis exploratory and use a genuinely unexposed future target for stronger confirmation.
