## Findings first

No inspected donor currently justifies adopting a new biological prediction algorithm. The useful contribution is a stricter scientific relation/test design and explicit refusal conditions. This is not evidence of gain over ordinary regression, biological transport, or novelty.

### 1. A useful candidate principle is a *shared intervention relation tested across held-out combinations*

The most useful candidate relation to test is of the form

`response/ranking = R(perturbation role, context role, dose/protocol role)`

where a perturbation or dose is held out in combination with a context, while its role-bearing constituents have support elsewhere. The source analogues say the relation should be judged on novel compositions rather than repeated token identity or average importance. This becomes meaningful only if the role assignment is scientifically admissible; arbitrary gene/drug grouping would make the transfer vacuous.

This notation alone is ordinary conditional prediction. A single neural network can still implement a different rule for every identifier. Neither calling the predictor “shared” nor attaching a factorization makes it a distinct scientific relation or a new method. A defensible next hypothesis needs a specified restriction (which intervention/context changes preserve which output relation), an observational distinction from the direct-regression null, and held-out combinations that challenge that restriction. No inspected donor supplies those target-specific restrictions automatically.

**Source-to-target mapping.** In COGS, a primitive lexical item and a grammatical role are separately familiar but their combination is held out. Target variables are perturbation identity/role and cell-background/assay role; the held-out unit is their combination, optionally including dose/protocol. The target output is an endpoint contrast or intervention ranking. In CompSub, a semantically self-contained component is exchanged only for another component with the same equivalence class and aligned output; target substitution is allowed only when a gene/drug or protocol component has a predeclared functional role and the measurement meaning is preserved.

**Source evidence.** COGS (Kim & Linzen, EMNLP 2020, DOI `10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.731`, https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.731/ and https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.731.pdf) was read at P level: pp. 1, 3–4. The abstract reports 96–99% in-distribution accuracy versus 16–35% compositional generalization and ±6–8% seed sensitivity. Sections 3.1–3.4 define novel combinations of familiar primitives and roles, deeper recursion and alternations; Table 1 gives explicit train/generalization gaps. This is a benchmark design result, not a biological result.

CompSub (Li et al., arXiv:2502.20834v1, 2025-02-28, https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20834v1) was read at P level: §III-A “Component Substitution”, §IV “Theoretical Insights”, Appendix A-C. Its method requires span alignment, a semantically self-contained/unitary component, an inferred equivalence class, and replacement only when the classes match. §IV states an “unbiased on average” transform assumption and derives a regularization effect under full/valid substitutions; its bound contains a Wasserstein gap between augmented and compositional test distributions. Those assumptions are much stronger than repeated perturbation IDs and do not establish a biological role taxonomy.

**Failure condition.** A gene/drug may alter the biological mechanism itself, not merely fill a role; assay protocol can change the measured quantity; dose may be a continuous effect modifier. If there is no independent evidence for role equivalence, no paired reference, or no support for each constituent, substitution is invalid. COGS also demonstrates that high in-distribution performance can coexist with poor compositional transfer; CompSub's guarantee depends on valid alignments and a small distribution gap.

**Discriminating prediction.** A genuine transferable rule should predict a held-out perturbation × background (and, where claimed, dose/protocol) combination after removing repeated identifier shortcuts. It should retain response-direction/ranking quality when study/batch is held out and should degrade sharply when role-equivalence or constituent support is withheld. A global importance/direct-regression baseline may remain strong on repeated IDs but should fail on this split. The comparison must include an identifier-unseen split; otherwise repeated genes/drugs can masquerade as compositional transfer.

**Concrete future-design implication.** Add precision to the initial note's requirement to “require a split that prevents repeated identifiers from explaining the relation”: (i) combinations held out while both constituents are seen in other contexts; (ii) perturbation-identifier-unseen and background/study-unseen stress splits; (iii) independent preparation/study holdout where available; (iv) compare to global importance/direct regression. Define the admissible information before fitting: baseline context and pre-treatment metadata only, with no post-perturbation outcome leakage. If the data cannot support these splits or role evidence, the relation is parked and the outcome is “no viable transfer.” This elaborates the initial boundary; it is not a demonstrated improvement.

### 2. Selective invariance is conditional on an intervention-preserved distinction

The source principle is not “make contexts invariant.” It is “remove variation only when an intervention says it is nuisance and preserve separation when alternative actions produce different outcomes.”

**Source-to-target mapping.** Background/assay appearance -> cell-background, plate, study or protocol nuisance; action branch -> gene/drug/dose intervention; divergent outcome -> a biologically meaningful response difference. The target representation may suppress measured batch/style variation only after a design or control establishes it should not affect the estimand.

**Source evidence (complete abstract only, A).** ISI-WM, “Interventional Selective Invariance for World Models,” OpenReview note `IemjZHggCA`, ICLR 2027 submission (under review), search metadata at https://api2.openreview.net/notes/search?term=selective%20invariance&limit=12 and landing page https://openreview.net/forum?id=IemjZHggCA. The abstract says a background intervention aligns latent encodings for the same physical state under different backgrounds, while an action intervention separates/ranks only when simulator outcomes diverge; it reports an unseen-background protocol. The official PDF and note endpoint returned HTTP 403 in this scout, so the reading level remains A, not P/F. It is a donor hypothesis, not a validated target method.

**Failure condition.** A cell background, plate or protocol may genuinely modify response; no intervention proves it is nuisance. Endpoint screens do not have simulator counterfactual branches. If the same preparation cannot be measured under the proposed nuisance intervention, invariance is an untestable assertion.

**Discriminating prediction and decision.** A selective-invariance version should improve held-out study/batch prediction only for factors shown to preserve the chosen response contrast, while preserving known positive-control differences. Before any representation design, require a nuisance-preservation table: factor, admissible intervention/control, expected invariance target, and response feature that must remain separable. If this table cannot be populated from available controls, do not impose invariance; use explicit batch covariates or stop the transfer.

**CV counterevidence that refines the test.** Kempf et al., “When and How Does CLIP Enable Domain and Compositional Generalization?” (arXiv:2502.09507, first public 2025-02-13; inspected v3 updated 2025-09-12; https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09507v3; formal acceptance unverified), P reading of §§3,5,6.2, holds the evaluation class × domain set fixed while changing training exposure. §5 finds that partial exposure to test-domain classes can hurt unseen-class generalization; replacing those training classes with classes not queried at evaluation improves the reported setting. §6.2 also finds that better final embedding alignment does not by itself improve generalization. This changes the next comparison: do not assume that adding a few target-context anchors helps, and do not use embedding alignment as the decisive endpoint. Compare the same held-out interventions with no target-context anchors versus a label-balanced admissible anchor set. This is a proposed future comparison; the initial note contained no anchor policy, so it is not a reversal of an existing decision. Cell contexts are effect modifiers, unlike image rendering styles, so only the test-design principle transfers.

### 3. Causal/model-level transport gives a hard identifiability gate

TraCA's source result is useful as a negative guardrail. A separate unconstrained map can always match one source distribution to one target distribution, and separate kernels can match each intervention. Therefore, per-intervention fit or a post-hoc alignment score does not establish transport. A single relation constrained across interventions is the informative object.

**Source evidence.** “Generalised Transportability via Causal Abstractions,” arXiv:2608.15645v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15645v1 (stored locally as `reference/papers/arxiv-2608.15645/paper.html`; P reading of §§3.1–3.2, §8.4 and §11). §3.1 requires source/target SCMs to share endogenous variables, DAG and intervention semantics, differing only at a known shifted-node set; available information is source observational/interventional plus target observational data. §3.2 defines observational and per-intervention consistency and proves both are automatic because a constant Markov kernel maps any distribution to any other. Interventional consistency requires one common kernel for all interventions. §8.4 explicitly restricts the loss to designated intervention/output query marginals; §11 says ambiguity geometry and directional priors are declared assumptions, and observational radius calibration can fail.

**Target mismatch and failure condition.** The biological setting may lack paired controls, target observational baselines, independent preparations, intervention semantics, known shifted nodes, or overlap. Study/batch can shift the outcome mechanism itself. Under those conditions an apparent alignment is not an identified intervention effect or transport formula.

**Discriminating prediction.** If a common transport relation is real, one fixed relation (or fixed conditional rule) should predict held-out perturbations and held-out contexts jointly, with a prespecified estimand, while separately fitted per-intervention maps should not be accepted as evidence. The prediction must be evaluated on query-restricted outputs that correspond to the actual decision (for example, response direction or top-k intervention ranking), and uncertainty must widen or coverage fail when the declared shift/support radius is exceeded.

This is an analogy to TraCA's common kernel, not a claim that an arbitrary shared predictor satisfies interventional consistency. TraCA's operator maps whole intervention-indexed distributions with common semantics; a flexible response regressor with intervention-specific ID effects can conceal separate maps. The target relation must be restricted and independently tested, and the source certificate cannot be imported without its graph/shift assumptions.

**Concrete decision changed.** Write the estimand and admissible information before choosing a model: absolute endpoint, within-preparation contrast, or noncausal ranking. Add a transportability gate requiring shared variables/measurement semantics, explicit possible shifts, overlap/support, and an independent preparation or study holdout. If paired references or valid target covariates are absent, downgrade to a descriptive ranking task or return no viable transfer. Do not call a common mapping causal merely because it is shared in a fitted representation.

### 4. Robotics/control is useful mainly as a refusal rule for dynamics claims

Latent Action World Models (LAWM), arXiv:2512.10016, https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10016v1 (stored locally; P reading of §§2–3), defines action-conditioned and action-free trajectory datasets. It assumes observation trajectories `o_1:T`; the action-free model infers a latent action from current state and future observation `o_{t+1}`, and uses a Markovian state/action factorization. The latent action prior is also a support constraint for offline planning.

**Target mapping and mismatch.** State -> measured baseline cell context; action -> perturbation and dose; trajectory -> repeated time-resolved response. A sparse endpoint screen has an intervention label and endpoint, but generally lacks trajectories, future observations, action support and a Markov state. It cannot identify a latent action or a stable transition law from one endpoint per preparation.

**Failure condition/prediction.** A dynamics or closed-loop intervention claim should fail when temporal ordering, repeated observations and action support are removed, even if static regression remains accurate. A static response-surface/ranking task may remain identifiable within observed context/dose support.

**Concrete decision changed.** Exclude state-transition, latent-action, and closed-loop control claims from this data regime. Treat dose as an action only for a static conditional response/ranking estimand, and require support checks for extrapolation. A future experiment would need repeatable time-course observations and known action branches; this scout does not authorize one.

## Design recommendation to coordinator

Use the following as a falsifiable *research design*, not as a confirmed method contribution:

1. Pre-register a query-level intervention estimand and admissible inputs (baseline context, perturbation/dose/protocol metadata, controls if present).
2. Require a blocked test with unseen perturbation × context combinations, identifier-unseen and study/batch-unseen stress splits, and independent preparation holdout where possible.
3. Test one shared conditional relation across all held-out interventions; do not accept separate per-intervention alignment maps.
4. Apply selective invariance only to factors with a control/intervention showing preservation of the estimand; keep positive-control response differences separable.
5. Treat static endpoint data as a static ranking/response problem. Refuse dynamics or causal transport claims when trajectories, paired controls, support, or target covariates are absent.
6. Compare against global importance and direct regression. A useful result must change intervention prioritization on a previously unmeasured context under the blocked split, with uncertainty and failure coverage reported.

The design is viable only if the actual data contain enough controls, context variation, constituent support and independent preparation/study structure. Otherwise the correct conclusion is no viable transfer.
