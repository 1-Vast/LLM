## Scientific object and candidate

Practical users are functional-genomics and preclinical screening teams prioritizing genetic targets or compounds under a specified assay budget. The current executable object is a known screen library's binary genetic-hit ranking from assay context. The allowed context fields are retrospectively curated; their prospective availability is not established. It is distinct from compound efficacy, measured pathway mediation, calibrated death rates, or a general biological simulator.

B1 asks whether a shared low-dimensional conditional gene-hit residual relation transfers across publication/provenance groups beyond universal gene importance and strong ordinary conditional prediction. Training inputs, equations, expected failure and practical continuation margins were frozen in protocol_b.md and protocol_b_v1_1.md. A gain on this resource would be a retrospective functional-prediction capability result, not algorithmic novelty or an identified biological mechanism. The model is ordinary residual/SVD/kernel-ridge learning. Independent-target validation and a decision/cost comparison are later gates; no Agent or core integration follows from this pilot alone.

The strongest alternatives are source imbalance, common essentiality, phenotype-conditioned hit frequency, same-information text neighbors, full-rank ridge and nonlinear trees. The context-permutation control tests whether task context contributes beyond fixed gene-level structure; it is not a hypothesis-test p-value. Rarity is recomputed from fit partitions and means uncommon historical hit frequency, not proven context specificity or nonessentiality.
