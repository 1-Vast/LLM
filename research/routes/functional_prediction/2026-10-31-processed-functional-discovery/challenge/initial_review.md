# Independent initial scientific review: repair_question_v1

Saved 2026-10-31 before receiving any investigator/developer rebuttal or coordinator verdict. This reviews question qualification, not numerical admission.

## Initial judgment

The practical question is worthwhile: the same nominal repair defect does not guarantee the same compound liability in a different background, and a small independent functional panel could conceivably improve compound triage. The present packet does not establish a new repair principle, an identifiable quantitative restriction, or a usable retrospective test of that restriction. It supports a biologically motivated, experimentally testable hypothesis with material missing observations. That is neither a biological refutation nor evidence of a learning-mechanism advance.

The strongest source-supported distinction is **transfer of a separately calibrated quantitative susceptibility rule**, beyond existing functional repair measurement and genotype-conditioned synthetic lethality. Functional measurement, drug potency measurement, genetic rescue, lesion-dependent reporter specificity and selective killing are already established parts of the surrounding work. Calling their combination a tolerance boundary does not by itself create a scientific difference. A new result could reside in a validated invariant relation between those measurements; the relation and its transport conditions remain hypotheses here.

No model fitting or further workbook response access is justified by this question packet. A complete numerical protocol is expressly absent, and more importantly the necessary independent calibration/support observations have not been authenticated in the available sources.

## Source findings and material corrections

**C1. The article's primary dose-description is internally inconsistent with its table identities/dose axes.** Original XML `Sec3`, paragraph 1, says 2,344 drugs at three concentrations, with 73 drugs tested at two additional lower concentrations, giving 7,240 conditions. Those operations give 7,178, not 7,240. A fresh permitted projection of `repair-data1.xlsx`, `raw_data`, A-D only, reproduces 7,240 rows and 2,344 stored IDs: 2,246 IDs with three doses, 92 with five, and six with seven. The observed schedules include 0.0001 and approximately 0.00001 micromolar for the six seven-dose IDs. The publisher HTML independently contains the same “73 drugs” sentence. This is a real source/table design discrepancy, not an invented response result. It does not invalidate the question, but the table must not be described as merely 73 two-dose extensions or used to infer a common acquisition rule without resolving provenance. The current frozen question correctly reports 7,240 conditions, but does not surface this discrepancy. Clarification from the source/supplement is needed if dose-selection policy matters downstream.

**C2. Replication has stronger and more limited source evidence than column names alone.** Original `Fig1` caption explicitly says one replicate per primary screening condition. `Fig2` caption calls the selected b/c experiments independent biological replicates, n=2. `Fig5` caption calls c n=4, d-f n=3, g n=2 independent biological replicates. These are author-reported biological replication, not independently authenticated preparation IDs. Repeated doses or paired `repl1/repl2` headers must not inflate the primary-screen independent sample size. The packet's caution about headers is sound, but the figure captions should be retained rather than leaving all replicate evidence unspecified.

**C3. A PRKDC mutation and a PRKDC deletion are different interventions.** `Sec7` and `Sec10` describe the DNA-PKcs K3753R mutation in hiPSCs and eHAP1, whereas projected axis labels use `PRKDC (-/-)`. The manuscript calls the state NHEJ-deficient/inactivated; it is not evidence of absence of the full DNA-PKcs protein. `Sec8` itself discusses how DNA-PKcs protein presence versus kinase inhibition can alter ATM/HDR. Preserve the precise allele/intervention. Similarly, Capan-1 was BRCA2-mutant and genetically corrected; `Fig5f` source-workbook “BRCA2 knockout” is an axis label, not a replacement for that provenance. The frozen prose mostly avoids these annotation traps.

**C4. The observation process cannot supply an independent route-impairment coordinate directly.** `Sec3` classifies sequence outcomes by a two-base-pair microhomology rule after recovery and targeted amplification. `Sec4`, paragraph 1, explicitly says direct effects cannot be distinguished from indirect secondary epiphenomena. The cytarabine example (`Sec4`, paragraph 3; `Sec8`, paragraph 3) gives increased endogenous NHEJ-assigned outcomes but decreased NHEJ reporter activity. `Fig2` also normalizes selected MMEJ changes to an assay detection floor derived from POLQ knockout. Thus neither raw outcome fractions nor a percentage decrease in them are a calibrated percentage loss of pathway capacity. A threshold formed from those fractions would need independent measurement justification; it is not already authenticated by the column name.

**C5. The assays are not one common exposure/outcome window.** `Sec3` describes 24-hour drug treatment and assessment three days after editing. `Sec14` describes one day with ROCK inhibitor followed by three days in normal medium before resazurin. `Fig5c-e` uses five-day treatments; `Fig5f` uses 14-day treatment in the BRCA2-corrected comparison. The first two descriptions are not fully reconciled by the saved text, and the latter schedules are plainly different. Do not silently combine these into an absolute viable-edit yield or a shared viability target. Resazurin measures metabolism associated with living cells; changes in signal can reflect growth and metabolic effects as well as cell loss. This matters especially when proposing a quantitative survival difference.

## Closest overlap actually assessed

**Macak et al., DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67243-0, `Sec7`, `Fig5`, `Sec8`.** The source already connects repair-modulating compounds, deficient/proficient comparisons, dose-response curves, BRCA2 restoration and selective killing. It explicitly reports the background-transfer failure of artemether and gefitinib: synthetic lethality in NHEJ-deficient hiPSCs but not in NHEJ-deficient eHAP1; raloxifene and erlotinib transfer. That is the relevant practical problem and a stringent adverse example. It does not tell us whether the failure comes from tolerance, compound engagement, exposure, compensatory routes, proliferation or other effects. Published selection and these findings are exposed evidence; they cannot later become a fresh blinded success/failure demonstration.

**Rajendra et al., DOI 10.1093/nar/gkad1196, original full-text XML retrieved for this review.** The article is specifically *Quantitative, titratable and high-throughput reporter assays to measure DNA double strand break repair activity in cells*. Its `SEC3-5`, paragraphs 2-6, already combines cellular dose-dependent functional inhibition, multiple DNA-PKcs inhibitors, target-knockout controls, comparison with biochemical or engagement assays and compound ranking. `SEC3-6`, paragraphs 1-3, includes wild-type versus catalytic-mutant POLQ rescue, ART558 versus inactive enantiomer, and the toxicity-confounded novobiocin example. `SEC4`, paragraph 3, connects these assays with selectively killing HR-deficient cells in its cited earlier inhibitor work. These are material prior overlap with independent functional action profiles and restoration controls, not merely conceptual background.

Rajendra also gives a particularly relevant warning: `SEC4`, paragraphs 10-11, describes lesion/substrate dependence, including residual MMEJ signal on resection-dependent substrates after POLQ loss but much more specific dependence on resection-independent substrates. `SEC4`, paragraph 15, leaves physiological/chromatin recapitulation uncertain. Lesion specificity is scientifically justified, but is already an explicit property of existing assay design. I did not find an unseen-background survival-tolerance restriction in the inspected passages. This bounded reading is not an exhaustive novelty search and does not prove its absence from all prior work.

**Villacampa et al., DOI 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-24-3148, saved original abstract/metadata only.** The predefined functional RAD51-low cutoff and its association with pathological complete response in a genomically preselected HRD population overlap with the idea that functional state adds information beyond genotype. This is a post hoc biomarker analysis under combination treatment, with similar associations in both olaparib and carboplatin groups; it does not establish choosing between them, de novo compound transfer, a tolerance-to-route-loss curve, or the proposed quantitative invariant. Full methods were not read. The abstract cannot establish absence of additional functional calibration methods elsewhere in that literature.

The other seven abstracts in the saved refined-prior JSON were inspected as abstracts, not full-text validation. They include treatment-dependent functional HRD discordance and ordinary pharmacodynamic/pharmacokinetic alternatives. They do not supply the missing calibration observations here. Macak's references CR21/CR60/CR95 were inspected as bibliography entries only, not treated as full-text verification.

## Simpler equivalent models and identification

For a single route and lesion, let independently measured impairment be x and background tolerance be T. A rule `P(death)=sigmoid[k(x-T)]` is an ordinary dose/impairment-response model with a background-specific midpoint. If x and T are freely learned only from candidate survival, it is an additive logistic model; with multiple learned routes and context-dependent weights it can become an ordinary low-rank interaction model. Reparameterization, scale changes and midpoint/slope tradeoffs do not become identifiable by naming one coordinate tolerance. A step rule is likewise a conventional threshold model, with an even stronger approximation to biological variability.

Independent calibration can anchor a coordinate and impose a real predictive restriction. But to earn the proposed scientific difference, its scale must be comparable to compound impairment, the mapping to outcome must be fixed, and the restriction must predict an intervention or transport condition that an equal-information ordinary model need not satisfy. A boundary alone does not determine the promised continuous survival difference without a slope/link or empirical response curve. This is a missing definition at qualification, not a demand to fit such a curve now.

A conventional functional panel plus a fitted potency/Hill response, or Ridge/nearest-profile prediction using the same acquisitions, remains a strong rival. An accurate transfer result could establish useful functional prediction without establishing a new learning mechanism. Two backgrounds with a few compounds could challenge a biological invariant; they would not support a reliable general performance ranking across model classes or the whole compound library.

## Unresolved premises and decisive evidence

1. **Transport of impairment is the largest missing premise.** An impairment profile measured in a reference cell background is not automatically the impairment at the same nominal dose in a new background. Uptake, metabolism, protein abundance, growth and lesion burden can change it. Existing source-specific dose optimization and the eHAP1 failures make this material. Evidence needed: independently authenticated functional impairment/engagement comparability at the relevant exposure, or a prespecified justified transport map. If every candidate needs target-background measurements to obtain that map, those acquisitions must be charged to the claimed practical budget; a fixed calibration panel alone has not solved the problem.

2. **Lesion and endpoint support must overlap.** The primary reference is a targeted blunt-end DSB with a donor, whereas basal synthetic lethality and cisplatin challenge need not produce the same repair substrate or demand. A lesion-specific rule is reasonable, but then the drug-discovery deployment must actually lie inside that lesion/exposure scope. Evidence needed: explicit assay substrate, timing, challenge intensity and matched endpoints, with uncertainty when these cannot be equated. More elaborate latent repair states inferred after seeing survival would not resolve this premise.

3. **Route-specific tolerance must separate from general frailty.** A calibration panel can measure slow growth, metabolic depression or generic susceptibility rather than tolerance to a particular repair loss. Restoration should change route-specific liability consistently, but a single corrected clone also carries clonal-selection uncertainty. Evidence needed: preparation/clone provenance and an appropriate restoration comparison, matched non-repair toxicity controls and orthogonal support for the proposed route coordinate. Two chemically distinct inhibitors with comparable impairment are useful, but shared off-target action or unmatched lesion generation would leave a failure inconclusive.

4. **A boundary reversal must be an actual prediction.** The published hiPSC/eHAP1 difference motivates the question but cannot validate a rule chosen to explain it. Evidence needed: fixed independent calibration, a fixed rule and an unexposed target-compound/background outcome. Comparable calibration and engagement followed by incompatible liabilities would refute the stated restriction within scope; missing engagement would not. A genotype-only rule predicting the same ordering would leave the additional restriction untested.

## What present resources permit

The primary and selected follow-up screens can support an audit of observation design and, under a later authorized protocol, ordinary within-setting functional prediction. The source has valuable selected isogenic comparisons and a BRCA2 restoration experiment; it is incorrect to dismiss it as having no intervention evidence. But it does not authenticate a candidate-independent tolerance-calibration panel, the same independently measured impairment coordinate across the relevant backgrounds, matched calibration and survival windows, or an unexposed adequately supported outcome panel for the proposed transfer claim. The Fig5/SuppFig19 axes identify some backgrounds/treatments, not those missing observations.

The proposed two-background falsification experiment could be informative if those measurements exist or are acquired. Current saved evidence does not demonstrate that it can be assembled cheaply from these processed workbooks. A negative disposition for **current numerical test feasibility** is justified; a negative disposition for **all biological versions of the hypothesis** is not. Novelty remains unresolved, with substantial ordinary overlap and a narrowly specified possible quantitative difference still to establish.
