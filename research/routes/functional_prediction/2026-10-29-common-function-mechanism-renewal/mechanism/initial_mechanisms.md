## Preferred opening: finite selection changes have finite power

Consider a deliberately restrictive but inspectable experiment. Before pretreatment A, initial cells have arbitrary stable latent types z with proportions pi_z. Under a fixed subsequent challenge B, the unchanged type-specific surviving fraction is b_z in [0,1]. Pretreatment retains a fraction a_z in [0,1] of each type, with no proliferation, no conversion, no hidden change to the B response, no carryover and matched challenge handling. Let p = sum pi_z a_z be the surviving fraction after A, q = sum pi_z b_z the B-alone fraction, and r = sum pi_z a_z b_z / p the B surviving fraction among A survivors.

For p > 0, static selection necessarily obeys the sharp bounds:

`max(0, (p + q - 1)/p) <= r <= min(1, q/p)`.

Reason: products of two quantities in [0,1] satisfy a*b >= a+b-1, a*b >=0, a*b <= a and a*b <= b. Averaging and dividing by p gives the bounds; mixtures of deterministic 0/1 phenotypes attain the endpoints. This is a Fréchet/selection bound, not a new probability theorem or a new inference algorithm.

Constructive thought case: p=0.95 and q=0.80 force r >= 15/19, approximately 0.7895. An r=0.20 cannot come from selecting away only 5% of initial cells while keeping each cell's B susceptibility fixed. This is an exact mathematical example, not a calculation run or biological observation. A strong simple rival is the fully arbitrary static-mixture model satisfying the same mass constraints; a tiny linear program or this analytic bound already implements it.

The usable output would be an exclusion of a specific explanation: “this susceptibility shift is too large for the independently qualified selection budget.” It could direct a screening team toward induction/recovery or carryover controls instead of interpreting every response change as enrichment of a resistant/sensitive subtype. Ordinary inference may implement the test; the potential substantive advance lies in a useful, empirically qualified measurement principle.

## Critical failure boundary known before development

Net live counts do not measure total turnover. Unrestricted births and deaths can offset each other while radically changing composition, so p near 1 does not certify weak selection. With pretreatment fold weights w_z >=0 and no finite upper bound, observed average growth m alone gives essentially no useful bound. If an independently justified bound w_z <= U exists, the same argument yields:

`max(0, (m - U*(1-q))/m) <= r <= min(1, U*q/m)`.

At U=1 this is the no-growth case. If U is large relative to m, the interval approaches [0,1]. A vehicle's observed average growth is not a valid universal U for every hidden subtype. Neither HTS001's two technical wells nor its control-only mean fit supplies that missing upper bound. No covariance-to-turnover inference is proposed.

Also, b_z must truly remain invariant across challenge preparations. Drug carryover, altered medium, unequal inoculum/density, altered cell cycle or delayed pharmacology can break it. Excluding selection alone does not isolate which violation occurred. OD, ATP or resazurin are not automatically live-cell survival fractions and cannot be substituted for p/q/r.

## Next evidence that could change the decision

The next read should ask whether a cheap, ordinary functional switch resource has (a) absolute viable mass before and after pretreatment; (b) a defensible restriction on within-pretreatment population expansion/turnover; (c) matching viable inoculum, wash/carryover controls and challenge timing; and (d) at least a few untouched challenge conditions with independent preparation identities. Existing history-response data with only endpoint normalized viability would not admit this bound. Lack of these measurements leaves the line data-unready, not biologically false.

No claim of novelty, empirically successful transfer, cost saving or Agent advantage is made. The lead should be killed as the current main candidate if an inspected closest prior already supplies this mass-constrained discrimination and useful transfer, or if its required mass/turnover information cannot be qualified in an authorized ordinary resource. A weaker generic history learner is not a rescue.
