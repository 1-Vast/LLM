## Scientific question

For one cell background, two prespecified prior histories H0 and H1, and exactly two supported subsequent actions A and B, can an inexpensive pre-action measurement S preserve the later action contrast across the two histories and an independently prepared holdout block?

The tested restriction is:

`E[Y(A)-Y(B) | S=s, H=h] = tau(s)`

on a source-defined common-support region. The initial S contract excludes a history identifier. It starts with cell count/confluence and growth/viability before the next action; a small functional probe is added only if endpoint-only data show a reproducible blind spot and the panel is frozen before holdout outcomes. H remains a design/stratification covariate and a comparator input for an S+H model.

The action menu and regret oracle are exactly `{A,B}`. A larger menu is a separate claim requiring every supported contrast against a declared reference action.

## Intended contribution

The possible contribution is a bounded biological response law: which measured state and assay conditions preserve a consequential two-action contrast across a defined history pair, preparation range and chemistry support, where the law fails, and whether it reduces actual assay cost or choice regret. PSR, reward/value equivalence, decision-focused optimization, active identification, causal transport, DBP and response-panel reconstruction are inherited principles or closest priors; none is claimed as a new algorithm.

A positive result would support only the named cell model, history pair, action pair, dose/time, washout/carryover rule, endpoint and preparation range. It would not establish a universal cell state, mechanism, absolute efficacy/safety equivalence, unseen-action transfer, or organoid behavior.

## Required evidence and input boundary

A resource is admitted only if the same lossless join contains history A, subsequent action B, physical dose/MIC, order, pause or washout, endpoint time, cell/background, preparation/biological replicate and technical replicate. It must include both A/B actions in the same eligible unit, a declared denominator, controls, and a pre-action S measured on exchangeable sisters or a nondestructive equivalent. Observational assignments require explicit identification assumptions. Technical wells are not independent preparations.

Easy inputs are bulk counts, confluence/growth, viability and recorded physical treatment metadata. Single-cell, spatial, proteomic and transcriptomic inputs are not prerequisites. A destructive functional probe is allowed only with randomized/exchangeable diagnostic sisters. A later drug chemotype holdout and a held history/preparation holdout are separate claims.

## Falsifier and positive gate

The candidate is falsified for the tested state contract if, within overlapping S support, the history difference in the A-versus-B contrast reproducibly exceeds a prespecified consequential equivalence margin in independent preparation blocks. A difference in absolute outcome under one action alone is not a falsifier; neither is a contrast difference outside support. Wide intervals are inconclusive, and failure of this S does not prove that no richer state exists.

A positive gate requires all of:

1. A predeclared outcome scale, dose/time/order/washout and margin; no margin estimated from holdout outcomes.
2. Randomized or otherwise identified A/B assignment with common support and block-level uncertainty.
3. A held preparation block (and, only if explicitly designed, a held history) scored before tuning.
4. Noninferiority or superiority to same-cost history-only, direct contrast regression, ordinary response-panel ridge/covariance or PLS, and an appropriate mechanism-specific probe such as DBP.
5. Choice utility (regret, sign/tie coverage) reported separately from contrast equivalence and absolute endpoint calibration.
