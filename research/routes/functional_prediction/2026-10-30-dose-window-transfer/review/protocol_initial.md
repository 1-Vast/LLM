## Significance and closest-prior overlap

The operative restriction is exactly

`y_target,d(x) = A_target,d + D_target,d * [u(x) + q_d(x)]`,

with drug/dose-specific q learned from source backgrounds. It is a fixed two-anchor affine transformation of a learned shape. Endpoint measurements, not a background ID, supply the target-specific functional state. This is an honest and potentially useful scientific question about conditional response regularity. Its practical output is several in-vitro dose-window choices after measuring two dose conditions per library compound. The proposed multi-window use avoids calling a single predicted condition cheaper than measuring that condition.

The ancestry is substantial. MOGP already predicts complete dose-response profiles by sharing information among dose outputs, and explicitly reports that transfer from additional cancer types can worsen rare-cancer prediction. Its input setting includes genomic/drug features rather than this exact two-anchor calibration task, so it is not a direct same-information numerical baseline without adaptation. DRP already uses measured drug-response panels as functional descriptors to predict other responses using ordinary trees. Thunor already uses ordinary constrained logistic curves and distinguishes growth-rate and viability conventions. BESTDR's inspected passages already describe hierarchical curve sharing. These sources remove a plausible algorithm-novelty claim; they do not answer whether this particular anchored restriction has useful performance against the frozen ordinary models under equal inputs. The current candidate appropriately makes no algorithm-novelty claim.

Even a passing matrix would initially be evidence for a task-specific regularity and an incremental capability, with scientific interest strengthened only by a stable boundary of when it works or fails and a genuinely useful reduction in screening burden. It would not identify drug targets, death, resistance mediation or clinical exposure. The finite endpoint is a vehicle-adjusted 24-to-60-hour growth contrast, not a verified post-stabilization DIP rate; Thunor/Harris cannot convert it into one by citation.

## Information, assay conditions and estimand

The information contract is fair in its stated scope: all models may use the same historical full curves, every target drug's same two calibration conditions, vehicles, dose identities and drug identities. The candidate may elect to ignore some of that available information. Cross-drug covariance, response-profile neighbors and trees are correctly allowed the whole anchor panel. No intermediate target responses may enter feature construction, tuning, priors, scaling, failure handling or eligible-task selection.

The two calibration conditions are 20% of a ten-condition dose grid and one third of six queried interior conditions. This is a count of dose conditions. The protocol correctly declines to equate that count with total assay-cost savings: technical wells, controls, imaging, historical collection and plate occupancy remain real costs. Success would justify testing that proposed use case; it would not yet beat an optimized alternative allocation of the same two assay conditions. An assay-design comparison is a later decision and is not necessary to falsify this fixed-input hypothesis now.

The primary regret is computable and interpretable: at an eligible same-physical-concentration pair, choose the lower predicted growth response and measure the observed penalty relative to the better member. Macro-averaging query concentrations within pair, pairs within background, then backgrounds prevents the largest roster from silently defining the target. The exact-dose rule is scientifically cleaner than comparing the same normalized dose index between differently scaled drugs. It can exclude singleton menus or drug pairs; actual complete rosters and denominators must be retained. The secondary full-menu best-drug regret is necessary because a lower average pairwise loss need not improve final selection.

This estimates performance on the finite eligible query menus, conditional on two-well anchor means and the fixed library. It is not unseen-drug, unseen-scaffold or arbitrary-concentration prediction. The smooth-GP rival may interpolate continuously, but the candidate's fitted q values only support the six specified query locations unless an additional interpolation rule is separately defined. The current finite-grid experiment needs no such rule.

## Identifiability, generalization and leakage

Two endpoints identify A and D, not an unrestricted target curve. Shared q is the empirical restriction under test. When D is near zero, the candidate collapses toward the anchor line even if there is strong intermediate curvature; when potency or shape changes independently of endpoint difference, it can fail. These are meaningful falsifiers, not numerical defects to rescue after scores. A passing prediction test would support sufficiency within this task, not the uniqueness or biological truth of q.

Outer background holding-out, inner source-only tuning and recomputation of fitted summaries are appropriate. The implementation review must check every source mean, covariance, variance, tree feature and Hill prior inside the current inner fit; an outer-source summary is not legal inside its inner validation. All target anchors are deliberately allowed calibration information, so their use is not leakage. All endpoints of a background, including its two technical wells, must remain in the same outer fold. Scoring each technical well without refitting is a sensitivity check conditional on the same calibration means, not a fresh technical or biological prediction experiment.

There is only one plate per HTS001 background, shared PC9 ancestry and no independent preparation series. Thus the six folds cannot separate background biology from plate/run effects or support six independent biological replications. Those limitations restrict the claim but do not bar an explicitly retrospective internal experiment. The current protocol states that boundary adequately.

HTS031 metadata shows a different background family, a different panel and seven-dose designs. If later admitted, its protocol must state whether the transfer is of the frozen hypothesis/procedure trained anew within that panel or of HTS001-learned drug shapes. A 126-drug replay cannot directly transport fourteen drug-specific learned shapes to new identities. This is a future-protocol requirement, not a blocker of the HTS001 matrix; that protocol is already reserved for a separate review before count decoding.

## Strong rivals, headroom and decision rule

The fixed panel addresses the main ordinary explanations: direct interpolation; additive, pooled and wrong-drug shape; a less restricted endpoint regression; full-profile covariance conditioning; a smooth per-drug GP; regularized Hill fitting; response-profile neighbors; and same-information trees. With only four/five source backgrounds, more flexible rivals can be unstable, but that is a real small-sample regime rather than a justification for withholding their inputs. Full covariance conditioning in particular tests whether ordinary response correlation explains the benefit. No deep model or omics-only baseline is required to make this bounded comparison informative.

Selecting the empirically lowest-loss ordinary rival makes the point comparison demanding. Reusing that selected rival in a fixed paired-background bootstrap does not create a valid population-level confidence statement: selection, shared training and plate dependence remain. The protocol already labels the interval conditional. Treat the interval and fold-count conditions as continuation heuristics only, and retain all six losses and all rival losses so that this limitation stays visible. No pair-level bootstrap or treatment-well pseudo-replication is appropriate for biological significance.

The absolute and relative margin, oracle headroom, drug-specific-shape checks, full-menu safeguard and technical-well sensitivities provide a clear stop condition. Their numeric values are predeclared research-decision thresholds, not externally validated screening-utility or clinical thresholds. If the strongest ordinary rival already has less than 0.05 regret, the chosen absolute improvement gate is unattainable in this matrix. Reporting that headroom resolves the interpretation; it is not a reason to change the gate after seeing results. Matrix/resource failure is inconclusive, not evidence against the biological restriction.

## Corrections to freeze before fitting

1. **Hyperparameter sharing level.** State that each model receives one listed hyperparameter configuration per outer fold, selected globally by the declared inner macro-regret across the complete eligible roster (with the same rule when fitting an inner training split). In particular, specify whether candidate/pooled lambda, ridge penalty, GP settings and Hill prior strength are shared across drugs/query positions. The intended global rule appears likely, but per-drug/per-position tuning would be a materially different procedure with far more choices and a nonseparable pair-choice objective. Resolve by writing the level once and making the implementation and inner-choice output conform to it. No outcome inspection is needed.

2. **Hill source-fit failures.** The stated fallback to a source-prior curve is well-defined for a failed target MAP fit, but not for a failed source curve used to construct that very prior. Freeze a deterministic source-failure policy before execution and ensure initial E values are clipped into the stated bounds. One acceptable policy is to count any unresolved source fit failure as a Hill-panel execution failure and hence an inconclusive overall gate while retaining the rest of the fixed outputs. Another is a predeclared bounded fallback parameter vector included in the prior with its failure flag. Do not omit failed source curves silently or choose a policy after seeing how often it occurs. State whether max-evaluation termination counts as failure and distinguish it from a finite successful boundary optimum.

3. **Shared fit-variance and ridge scaling.** Define the referenced global fit variance unambiguously, for example the mean of squared deviations of each drug/dose response from its fit-background mean, floored at 0.01, and reuse that quantity wherever the protocol references it. Freeze the ridge A/D standard-deviation convention and deterministic handling of a zero-variance feature. Grand-mean centering versus response-column centering changes the covariance regularization and Hill data/prior balance; zero-variance scaling must not turn a complete panel into an avoidable numerical failure. These are prefit computational conventions, not new tuning choices.

After these clarifications and the planned independent code review, execute the one frozen matrix within its existing compute budget.
