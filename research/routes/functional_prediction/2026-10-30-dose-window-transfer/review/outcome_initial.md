## Decision and evidence

Park this particular anchor-scaled shape restriction under the frozen continuation rule. Do not open the separate HTS031 test for it, revise the gate after seeing headroom, or perform another matrix to rescue the result. The matrix answers a useful internal question, but it does not demonstrate that the proposed restriction improves drug choice beyond ordinary response sharing.

The primary point estimate is worse than joint covariance conditioning: candidate regret is **0.0253236**, compared with **0.0223453**, a candidate deficit of **0.0029783 log2-growth units** or **13.33%** of rival loss. It improves only two of six backgrounds (BR1 and DS8). The deficit persists when scoring either technical well separately: rival-minus-candidate differences are -0.0020493 and -0.0039073. This is a completed empirical failure to demonstrate the specified advantage, not an incomplete-execution result.

The conditional paired-background interval is **[-0.0093893, 0.0021618]** for rival-minus-candidate regret. It crosses zero. Thus the data do not establish population-level inferiority, equality or superiority. Shared training, related PC9 backgrounds and one plate per background further prevent interpreting that interval as confirmation across independent cultures. A point deficit and inconclusive population inference can coexist; neither justifies describing a demonstrated advantage.

## What the shape and calibration comparisons support

Candidate improves on linear interpolation by 0.0253047, pooled shape by 0.0238157 and wrong-drug shape by 0.0506403. These are real within-matrix predictive contrasts consistent with useful drug-specific historical curvature. They do not establish the need for the specific assumption `r_target approximately D_target*q_drug`: an ordinary additive residual curve has better primary loss, better best-drug loss and lower MSE (0.0192061 versus candidate 0.0211391). Less restricted endpoint/profile models also compete successfully. The result leaves the proposed amplitude normalization unsupported as an advantageous restriction; it does not show that all curve sharing is useless or identify which biological process caused its failures.

Target calibration value is task-dependent here. The candidate's primary loss is already worse than a source mean that receives no target treatment measurements. Joint covariance improves primary loss over that source mean by only about 0.00127. Consequently, these primary scores do not establish a consequential assay-cost benefit from measuring the anchor panel. In contrast, the candidate and additive model substantially improve the full-menu point loss over source mean. That is useful internal evidence that calibration can affect particular compound selections. It remains a comparison of specified predictors, not a causal isolation of calibration value or a measured cost-effectiveness result.

## Dose-rank phenomenon and final selections

There are **35 technically concordant observed flips among 336 background/pair units** on the six declared interior query doses. This is the restricted query roster; it must not be confused with the earlier all-dose descriptive census. Technical concordance does not identify an independent biological reversal mechanism.

Candidate recovers 19 of those 35 flips and predicts 27: sensitivity 54.29%, precision 70.37%. Joint covariance recovers 26 (sensitivity 74.29%, precision 72.22%); additive shape recovers 27 (77.14%, 72.97%); source-neighbor recovers 28 (80%, 73.68%). Linear and pooled shape recover only six, with no false positives. Their 100% precision therefore accompanies very low sensitivity, rather than resolving the phenotype. The phenomenon exists in these measurements, but it does not uniquely favor the candidate.

Candidate best-drug regret is lower than the fixed primary rival by 0.0071461, so that secondary safeguard passes. Additive shape is better still. This secondary advantage cannot replace the failed predeclared primary objective. The menu metric also represents a different weighting: each background contributes six two-drug panobinostat/trametinib menus and six eleven-drug menus. Primary pair averaging contains 55 pairs from the eleven-drug group and one from the two-drug group. These are specific overlapping menus, not twelve complete fourteen-drug screens; singleton temsirolimus menus are excluded. Their different weights help explain why primary and final-selection rankings can differ.

## Headroom, optimization and next-step boundary

The strongest ordinary rival has only **0.0223453** oracle headroom. The frozen 0.05 absolute improvement threshold is therefore unattainable in this matrix even for a zero-regret predictor. This gate limitation was anticipated prospectively and should be reported plainly. It is not evidence that shape transfer is biologically impossible. Separately, candidate has a negative point improvement, misses the relative/fold/technical criteria and lacks a positive conditional interval; the conclusion does not rest only on the mechanically unattainable absolute threshold.

The run receipt reports a complete twelve-method matrix in 53.703 seconds with four configured threads, 10,080 prediction rows and no matrix exception. Its pilot/protocol/amendment hashes match the prefit-reviewed versions. The independent arithmetic result reports a valid complete matrix and agreement across 176 schema-field groups. That verifies output arithmetic within its stated scope; it does not refit or independently validate the learned functions.

There were **49 reported optimizer non-successes**, handled by the prospectively specified Hill fallbacks. The allowed aggregate receipts do not provide their source/target or final/inner breakdown, so they should not be represented as 49 independent failed prediction tasks or as an inferred failure rate. They qualify any favorable candidate-versus-Hill comparison. They do not invalidate the complete matrix or explain the candidate's deficit to covariance, additive and other rivals that do not depend on Hill optimization.

The justified action is to retain a bounded negative result for C2, including its informative ablations and secondary benefits. This does not rule out other functional-response hypotheses. It does not support a new algorithm, a mechanism claim, an Agent, core promotion, or release of the gated new response panel. No further test is warranted for this restriction under the current frozen continuation contract.
