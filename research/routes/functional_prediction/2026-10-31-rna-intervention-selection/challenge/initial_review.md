## Verdict

**Not admitted for numerical execution in its present specification. Conditionally defensible as one small exploratory comparison after the measurement and numerical gates below are closed. No algorithmic novelty or general Virtual Cell claim is admitted, even if the proposed score threshold is met.** The scientific question is intelligible: given the same intervention already measured in one cell line, does an ordinary odds-scale transfer predict the other cell's exon responses better than equally informed conventional predictors? Its usefulness is limited to prioritizing follow-up splicing responses within these public experiments. It does not predict a previously unseen perturbation from baseline cell state alone.

The main blockers are genuinely pre-value issues: replicate identity is not established by the current inventories, boundary clipping creates a nonzero response even when the proposed scale a is zero, bounded ordinary response transfer is missing as an explicit rival, and the eligibility/fold/information-flow contract is incomplete. These are repairable without reading response values. Failure to authenticate distinct biological controls should end this pilot rather than relax its premise.

## What the original evidence supports

1. The ENCODE original Nature article (DOI 10.1038/s41586-020-2077-3; PMC7410833) explicitly identifies ENCSR413YAF as the secondary-analysis collection and distinguishes it from the batch-corrected ENCSR870OLK collection. Its data-availability statement says analyses used hg19 and GENCODE v19 unless stated otherwise. XML Par57 says rMATS v3.2.1.beta used knockdown BAMs and control BAMs with GENCODE v19. It describes significance filtering in the published analysis; therefore the publication's significant-event universe cannot be reused as an outcome-blind test universe.
2. The saved official publication object includes all six selected archive IDs in related_files. The saved official splicing inventory identifies these six as hg19 differential-splicing quantifications and provides four upstream BAM IDs per archive. All six advertise biological_replicates [1,2]. This authenticates collection membership and an advertised upstream structure, **not two distinct biological control samples** or replicate-slot ordering. The inventory does not provide the sample/library crosswalk required for that inference.
3. ENCODE XML Par60 directly warns why this matters: controls were shared within batches, and when library preparation was split, additional libraries were made from the same non-specific control RNA samples. Two control BAMs can therefore represent libraries from the same biological RNA sample. Different filenames, library IDs, accession IDs or comma-separated rMATS slots are insufficient proof of biological independence. Control metadata must resolve the biological sample/biosample identity, biological versus technical replicate, and batch relationships.
4. ENCODE already studied across-cell preservation of binding (Par23) and splicing maps (Par30). Such comparisons are prior art, not validation of the proposed event-wise numerical transfer formula. Neither eCLIP availability nor a source-cell knockdown effect establishes a direct functional binding mechanism at any particular exon.

## Closest-prior challenge: stronger than a generic KATMAP mention

KATMAP's primary publisher article (DOI 10.1038/s41587-025-02881-9) explicitly uses log-odds inclusion changes and explains almost exactly the proposed response-scale motivation: raw deltaPSI depends on starting inclusion and obeys 0/1 bounds, while odds ratios treat changes near boundaries differently (HTML p[148]-p[150]). It also uses baseline inclusion and its square as predictive confounder adjustments. Therefore **baseline-dependent odds-scale representation itself is established, not an innovative mechanism**.

KATMAP also predicts splicing direction with interpretable models and transfers models between HepG2 and K562 when inferring which SF was perturbed (p[58], p[159]). It directly compares this with an inclusion-based approach that uses observed changes from other knockdowns as predictors (p[59]); this is closer than calling it merely a motif-attribution task. However, this inverse factor-identification use is not the same endpoint or information set as predicting quantitative target-cell deltaPSI for a known intervention with no target-cell response labels. KATMAP explicitly says it does not aim to predict the magnitude of inclusion changes (p[149]).

Thus a narrow numerical comparison remains a legitimate empirical question, but this is a conventional response-scale/baseline comparison adjacent to well-developed prior work. A positive result would not establish a new class of functional RNA predictor, distinguish direct from indirect targets, or beat KATMAP. I did not search the entire literature; absence of an exact formula in the selected sources is not evidence of novelty. No broad novelty search is necessary to permit an explicitly non-novel one-shot diagnostic.

## Required closure before response values or fitting

### A. Resolve actual samples and observation support

For each of the six archives, record source authenticity and hash, the KD/control orientation, the exact SE junction-count member, assembly/annotation, upstream BAM accession -> experiment -> biological sample/replicate -> library/technical replicate crosswalk, and the control experiment link. Verify two distinct KD biological samples and two distinct control biological samples. Multiple independent sequencing runs of one RNA sample do not qualify. Explain any reuse of controls across the three RBPs and batches. Where replicate slot order cannot be crosswalked, prove that the two slots still correspond to the two distinct samples and treat slot labels as arbitrary; do not call slot0 replicate1.

Annotation-based event eligibility requires more than an event having coordinates in an rMATS output. The program can enumerate events supported by treatment reads; requiring an event in both treatment-containing archives can then impose treatment-informed ascertainment. Use a genuinely fixed reference/control-supported event definition; establish how original archive presence/absence interacts with this. Freeze coordinate convention, strand, gene-version normalization, unique event-to-gene handling, duplicate handling and event matching. All eligibility/coverage thresholds must use the permitted controls and reference, not significance, treatment PSI, delta, or treatment read coverage. If undefined treatment PSI prevents labeling a control-eligible event, predefine the missing-label policy and report its count separately; do not silently relabel a complete-case subset as an outcome-independent universe.

### B. Complete the scientific/numerical contract

- Freeze the precise train/validation/test gene hash assignment using **gene identity alone across all RBPs and both cells**. A hash of (gene,RBP,cell) would leak genes across the split. State which intersection of intervention and gene split each stage uses. HNRNPC is the only training intervention; HNRNPK is validation; RBFOX2 is the only decisive test intervention. Do not refit on validation labels after selection unless that operation is explicitly frozen for every applicable method.
- Freeze separate direction fitting and information access. A target response in one direction is a source input in the opposite direction. That reuse is allowed for this task, but the two directions are not independent validations. No target test response may enter scaling, imputation, feature selection, hyperparameters or stopping through the opposite-direction code path. Source test responses are allowed inputs, so held-out genes/interventions are not globally response-unseen.
- Freeze the complete source feature vector for every ordinary learned model, including all quantities needed to reconstruct the candidate's source log-odds. No treatment significance, FDR, treatment read-depth or knockdown-efficiency feature may be accidentally exposed. Fit all preprocessors on training only. Declare global versus direction-specific parameter choices.
- Repair or explicitly reject the epsilon boundary artifact. As written, when a=0 and target baseline b=0, sigmoid(logit(clip(b))) - b = 0.01, not zero. The same issue produces -0.01 at b=1; the candidate does not represent zero-response at its zero-scale value. Choose a principled zero-preserving boundary convention before values (or control-only boundary exclusion), enforce feasible predictions, and analytically check a=0, source zero change, and b near 0/1. Do not pick the convention after observing results.
- Include an explicit ordinary **bounded raw-delta transfer** rival, and bounded fitted linear rescaling if rescaling is a rival: clip(source_delta or fitted_delta, -target_baseline, 1-target_baseline). Otherwise a logistic predictor may win merely because competitors make impossible target PSI predictions. Declare how any prediction bounds apply to Ridge/polynomial/boosting too. The relevant comparison is odds geometry versus equally informed ordinary conditioning and support bounds.
- Freeze all estimator grids, intercepts, feature scaling, polynomial order, regularization, boosting settings, model selection criterion and tie rules, random seeds, handling of invalid PSI/counts, and minimum eligible gene/event counts. The 5% threshold is a proposed continuation rule, not a statistically established biological effect size.
- Define Top100 exactly: ranking by absolute predicted effect or otherwise, tie resolution, fewer-than-100 behavior, duplicate events, unit counts, and signed hit denominator. A sign-correct hit should require both the frozen absolute observed response threshold and correct predicted sign. Multiple exons per gene must be visible in reporting. This selects follow-up events, not separately purchasable RNA-seq assays.
- Define equal-gene MSE explicitly (mean event loss within each gene, then mean genes), paired uncertainty procedure, interval level, seed and the meaning of positive support. Event-wise resampling is invalid for the stated gene-cluster analysis. Report descriptive gene-cluster uncertainty as conditional on these experiments; genes share experimental and regulatory shocks, so it is not a confidence interval over new cell lines or interventions. Preserve the both-directions/every-rival conjunctive rule without selecting the most favorable endpoint or direction.
- Freeze slot reversal as a frozen-model sensitivity with an exact input/label swap definition. Clarify whether it reverses source controls as well as target controls. Use it to detect sensitivity, not select a better slot or rescue a failed primary analysis. Predefine how a large disagreement affects interpretation.
- Freeze execution code and its caller-flow review after these scientific decisions, plus the exact implementation-failure reserve. An outcome-unfavorable result, low response prevalence, failed continuation threshold or disappointing baseline is not an implementation failure.

### C. Interpretability and causal boundary

Separating target control slots removes direct reuse of one observed baseline value inside its own response label. It does not prove error independence, because distinct cultures can share batches, library processing, cell-state shifts and depth effects. Even with distinct samples, this is a noisy cross-replicate target: the fitted label is mean KD PSI minus a different control sample, not the response to the particular baseline sample supplied as input. Replicate reversal is useful but does not identify the true latent baseline-conditioned causal effect.

RBP knockdown is an intervention on the RBP under the assay protocol; observed exon changes can include direct binding effects, indirect SF cascades, incomplete knockdown, off-target effects and cell-state/composition changes. Without eCLIP/sequence modeling and perturbation validation, a correctly predicted exon response is not a direct functional binding site, RNA-editing effect, therapeutic target or molecular mechanism. Different knockdown efficiency across cell lines can produce or obscure apparent transport; the present panel cannot disentangle it from the odds-scale hypothesis.

The sample-size statement must lead with **three intervention identities in total and one held-out test identity, RBFOX2, measured in two related public cell-line experiments**. Thousands of exon/gene labels do not create thousands of independent intervention tests. Lexical ordering prevents outcome-based choice of the test target, but does not turn this biology-selected panel into a representative sample. Positive results justify only a larger independently frozen panel; failure rejects this candidate under this setting, not splicing transfer generally.
