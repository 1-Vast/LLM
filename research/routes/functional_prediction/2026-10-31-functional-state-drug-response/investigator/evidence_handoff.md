## Important unresolved phenomenon, practical need and next discriminator

A cancer pharmacology team wants to select a subsequent treatment after transient pretreatment or withdrawal. The documented source challenge is that state-changing pretreatment can alter clonogenic persistence, and short-term cytostasis need not measure recovery competence. The practical application—using measured state to predict an unmeasured schedule before screening it—is proposed, not a demonstrated source capability or assay-saving benefit.

Strongest unresolved question: does a state measurement at the actual end of a controlled pretreatment carry information about a later clonogenic response beyond treatment labels, initial surviving-cell abundance and a simple phase/compartment model? Alternative explanations include direct residual-drug effects, differential reseeding, induction versus selection, broad cell-cycle suppression and genotype/preparation differences. Marker association and reported rescue findings cannot be converted into a new measured-state prediction result.

Smallest useful discriminator is currently DATA ELIGIBILITY, not fitting: an original source record linking independent preparation ID, exact history/dose/washout, state observed at2dayTNF endpoint BEFORE Doxo, reseeded-cell denominator, later clonogenic frequency or raw organoid counts, and repeated preparations with a predeclared held history. Either paired sister aliquots or a nondestructive population measurement can be valid; same-cell tracking is not intrinsically required for a population endpoint. If that contract exists, freeze the exact schema and compare treatment-label-only/initial-abundance/phase-compartment rivals with measured-state prediction on held preparations and a held schedule. Without it, no numerical model is admitted and no biological mechanism is refuted.
