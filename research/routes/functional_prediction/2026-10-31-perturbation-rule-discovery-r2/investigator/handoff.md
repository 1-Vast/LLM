## Decision

The strongest new opening is **composition-dependent functional intervention**, specifically asking when complementary state coverage transports into functional combination benefit when the mixture of states changes. This differs from an ASO sequence predictor and from predicting expression. However, 'target complementary cell states', 'use single-cell expression plus single-agent responses to nominate combinations', and 'distinguish cell-autonomous synergy from tumor-level benefit' are already explicit target-field prior. A new claim would need a falsifiable, quantitatively calibrated relation between separately measured state-resolved drug action, initial population composition and total functional burden, and evidence that this relation predicts a changed composition or model without target combination calibration. Merely applying weighted Bliss, adding a state encoder or testing published selected pairs is insufficient.

This remains an opening, not a developed/recommended new mechanism. There is no evidence yet that a common intervention law survives a composition change. State proportions after treatment alone cannot distinguish depletion, differentiation/state conversion, growth and sampling. DMG's independent in vitro/in vivo comparisons are biologically valuable but do not supply a randomized composition manipulation or a common assay frame. AML has matched disease stages and functional subpopulation assays, but only one patient's selected combinations were validated. Existing data may still support a narrower falsifiable enabling relation; its schema must establish that before outcomes are opened.

## P-INV-01: practical need, method and assay contract

Preclinical teams choosing DMG drug combinations need to address resistant minority states absent from routine culture. Sec14 p1: **'most approaches rely on synthetic lethality screens or empirical testing, limiting in vivo translation and generalizability.'** Sec14 p6: **'in vitro DMG models are biased toward proliferative OPC-like states with idiosyncratic vulnerabilities'**. This is a source-documented workflow pain, not an invented benefit.

Current method: infer essential state master regulators with ARACNe/metaVIPER and CRISPR; use drug-induced protein-activity inversion (OncoTreat/OncoTarget), then choose agents targeting complementary states. Sec34 p2: **'OncoTreat assessed each drug’s ability to invert the top or bottom 50 tumorigenic MRs per state based on enrichment (aREA) of tumorigenic MRs within drug-induced protein activity signatures from DIPG6 and SF8628'**. Sec33 p2: **'Drug perturbation profiling was performed using the lower of EC20 or Cmax (≤10 μM) to avoid stress-related transcriptional responses'**; 24h profiles, two biological replicates per cell line. Those are paid post-drug action profiles, not unperformed-intervention inputs.

Functional measurements and units differ:
- In vitro drug curves/10x10 combination matrices: 48h CellTiter-Glo, triplicate wells, normalized plate controls (Sec33 p1, Sec50). ATP luminescence is a viability proxy.
- Subcutaneous DIPG17 xenografts: mice randomized at target size, nine drug arms, 5d treatment; killed 2h after final dose for scRNA-seq (Sec37). State readout uses **'log(fold change) in the proportion of cells assigned to each state'** (Sec42 p1), with 2-3 treated and four vehicle biological replicates (p2). This is relative state occupancy, not absolute state-specific killing.
- Orthotopic syngeneic functional study: mouse survival under mono/combination dosing, median 3.5 mice per combination (range 3-5), 5days-on/2off (Sec49). Endpoint neurological symptoms or >15% weight loss. Drug pairs staggered when possible. One arm discontinued for toxicity. These are not same-culture matched trajectories or clinical benefit evidence.

Central prior: Sec12 p1 explicitly reports **'synergy driven by independent activity in distinct states should disappear in cultures dominated by a single state'**, and reports additive rather than synergistic in vitro interaction in three OPC-predominant lines. The paper's wording about Bliss 'sum' is not adopted as a mathematical definition. Sec14 p7 already proposes sequential/alternating state-targeting schedules. Sec14 p11 explicitly calls resistant-to-sensitive state reprogramming a natural extension. Thus these concepts are not new merely because a model formalizes them.

Unresolved source-owned condition: Sec14 p9 says **'it requires perturbational profiles from models that preserve cell state MR activity'**, and **'in vivo validation was achieved in genetically similar models and modest cohort sizes'**; **'optimal drug sequencing and dosing also require further study.'** These provide a concrete boundary: functional composition transfer must not presume constant state drug action when environment/model changes. Sec14 p8 explicitly says MR targeting can alter state via **'direct toxicity, differentiation or microenvironmental modulation'**, defeating a simple interpretation of occupancy depletion as killing.

Availability (paper claim, endpoints not independently fetched): GEO GSE264130 (372-drug PLATE-seq in two lines), GSE267415 (pooled CRISPR), GSE264412 (three biopsy drug-screen RNA), GSE262774 (orthotopic tumor/normal scRNA), GSE262775 (DIPG17 treated/vehicle scRNA). No claim that any workbook contains an admissible absolute-count/composition-response crosswalk. ## P-INV-02: practical need, method and assay contract

Preclinical precision-oncology teams face scarce primary material and patient-specific relapse composition. s1 p3 identifies a previous gap in **'capturing differential contributions of heterogeneous cells to treatment sensitivity and resistance'**, but this paper explicitly develops such a personalized approach; one cannot claim that exact gap unchanged.

Current ordinary procedure: sample-specific XGBoost from target-enrichment single-cell expression, drug-target activity and measured bulk single-drug DSS, fivefold CV and grid search. s2-6 p2: **'we constructed a combination–gene interaction matrix, defined as a binary matrix in which the rows correspond to combinations and the columns to drug target genes'**; all potent targets coded 1, multiplied by scRNA expression and passed to the trained single-drug model. HSA scores then prioritize relapse-specific combinations; no separate combination training required. This is a particularly strong ordinary baseline for any proposed union-of-actions composition learner, not an absence of such a method.

Measurement/units: five paired diagnosis/relapse patients (ten samples). Fresh primary bone-marrow bulk single-agent CTG at five concentrations, plate normalized and fitted four-parameter log-logistic curves summarized as DSS; compounds retained by curve-fit quality (s2-2). Drug library up to 544, versions differ by sampling year. It is not a fixed identical patient-by-drug factorial panel.

Only four selected pairs/seven agents were functionally validated in the index patient's paired samples because of material shortage. s2-8 p1: **'we selected four drug combinations ... in the paired patient samples of the index patient (AML2 D and AML2 R), for which enough primary cells were available'**. The 4x4 matrix includes singles and lower doses than single-drug CTG. s2-8 p2: thawed mononuclear cells, 35000 viable cells/well, HS5-conditioned medium, 72h exposure, antibody+AnnexinV/DRAQ7 live-cell gating. Readouts include blast, stem-cell and normal lymphocyte subpopulations. Native bulk CTG and thawed subpopulation flow assays are not interchangeable endpoints/preparations. Absolute-count denominator and preparation replication are not authenticated by this main passage.

Necessary practical gap: s4 p3: **'due to the cell population unspecificity of the bulk viability CTG drug assay, the predictions ... need to be validated with higher-precision flow cytometry population assays'**. Source also notes normal lymphocytes do not represent renal/cardiac toxicity (s4 p4). Faster prediction alone does not remove the separate functional-validation requirement. Reported workflow cost/time (s2-9) about $3200/sample, two weeks; these are source estimates, not our measured costs.

Availability: s2-13 p1 **'The ex vivo drug responses of the single compounds tested on the 10 AML samples are available in Supplementary Table S4.'** Main XML attachment confirms can-24-3840_supplementary_table_s4_suppst4.xlsx. Other raw data by request; VenEx clinical/research data by PI request. Some scRNA is EGA-controlled, others SRA raw. s2-13 p2: **'The processed Seurat RDS objects of the index AML2 patient’s scRNA-seq data (diagnosis and relapse) have been deposited in the GitHub repository as example datasets for running the codes.'** Source-linked official code https://github.com/yingjchen/RR-AML and Code Ocean capsule9565667.
