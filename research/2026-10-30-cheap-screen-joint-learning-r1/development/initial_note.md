## What the archive changes

The functional-index pilot stopped at its training gate: a non-target probe was worse than direct target measurement. The local-relation pilot's extra dose interaction lost to the simpler full-history model. The earlier feedback sampler changed predictions and actions without demonstrating its frozen adaptive gain. These are concrete limits of those models and menus, not evidence that every conditional functional relation is false.

The strongest general lesson is an information and attribution constraint: a model update is not a scientific contribution, a more complicated relation need not help, and learning a relation is worthwhile only if it changes decisions that remain unmeasured. If all requested decisions are already assayed, a direct lookup can be the correct rival. If an ordinary joint model can express a proposed relation, that defeats an algorithm claim only when the claimed algorithm is equivalent; it does not settle whether the biological relation is useful or empirically true.

Three old constraints remain relevant. Monotherapy histories do not identify unrestricted combination outcomes. A calibrated scalar endpoint is only the declared endpoint unless another function is measured. A sharing law must make a falsifiable prediction on a distinct drug/context/preparation, with calibration and action costs included. None requires omics or molecular mediation for a narrow assay-specific claim.

## One possible restriction, explicitly proposed rather than frozen

For a fixed cell background, common endpoint, exposure duration, rescue input, and predeclared compound-dose set, consider the extra effect of a candidate compound in a rescued stress condition relative to its own matched basal counter-screen. Let `Y(c,e,r,b)` denote the qualified endpoint under stress implementation `e`, rescue `r`, and candidate `b`; let `b=0` be vehicle. Define the within-condition log response `L_e(b) = log(Y(c,e,r,b)/Y(c,e,r,0))` only where both values are above a source-qualified detection floor. Let `L_base(b)` be the analogous basal effect measured in a compatible assay. The contrast is `D_e(b) = L_e(b) - L_base(b)`.

The weakest potentially useful proposed relation is **sign transfer of substantial conditional effects**: compounds with a sufficiently negative `D_chemical(b)` and little basal suppression retain a negative `D_physical(b)` on untouched target decisions, within a stated cell/dose/protocol scope. This is not implied by matching the untreated rescue amplitude. It is not asserted to hold, and the logarithmic scale is a provisional operational choice, not a biologically privileged law. Ratios from incompatible denominators or different assay protocols cannot be combined by this formula.

Inputs at use time would be the source and basal contrasts plus permitted calibration data. The operative rule would propose target-context follow-up only inside the scope supported by previous paired contrasts; a discordant target observation would withdraw that sharing claim for the predefined family. The useful output would be a list of target-context decisions whose predicted direction is justified by a tested transfer rule, and explicit exceptions that require direct assays. Standard models and experimental design can implement this. No new learning algorithm or special LLM role is proposed.

A constructive possibility is a compound affecting a stress-dependent process shared by both stress implementations; a boundary case is an interaction specific to the SCD inhibitor, with identical source score but no effect under hypoxia. Another boundary is oxygen-dependent drug activation, which can reverse rather than preserve the effect. These are reasoning cases, not observed examples. The feedback can change the next action only when another unmeasured compound's target decision genuinely depends on whether the relation is retained; otherwise it is ordinary direct confirmation.

This question is **not frozen as the round's developer candidate**. Its exact source/prior difference may collapse to the authors' existing surrogate-screen strategy, and the necessary paired public data may be absent. The source's positive selected examples cannot serve as untouched discovery or confirmatory outcomes. A generic source-to-target regression, a fixed diverse validation panel, direct target testing, and the original screen/follow-up rule are mandatory rivals. A negative sign-transfer test would support only the stated scope boundary, not a universal failure of chemical stress mimics.
